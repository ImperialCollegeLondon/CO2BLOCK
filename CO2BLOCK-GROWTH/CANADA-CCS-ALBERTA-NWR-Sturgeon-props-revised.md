# NWR Sturgeon Refinery CO₂ Capture — Capture Facility Profile

**Last updated**: 2026-07-23

### The "1.6 Mtpa" Figure

The figure 1.6 Mtpa appears in several secondary sources:
- **Wolf Midstream** (S8) states: *"Our system currently gathers 1.6 million tonnes of CO₂ per year from the NWR Sturgeon Refinery and Nutrien Redwater Fertilizer facility"* — this is the **combined total** from both sources, not NWR alone.
- **Incorrys** (S16) lists "CO2 Capacity: 1.6M tonnes" for the NWR facility specifically — this appears to be an error or conflation with the system total.
- **CCS Knowledge Centre** (S7) writes: *"These two large-emitting facilities have the capacity to capture about 1.6Mt/yr"* — again, the combined figure.
- **CER Market Snapshot** (S18) lists ACTL storage capacity at 1.6 Mt/yr.

**Verdict**: The design capture capacity of NWR Phase 1 alone is 1.2–1.3 Mtpa. The 1.6 Mtpa figure represents the **combined** NWR + Nutrien throughput. The maximum single-year capture at NWR to date is 1.25 Mt (2023). Do not use 1.6 Mtpa as an NWR-only value.

## Capture Process Detail and Infrastructure

1. **Gasification**: Heavy bottoms (hydrocracked residue / asphaltene) from bitumen refining are fed into the Lurgi MPG® (Multipurpose Gasification) unit (S6; S7).
2. **Syngas shift**: Raw syngas (CO + H₂) undergoes sour water-gas shift conversion to increase H₂ content: CO + H₂O ↔ CO₂ + H₂ (S6).
3. **Rectisol® absorption**: Refrigerated methanol physically absorbs CO₂ and H₂S from the syngas. H₂ exits as the main product stream; high-purity CO₂ (≥99.5 mol%) is recovered during solvent regeneration (S6; S7).
4. **Solvent regeneration energy**: 7,452 kW external heat required for methanol regeneration (S6).
5. **Boost compression** (NWR CRU site): 3-stage centrifugal booster compressor, ~17,000 HP, raises pressure from ~14.7 psi to ~190 psi (S17; S7).
6. **Main compression** (WCS Sturgeon Compressor Station, 3.2 km from refinery): ~13,000 HP, raises pressure to 2,600 psig (17,926 kPag) for ACTL pipeline (S17; S7).
7. **Transport**: CO₂ enters the 240 km ACTL pipeline (16" diameter, 12" in northern segment) at dense-phase conditions (S6; S7).
8. **Injection**: Enhance Energy injects CO₂ into the Leduc D-3A pool at Clive for miscible EOR and permanent storage (S7; S5).

**Design note**: The NWR CO₂ stream is pure and dry — *no dehydration, refrigeration, or additional processing is required* at the compressor station (S17; S7). This contrasts with the Nutrien stream, which requires full dehydration and refrigeration.

## Capture-to-Storage Chain: MMV and Accounting

### MMV (Measurement, Monitoring & Verification)

The ACTL system's MMV program is documented in the annual ACTL Knowledge Sharing Reports (S5; S6) and is operated by Enhance Energy at the Clive site. Key findings:

- As of year-end 2021: *"Injection has proceeded without incident and no trigger events (that would suggest CO₂ leakage) have been detected by the MMV program."* (S5)
- Fresh CO₂ injection concentration averaged 99.3% in 2021 (S5).
- The Clive injection project is registered in the Alberta Emission Offset Registry under Project ID 157 (S5).
- Third-party verified Offset Project Reports are filed with the Alberta TIER system (S5).
- The Alberta government publishes annual CO₂ sequestration data: cumulative ACTL sequestration reached ~10 Mt by September 2024, with combined Quest + ACTL at >17 Mt cumulative by early 2026 (S12).

### EOR vs. Permanent Storage

The CO₂ captured at NWR is used for miscible EOR at Clive. A portion of the injected CO₂ is produced back with the oil and **recycled** (re-injected) — the recycle compressor at Clive was commissioned in February 2021 and averaged 1,880 t/d recycle at 90.1% CO₂ concentration (S5). The net storage fraction (purchased CO₂ minus produced CO₂ plus recycling losses) is tracked through the Alberta Offset Registry and verified by third-party auditors. The registry figures in S12 represent **net permanent storage**.

### Mass Balance Attribution

The ACTL system is a **shared pipeline** with two current sources:
- **NWR Sturgeon Refinery**: ~1.1–1.25 Mt/yr (~80–85% of total)
- **Nutrien Redwater Fertilizer**: ~0.2–0.3 Mt/yr (~15–20% of total, increased from 0.2 to 0.3 Mt/yr in 2023 per S9)

All CO₂ entering the pipeline is commingled. The ">5 Mt cumulative captured" figure (S1) is NWR-specific. The ">10 Mt cumulative stored" figure (S12) is ACTL system total.

The physical absorption process means energy requirements depend on total gas flow and pressure, not CO₂ concentration in feed gas (S6).

## Phase 2/3 Expansion Status

The refinery was designed for **two additional identical phases** (each 50,000 bbl/d bitumen processing, ~1.2 Mtpa CO₂ capture). **Current status**:

- **Phase 2**: A formal proposal was submitted to the Alberta government in 2017 but **withdrawn in 2018** as the project evaluated changing economic conditions (S14). Canadian Natural Resources Ltd. (CNRL) adopted a "wait-and-see" approach, prioritizing Phase 1 reliability (S14).
- **Phase 3**: Never formally proposed. No funding, permitting, or construction timeline exists.
- **As of 2024–2025**: No public announcements indicate active development of Phases 2 or 3. The focus remains on optimizing Phase 1 operations (S1; S14).
- **Regulatory approval**: NWR holds regulatory approval for three phases (S17), but this is a standing approval, not an active development plan.

> **Recommendation**: Report Phase 2/3 as "design allowance — not funded or in development" rather than implying imminent expansion.

## Claims Verification

### "Eliminates up to 70% of the refinery's total CO₂ footprint"
**Source**: Multiple NWRP Mission Matters reports (S1; S2; S3; S4). *Verbatim* (S1): *"Gasification produces CO₂, which is captured instead of vented into the atmosphere, eliminating up to 70% of the refinery's operational CO₂ footprint."* The claim is explicitly qualified with "at optimum capacity" (S4) and "operational CO₂ footprint" (S1). **Status**: Sourced and verified.

### "Lowest 'wells-to-wheels' CO₂ transportation fuel based on heavy feedstock"
**Source**: CCS Knowledge Centre media release (S11) and NWRP Mission Matters 2020 (S4). The full quote from S11: *"Because of its carbon capture solution, diesel produced at the Sturgeon Refinery is the lowest 'wells-to-wheels' CO₂ transportation fuel based on heavy feedstock."* The claim is comparative (lowest among heavy-feedstock fuels), not absolute. **Status**: Sourced, but note the narrow scope of the comparison.

### "World's first refinery designed with integrated carbon capture"
**Source**: Multiple sources (S1; S4; S7; S15). *Verbatim* (S15): *"The world's first bitumen refinery designed from inception with a carbon capture and storage solution."* **Status**: Verified.

### "~70% reduction compared to traditional bitumen upgrading"
**Source**: This is a restatement of the 70% CO₂ footprint reduction, not a separate claim. The comparison basis is "traditional bitumen upgrading" (coking + without CCS). S4 explicitly states: *"eliminating as much as 70% of the refinery's total CO₂ footprint at optimum capacity."* **Status**: Sourced.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Actual capture volumes are well-documented** from NWR's own annual Mission Matters reports (S1–S4), which provide consistent year-over-year data. These are operator-reported but cross-verified against APMC financial filings (S10) and the ACTL Knowledge Sharing Reports (S5).

2. **The 1.6 Mtpa figure is a system-level metric**, not an NWR-specific capacity. It conflates NWR (~1.2 Mt/yr) and Nutrien (~0.3 Mt/yr) contributions. The Wolf Midstream source (S8) and CCS Knowledge Centre (S7) both confirm the combined nature of this number.

3. **The 70% reduction claim is well-sourced** but context-dependent — it refers to the fraction of the refinery's *operational* CO₂ footprint that is captured via gasification + CCS, *at optimum capacity*. The actual capture as a fraction of total site emissions may vary with utilization.

4. **Phases 2 and 3 are design allowances only** — no active development, funding, or construction timeline exists since 2018. The original 2012 projections of 3 × 1.2 Mtpa should not be presented as current or imminent.

5. **The Clive file (`-ACTL-Clive-props.md`) should be consulted for reservoir properties**, injection well data, and detailed storage capacity estimations. This file covers only the capture side of the value chain.

6. **Economic data** (levelized costs, capital costs) come from the ACTL Knowledge Sharing Reports (S5; S6), which are government-published regulatory submissions — these are the most authoritative source for project economics.
