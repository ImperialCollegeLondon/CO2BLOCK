## Permeability — THE CRITICAL REVISION

### Edmonton-Region-Specific Values

| Value | Source | Applicability | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| **Edmonton-region avg: 1.6 mD** | **Hofmann et al. 2013 — Stanford Geothermal Workshop (Source 22)** | **Most relevant to Polaris/Atlas (~45 km E of Edmonton)** | *"Core test from the wells around Edmonton result in an average regional scale permeability of 1.6 mD. This low expected permeability makes stimulation treatments imperative for efficient heat production."* |
| Central Alberta basin avg: ~10 mD | Weides et al. 2014 (Source 10) | Basin-wide (includes northern high-perm zone) | *"average porosity and permeability of the BSU is 10% and <1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m², respectively"* |
| Quest site avg: **~1,000 mD** | Smith et al. 2022 (Source 16) | Quest-specific (anomalously high) | *"average permeability close to 1000 md"* |
| Range: **1 mD to >1 D** | Quest D65 Application (Source 14) | Across BCS wells | *"permeability of 1 mD to > 1 D"* |
| Regional aquifer range: **<10 mD to several thousand mD** | Geoscience BC (Source 12) | Basin-wide | *"Aquifer permeability varies in a very wide range"* |

### Cross-Validation & Discrepancy Resolution

**This was the single largest issue identified in adversarial review.** The original file cited Weides et al. 2014 for a "regional average" of ~10 mD without noting that:

1. Hofmann et al. (2013) — a companion study using the same core data plus additional Edmonton-area wells — found **average regional permeability of 1.6 mD** specifically for the Edmonton area. This is the most relevant dataset for Polaris/Atlas (~45 km east of Edmonton).
2. Weides et al. explicitly note a *"zone of high porosity and permeability"* in the northern part of their study area (likely corresponding to the Quest/Thorhild area), which inflates their basin-wide average.
3. The PMC geothermal modeling study (Source 19) uses 1.9 mD horizontal permeability — consistent with Hofmann's 1.6 mD but *derived from literature* rather than site-specific measurements.

**Resolution:** The Edmonton-region average of **1-2 mD** (Hofmann 2013) is adopted as the best estimate for Polaris/Atlas. The Weides ~10 mD is retained as the central Alberta basin-wide average with appropriate annotation.

### Critical Permeability Control (AGS 2024)

> Source [9]: *"Interstitial clay and silt were found to be a critical control on permeability and even a 2-3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude, even in coarse-grained sediments."*

This is a material risk factor for Polaris/Atlas: small variations in clay content within the Atlas lease could produce permeability vastly different from either the Edmonton average or the Quest exceptional values.

## Storage Capacity Analysis (New — Addressing CRITICAL 3)

The 300 Mt lifetime storage claim (Source 2, Alberta Major Projects) can be validated with a back-of-envelope calculation:

### Volumetric Estimate

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| BCS thickness (h) | **45 m** | Estimated for Polaris/Atlas area |
| Porosity (φ) | **10%** | Regional average (Weides 2014) |
| CO2 density at reservoir conditions (ρCO₂) | **~700 kg/m³** | At ~20 MPa, ~76°C (supercritical) |
| Storage efficiency factor (E) — P50 | **2–4%** | USDOE range for saline aquifers (Source 23); IEAGHG 2023: P50 ~4-10% site-specific |
| Storage efficiency factor (E) — DOE-NETL 2023 clastics | P10=4.5%, P50=10.0%, P90=19.1% | Site-specific scale (Source 24) |
| Lease area (SLA) | **10,312 km²** | Atlas CSA (Source 26) |

### First-Order Calculation

Using the USDOE volumetric formula: **MCO₂ = A × h × φ × ρCO₂ × E**

For Phase 1 use of a fraction of the SLA:
- Assuming 5% of lease area used for Phase 1 plume (~500 km² effective area)
- **MCO₂ = 500 km² × 45 m × 0.10 × 700 kg/m³ × 0.025 (E = 2.5% P50)**
- **MCO₂ ≈ 39 Mt** (per 500 km²)

For the full 300 Mt claim:
- Required pore volume: 300 Mt / (700 kg/m³ × 0.025) ≈ 1.71 × 10¹⁰ m³
- Required rock volume: 1.71 × 10¹⁰ / (0.10 × 0.025) ≈ 6.86 × 10¹² m³
- At 45 m thickness: requires ~1,525 km² of effective plume area (~15% of SLA)
- This is **physically plausible** but depends on achieving the efficiency factor across a large area

### Capacity Classification (SPE SRMS Framework)

| Classification | Volume | Confidence | Notes |
|---------------|--------|------------|-------|
| Theoretical Storage Resource | >1,000 Mt | Low | Pore volume of entire SLA |
| Contingent Storage Resource | 100–500 Mt | Medium | Subject to AER D65 approval, drilling results |
| Commercial (Phase 1 contracted) | ~20 Mt | High | 650 ktpa × ~30 years; firm FID |
| Phase 2 aspirational | 7–10 Mtpa | Low | No FID; subject to 3rd-party commitments |

> **The 300 Mt figure appears to be a P50 contingent resource estimate, not a proven commercial capacity.** It is physically plausible given the lease area but requires confirmation through AER Directive 65 approval, drilling, and initial injection performance.

## Injectivity Risk — Quest Halite Damage (New — Addressing CRITICAL 4)

The adversarial review identified the absence of Quest's halite injectivity degradation as a significant gap. Key findings from Smith et al. 2022 (Source 16) and Tawiah et al. 2020 (Source 17):

### Halite Precipitation Mechanism

- Quest injects dry CO₂ into the BCS, which is initially filled with NaCl-saturated brine
- Water evaporates from brine into the dry CO₂ stream, driving halite (NaCl) precipitation
- Precipitate accumulates in: wellbore, perforation tunnels, near-wellbore pore throats
- Injectivity declines over time as pore throats become plugged

### Quantified Impact at Quest

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Pre-damage injectivity | ~1.08 Mtpa (3 wells, high) | Quest D-65 |
| Post-damage skin factor | **147** (pre-remediation) | Smith et al. 2022 (Source 16) |
| Damage zone permeability | **~10 mD** (99% reduction from native 1,000 mD) | Smith et al. 2022: Hawkins' formula calculation |
| Damage zone radius | **~15 m** | TOUGHREACT geochemical modeling |
| Injectivity decline | **~35%** permanent shift after first workover | Tawiah et al. 2020 (Source 17) |
| Salinity | NaCl-saturated brine in BCS | Shell analysis |
| Remediation method | Fresh-water based halite dissolution treatment | First performed 2020 |
| Post-remediation injectivity improvement | **263%** relative to pre-treatment baseline | Smith et al. 2022 |
| Post-remediation skin factor | **20** (improved from 140-160 range) | Smith et al. 2022 |
| Reprecipitation risk | Injectivity deteriorated rapidly upon CO₂ re-injection | Subsequent studies confirm temporary nature of remediation |

### Implications for Polaris/Atlas

- Quest's native BCS permeability (~1,000 mD) is **500-1,000× higher** than the Polaris/Atlas Edmonton-region estimate (~1-2 mD)
- At Quest, halite damage reduced near-wellbore permeability to ~10 mD (a 99% reduction)
- **If Polaris has native permeability of 1-2 mD, equivalent halite damage would reduce near-wellbore permeability to levels approaching 0.01-0.02 mD** — potentially making injection impossible without frequent remediation
- The freshwater remediation method demonstrated at Quest may not be sufficient if native permeability is two orders of magnitude lower
- This risk is partially mitigated by Polaris's lower injection rate (650 ktpa vs Quest's 1.08 Mtpa) and the potential use of pre-saturated CO₂ injection (Papi et al. 2025)

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Polaris-specific subsurface data is NOT publicly available** as of mid-2026. The Atlas Hub signed its Carbon Sequestration Agreement (CSA) in July 2024 and has not yet received final AER Directive 65 approval. Site-specific well logs, core analyses, and injection tests have not been published.

2. **Quest data is the best available analog** but must be clearly distinguished:
   - Quest operates in the same BCS formation but a **different physical lease area** (Quest injection near Thorhild/Radway; Atlas ~45 km east of Edmonton).
   - Quest's reservoir quality is **exceptional** (~1,000 mD, ~17% porosity) and is **not representative** of typical BCS.
   - **Hofmann et al. (2013) Edmonton-region average of 1.6 mD is the most relevant reference** for Polaris/Atlas permeability.

3. **The AGS BCS Case Study (2024)** provides the most comprehensive regional analysis: 28 cores, four facies associations, 14 distinct facies. Key finding: clay content is the dominant control on permeability — a 2-3% increase can reduce permeability by three orders of magnitude.

4. **Halite-induced injectivity damage is a material risk** for any BCS injection project. Quest's native permeability of ~1,000 mD made halite damage manageable (remediable with freshwater washes). At Polaris's expected 1-2 mD native permeability, halite damage could be prohibitive without significant remediation capability.

5. **Polaris capture capacity was revised** from the original 2021 proposal of 750,000 tpy down to 650,000 tpy at FID in June 2024. The original 2021 proposal also mentioned a 12 km pipeline; the FID announcement specifies 22 km.

6. **Atlas Phase 2** (open-access hub for third parties) is subject to a future investment decision and has no firm capacity or timeline.

7. **Regulatory pathway**: CSA signed → AER Directive 65 approval required for injection → final regulatory applications still pending. The AER regulates CO2 capture, pipelines, and subsurface injection under Alberta's Carbon Sequestration Tenure Regulation.

8. **The 300 Mt lifetime capacity claim** is not independently verified. Back-of-envelope calculations suggest it is physically plausible given the 10,312 km² lease area, but it requires confirmation through AER D65 review and initial injection performance.

9. **Regional pressure interference** between neighboring CCS hubs in the BCS is an emerging consideration (SSRN 2024, Source 27). Multiple hubs sharing the same regional aquifer may experience competitive injection effects that reduce individual hub capacity.
