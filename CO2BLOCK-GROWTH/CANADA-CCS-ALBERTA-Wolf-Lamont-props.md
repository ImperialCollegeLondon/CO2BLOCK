## Porosity

| Parameter | Value | Source | Notes |
|-----------|-------|--------|-------|
| BCS porosity range | **8–24%** | Moradi (2016); Shell Quest core data | Fine-to-coarse sandstone |
| BCS average porosity (Quest area) | ~18% | Moradi (2016), density-log derived | BCS interval at ~2 km depth |
| BCS average porosity (regional BCS aquifer) | **14.4%** | Alberta Innovates (Meikle, 2024) | Basal Aquifer study — 14.4% avg for CO₂-storage-suitable area |
| BSU (Basal Sandstone Unit) average | **10%** | Weides et al. (2014) | 6 wells; central Alberta; includes tighter zones |
| LMS porosity (immediately above BCS) | ~6% | Moradi (2016) | Effective — acts as baffle |
| UMS porosity (major seal) | 1–2% | Moradi (2016) | Effective — primary aquitard |
| Devonian Basal Red Beds porosity | ~5% | Moradi (2016) | Additional baffle |

> **Note**: Porosity in the Basal Cambrian is heterogeneous. AGS PRS-2024-001 reports that even a 2–3% increase in clay content reduces permeability by three orders of magnitude. Secondary porosity from leached quartz grains is common. For Wolf Lamont, expect Quest-like ~18% in high-quality channel/estuarine facies, with significant lateral variability.

> **Critical observation (AGS PRS-2024-001)**: "Interstitial clay and silt were found to be a critical control on permeability and even a 2-3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude, even in coarse-grained sediments."
>
> **Quest operation (Stantec, 2024)**: "The successful QUEST CCS Project includes Basal Cambrian Sandstone estuarine and shoreface deposits and has performed well, with anecdotal evidence of high perm streaks in associated tidal channels that have not materially affected the project."
>
> Wolf-specific permeability data is not publicly available. A 2024 core presentation by Whitecap (well 100/06-05-055-17W4/00) at the CEGA Core Conference directly targets the Basal Cambrian Sand for the Lamont Carbon Hub, indicating ongoing reservoir characterization.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Wolf-specific data is limited.** No public reservoir properties report specifically for the Wolf Lamont Carbon Hub has been released. Whitecap Resources (the subsurface partner) presented core from well 100/06-05-055-17W4/00 at the CEGA Core Conference in June 2024 for the "Basal Cambrian Sand for Carbon Sequestration" evaluation at Lamont — this data is likely proprietary.

2. **Regional AGS data (PRS-2024-001) is the primary public characterization** of the Basal Cambrian Sandstone. It covers 28 cores and establishes the regional stratigraphic/diagenetic framework. The Weides et al. (2014) study provides the only published geomechanical tests for the BSU in central Alberta.

3. **Quest CCS is the direct operational analog.** Quest has injected >7 Mt CO₂ into the BCS since 2015 with no containment issues. The Wolf Lamont hub targets the same regional aquifer system approximately 60–80 km north/northeast of the Quest injection site. The Stantec (2024) review states QUEST has "performed well, with anecdotal evidence of high perm streaks in associated tidal channels that have not materially affected the project."

4. **The AER Approval 13513 lists the pool as "WILLINGDON/BSL QTZ UND"** (Willingdon field / Basal Quartz undefined). This confirms the target is the Basal Quartz (Lower Cretaceous) rather than the deeper Basal Cambrian Sandstone. Note: in the Lamont area, the "Basal Quartz" (Kbs_qtz, code 3340) is the Lower Cretaceous basal sandstone, stratigraphically distinct from the Cambrian BCS. However, in practice, both are quartz-dominated sandstone aquifers with similar CCS characteristics, and the Lamont area has been historically referred to in press releases as targeting the "Basal Cambrian Sand." Clarification on the exact target is needed from operator data.

5. **Key uncertainty — heterogeneity**: As AGS PRS-2024-001 notes: "a critical control on permeability and even a 2-3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude."

6. **Area data is preliminary.** The 9-township extent from the AER application represents the application boundary. The final sequestration lease and pore space unit agreement areas will be determined by Alberta Energy and Minerals post-evaluation.

7. **Timeline**: Selected Mar 2022 (RFPP) → AER approved Mar 2026 (Approval 13513). The CCS Knowledge Centre notes that "recent Directive 065 sequestration application reviews took less than six months for the Wolf Lamont Carbon Hub." Initial target operation date slipped from "end of 2024" (2022 announcement) to "2025" (CER Jan 2025) and remains in development.
