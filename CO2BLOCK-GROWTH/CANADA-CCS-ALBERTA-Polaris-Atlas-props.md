> Source [10]: "The average porosity and permeability of the BSU is 10%"
>
> Source [16]: Quest BCS "porosity of about 17%"
>
> Source [14]: BCS "porosity of 8-24%" (Quest D65 Application: Table 4-1)
>
> Source [12]: "average well-scale porosity of the Basal Aquifer varies from less than 1% in the deep portions of the Alberta basin, to more than 25% in shallower regions"

**Key observation:** The BCS exhibits significant porosity heterogeneity controlled by facies, diagenesis, and clay content. Quest's site-specific ~17% is notably higher than the regional average of ~10%, indicating better reservoir quality in the Fort Saskatchewan area.

### Data Quality Note — Polaris vs. Quest
Polaris/Atlas targets the BCS in a **different lease area** than Quest (Quest injection near Thorhild/Radway; Atlas ~45 km east of Edmonton). While the formation is the same, porosity can vary laterally. Quest's published data is the best analog until Polaris-specific core data becomes available.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Polaris-specific subsurface data is NOT publicly available** as of mid-2026. The Atlas Hub signed its Carbon Sequestration Agreement (CSA) in July 2024 — the first in Alberta — and has not yet received final AER Directive 65 approval. Site-specific well logs, core analyses, and injection tests for the Atlas lease have not been published.

2. **Quest data is the best available analog** but must be clearly distinguished:
   - Quest operates in the same BCS formation but a **different physical lease area** (Quest injection near Thorhild/Radway; Atlas ~45 km east of Edmonton).
   - Quest's reservoir quality is **exceptional** (~1,000 mD, ~17% porosity) and may be better than average BCS properties.
   - Regional BCS data (Weides et al. 2014: ~10% porosity, ~10 mD permeability) is more conservative and may be more representative of typical BCS away from the Quest area.

3. **The AGS BCS Case Study (2024)** provides the most comprehensive regional analysis: 28 cores, four facies associations, 14 distinct facies. Key finding: clay content is the dominant control on permeability — a 2-3% increase can reduce permeability by three orders of magnitude.

4. **Polaris capture capacity was revised** from the original 2021 proposal of 750,000 tpy down to 650,000 tpy at FID in June 2024. The original 2021 proposal also mentioned a 12 km pipeline; the FID announcement specifies 22 km.

5. **Atlas Phase 2** (open-access hub for third parties) is subject to a future investment decision and has no firm capacity or timeline.

6. **Regulatory pathway**: CSA signed → AER Directive 65 approval required for injection → final regulatory applications still pending as of FID. The AER regulates CO2 capture, pipelines, and subsurface injection under Alberta's Carbon Sequestration Tenure Regulation.

7. **Regional BCS mapping** by Canadian Discovery Ltd. covers ~250,000 km² and provides a Stage 1A/1B storage resource assessment aligned with SPE SRMS — a useful reference for understanding regional heterogeneity.
