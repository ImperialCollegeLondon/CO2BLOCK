## Emissions & Source Context

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Newfoundland & Labrador total emissions | ~9 Mt CO₂/yr (ECCC 2024) |
| Offshore oil & gas production share | ~5.5 Mt CO₂/yr (Hibernia, Terra Nova, White Rose) |
| Come By Chance/Newfoundland Refinery | ~0.6 Mt CO₂/yr |
| Largest single offshore source | Hibernia platform (~2 Mt CO₂/yr) |

Grand Banks CCS would likely be sourced from offshore production platforms (Hibernia GBS, Terra Nova FPSO, SeaRose FPSO) or potentially from Atlantic Canadian industrial sources via marine transport. The existing produced-water reinjection infrastructure at Hibernia and Terra Nova could inform technical pathways for CO₂ injection, though no operator has announced CCS integration. Transatlantic CO₂ shipping from European sources (cf. Northern Lights model) is a speculative longer-term possibility given Grand Banks proximity to North Atlantic shipping lanes and Equinor's established cross-Atlantic logistics chain.

### Atlantic Accord Modernization & CCS

The Canada-Newfoundland Atlantic Accord is undergoing modernization (process initiated 2023, continuing through 2024–2025). Key CCS-relevant developments:

- **2024 Canada-Newfoundland MOU on Offshore Renewable Energy and Clean Fuels**: While primarily focused on wind energy and hydrogen/ammonia production, the MOU establishes a framework for discussing shared jurisdiction over emerging offshore technologies, which could set precedent for CCS regulation.
- **C-NLOPB mandate expansion**: Industry and policy discussions have explored whether the C-NLOPB mandate should extend to CCS and offshore renewables. As of 2025, **no legislative amendment has been passed** to explicitly include CCS pore space tenure.
- **Accord modernization discussions**: Publicly available meeting summaries from the 2024 Canada-Newfoundland Offshore Petroleum Board roundtables indicate CCS was acknowledged as a future consideration but not yet within scope of the modernization process.
- **Comparison with Nova Scotia**: Nova Scotia has moved further on offshore CCS regulatory development (CNSOPB mandate expansion discussions, NS Offshore CCS Legislation under consideration). Newfoundland and Labrador has not yet followed suit.

**Critical Gap**: No CCS pore space tenure regime; no long-term liability framework; C-NLOPB mandate = petroleum only.

## Conclusion

**Grand Banks Basin has ZERO CCS projects.** Only **one Tier 4 academic feasibility study** (EGU26-770, 2026) evaluating fault-salt structural controls on CO₂ storage in the Jeanne d'Arc Basin. No regulatory framework, no pore space tenure, no proponent. Basin assignment confirmed.

Despite significant existing offshore infrastructure (Hibernia GBS, Terra Nova FPSO, SeaRose FPSO), major operators (Equinor, ExxonMobil, Suncor, Cenovus) with CCS capability, and publicly available geological datasets (3D seismic, well penetrations, reservoir studies) through C-NLOPB, no operator has made a CCS commitment specific to the Grand Banks. Newfoundland and Labrador's ~9 Mt/yr CO₂ emissions profile provides a domestic CO₂ source base, but regulatory gaps (no CCS pore space tenure, no C-NLOPB mandate for CCS) remain the principal barrier.
