## 1. Reservoir Thickness (Leduc Formation D-3A Pool)

### Primary Values

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Average reservoir thickness (net pay / injection interval) | **8.75 m** | S2, S3 (ACTL 2018 & 2017 Summary Reports) |
| Total Leduc Formation thickness at Clive | **~250 m** (pervasively dolomitized platform carbonates) | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Uppermost reservoir interval (well-represented in core) | **~35 m** (83 cored wells, 1,100 m recovery) | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Massive Sandy Zone | Extends below oil/water contact; true thickness not known | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Layered Muddy Zone | **~10 m** thick across Central & South Clive; thins to north | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Transition zone (cemented layer) | **2–3 m** heavily cemented, reduced porosity | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Hydrocarbon column (pre-production) | 11 m gas cap + 18.5 m oil column = **~30 m** total | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Depth from surface | 1,845–1,938 m (mean: 1,874 m) | S2, S3 |
| Nisku reservoir average thickness (secondary) | 6.4 m | S2, S3 |

### Confirming Evidence

- The 8.75 m average thickness appears consistently across both the 2017 and 2018 Summary Reports (S2, S3), representing the perforated/net-pay interval used for injection.
- The MMV Plan (S6) provides higher-resolution vertical subdivision: the reservoir-quality rock in the uppermost Leduc comprises the Massive Sandy Zone (good porosity/permeability, extending below OWC) and the Layered Muddy Zone (~10 m), separated by a 2–3 m cemented baffle.
- Total Leduc Formation thickness (~250 m) is largely non-reservoir dolomitized platform carbonate; only the uppermost ~35 m has been extensively cored and characterized.

### Recommended Value for CO₂ Storage Work

| Parameter | Recommended Value | Basis |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Net-pay thickness for injection | **8.75 m** (range: 8–35 m, depending on zone) | Primary reported value from ACTL reports; consider ~30 m for full reservoir interval if combining oil + gas column height |
| Total storage interval thickness | **35 m** (uppermost Leduc reservoir interval) | Conservative working assumption for volumetric calculations |

## 2. Porosity (Leduc Formation D-3A Pool)

### Primary Values

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Average porosity (Enhance geo-model)** | **6.1 %** | S7 (Tavallali et al. 2025, Wiley, citing Enhance internal model [23]) |
| Bashaw Platform average porosity (bulk aquifer) | ~4 % (pore volume ~30 km³) | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Cooking Lake Formation (supporting aquifer) | Avg **3 %** (max 9 %) | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Core observations | Leached Amphipora moldic porosity in lagoon/shoal sediments; vuggy and granular porosity in Massive Sandy Zone | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |

### Confirming Evidence

- The 6.1 % average porosity is the single most authoritative published number, sourced from a 2025 peer-reviewed book chapter that directly cites Enhance's geo-model.
- The MMV Plan's 4 % average for the broader Bashaw Platform aquifer (30 km³ pore volume) is consistent with 6.1 % for the higher-quality reservoir zone.
- Cooking Lake porosity (3 % avg) is tighter and represents the underlying platform, not the reservoir.
- The 2011/2014/2017/2018 ACTL reports all contain porosity–permeability crossplots (figures only, not tabulated) for both Nisku and Leduc; these are the underlying data that informed the geo-model.
- Reservoir quality is facies-controlled: Massive Sandy Zone has well-distributed granular porosity; Layered Muddy Zone has lower porosity due to muddy interbeds.

### Recommended Value for CO₂ Storage Work

| Parameter | Recommended Value | Basis |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Average porosity (reservoir zone) | **6.1 %** | Directly from peer-reviewed citation of Enhance geo-model |
| Range | 3–9 % (locally up to 12 % in vuggy intervals) | Cooking Lake lower bound; Amphipora moldic zones higher |

## 3. Permeability (Leduc Formation D-3A Pool)

### Primary Values

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Mode permeability (Enhance geo-model)** | **30 mD** | S7 (Tavallali et al. 2025, Wiley, citing Enhance internal model [23]) |
| Massive Sandy Zone permeability | "High vertical permeability" (qualitative); well-connected vuggy/granular fabric | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Cooking Lake Formation (background) | Avg **0.24 mD** (max 1.3 mD) | S6 (MMV Plan 2019) |
| Injectivity context | CO₂ injectivity estimated at ~3× water injectivity; substantial water injection for 5 decades confirms injectivity | S2, S5 |
| Basin-scale context | Leduc Formation on Bashaw Platform benefits from extensive reef-complex connectivity over >3,500 km² | S6 |

### Confirming Evidence

- The 30 mD mode permeability is the only published quantitative value, sourced from the same peer-reviewed 2025 Wiley chapter that reports the 6.1 % porosity.
- No arithmetic or geometric mean permeability has been published; only the **mode** is reported.
- Permeability in the carbonate reservoir is highly heterogeneous due to facies control (moldic, vuggy, intergranular porosity types).
- Crossplots in the ACTL Detailed Report (Figures 3.4.8–3.4.9 for Nisku and Leduc) show the full porosity–permeability relationship but are image-only and not digitized in the available PDF metadata.
- The Massive Sandy Zone is described as having well-connected permeability, while the Layered Muddy Zone has reduced vertical permeability due to muddy interbeds.

### Recommended Value for CO₂ Storage Work

| Parameter | Recommended Value | Basis |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Mode permeability | **30 mD** | Directly reported from Enhance geo-model |
| Expected range | 1–100 mD (locally higher in vuggy zones) | Consistent with carbonate reef facies; crossplots in ACTL reports |
| Permeability anisotropy | Consider kᵥ/kₕ reduction in Layered Muddy Zone | MMV Plan notes muddy interbeds impede vertical flow |

### Confirming Evidence

- The Origins lease area derives from the broader CarbonStorage.io project page and represents the total pore-space tenure for Enhance's planned sequestration hub at the southern ACTL terminus.

- The Bashaw Platform reef complex (>3,500 km²) provides the regional geological context; the Clive field is one of several fields on this platform (Bashaw, Chigwell, Haynes, Innisfail, Nevis, Wimborne).
