## Issues & Recommendations

### 1. Negativity Bias Risk

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| The document emphasizes zero activity but the "Common Reasons" section generalizes across very different basin types | A passive margin basin (e.g., Labrador, Georges Bank) has fundamentally different geology, infrastructure access, and regulatory context than a remote Arctic basin (e.g., Foxe, Cumberland). Grouping them all under "no CCS regulatory framework" and "economic non-viability" obscures important distinctions. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a basin-type breakdown to the Barriers section: "Passive Margin Basins" (e.g., Labrador, Georges Bank) face different barriers than "Intracratonic Basins" (Hudson Bay) or "Arctic Basins" (North Baffin Bay). At minimum, distinguish Arctic/northern (where CCS is decades away) from southern-accessible basins (Michigan, Lake Superior, Moose River) where CCS might eventually be viable. |

### 2. Sverdrup Basin Reference Error

| Issue | Detail |

| **Recommendation** | Verify whether CANADA-CCS-SVERDRUP.md exists. If not, either create it or correct/remove the reference. The Sverdrup Basin (Evenick UBI 650) is the largest Canadian Arctic basin and deserves a dedicated file if others exist. |

### 3. Hudson Bay Basin Potential Underexplored

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Hudson Bay is listed as "Zero activity; no regulatory framework" | This is correct, but Hudson Bay has significant geological potential (1,597,536 km², Paleozoic carbonate platform, analogous to the Williston Basin). It also has a unique tri-jurisdiction (federal/Nunavut/Manitoba/Ontario/Quebec) that makes CCS governance complex but theoretically possible. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a more detailed note on Hudson Bay: (a) existing petroleum exploration history (TRI well, seismic, shows), (b) potential storage formations (Paleozoic carbonates), (c) the jurisdictional complexity of 4 provinces/territories + federal. |

### 4. Michigan Basin Underdocumented

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Michigan Basin (UBI 399) is listed as "Shared; zero Canada side" | The Michigan Basin underlies southwestern Ontario (Sarnia-Chatham area), which has Canada's largest concentration of petrochemical refineries and CO₂ emissions (~25 Mt/yr from Sarnia/Lambton alone). The potential for CCS in the Canadian Michigan Basin is significantly higher than for most other empty basins. |
| **Recommendation** | Significantly expand the Michigan Basin note: (a) document the Sarnia-area emitter inventory, (b) note Ontario's regulatory readiness (if any — Ontario has no CCS Act), (c) reference US-side Michigan CCS activity (Core Energy CO₂-EOR, US DOE CarbonSAFE projects) as analog for what Canada-side could do, (d) the 337,560 km² area includes potentially favorable geology. |

### 5. Lake Superior Basin

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| "GSC Open File 6582 (CO₂ hydrate)" referenced | This is one line. Lake Superior Basin is a Midcontinent Rift basin with unique geology (flood basalts, clastic sediments, CO₂ hydrate potential). The document could note that carbon mineralization (basalt CO₂ reaction) is a potential storage pathway. |
| **Recommendation** | Expand the Lake Superior note to mention carbon mineralization potential in Midcontinent Rift basalts (analogous to CarbFix in Iceland and basalt storage research in Washington State). |

### 6. Missing Domestic Basin — Georgia Basin

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| The **Georgia Basin** (Evenick UBI 256 — Forearc, On-Offshore) is not listed | The Georgia Basin underlies the Vancouver Island / Georgia Strait / Lower Mainland area. It has a listed UBI (256) and is relevant because: (a) the Metro Vancouver region has significant CO₂ emissions, (b) BC's CCS targets exist, (c) there may be assessment literature. |
| **Recommendation** | Check whether the Georgia Basin was intentionally excluded or accidentally omitted. If excluded, add a note explaining why. |
