### Confirming Evidence

- **Regional context**: The Leduc Formation in the Rimbey-Meadowbrook reef trend ranges from thin platform margins (<50 m) to thick pinnacle reefs (>250 m). The Origins well (Tp 39-25W4) lies in the Bashaw Reef Complex area where buildups are typically thick.
- **Clive analogue**: The Clive Leduc D-3A pool (same regional reef trend, ~30 km away) has an average reservoir thickness of **8.75 m** at the producing interval (ACTL Knowledge Sharing Report), but this represents only the oil-bearing zone within a much thicker Leduc buildup. The gross Leduc section there is considerably thicker.
- **Cross-validation**: The 137 m gross thickness from CarbonStorage.io is a single-well measurement and is intermediate between the thin Clive producing interval (~9 m) and the regional maximum buildup (275 m). This is consistent with a flank or intermediate position on the reef complex.
- **Recommended value**: **~137 m gross thickness** at the injection well (CarbonStorage.io). Regional range 180–275 m for the overall Lacombe reef buildup context.

### Confirming Evidence

- **Well value (6 %) vs. regional avg (~8 %)**: The well-specific value is below regional average but within the published range (5–20 %). Below-average porosity at a specific location is expected and does not indicate a problem.
- **Depth context**: At Origins' depth of ~2,000 m TVD, Amthor et al. (1994) note that limestones and dolomitized buildups have comparable porosity at <2,000 m, but at >2,000 m dolostones are significantly more porous. The Origins well targets dolomitized Leduc.
- **AGS cutoff**: AGS Digital Data 2024-0014 uses a ≥3 % porosity cutoff for net reservoir thickness determination, confirming that even the 6 % porosity at the Origins well is well above the minimum considered reservoir-grade.
- **Recommended value**: **6 %** (well-specific, CarbonStorage.io). Use 8 % as regional average context. Both values are within 5–20 % range from peer-reviewed literature.

## Permeability

### Primary Values

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote / Note |
|-------|--------|-----------------------|
| **Well permeability range: <10 mD to >100 mD** at well 4-36-39-25 | CarbonStorage.io — Origins Project | Raw notation: "Permeability (md): <10-+100" — interpreted as <10 mD to >+100 mD. **Note: ambiguous notation**; the dash-plus character likely indicates a range from below 10 mD to above 100 mD. |
| **Regional Leduc permeability range: 1–1000 mD** | Amthor et al. (1994); AGS synthesis | "Upper Devonian carbonate rocks…characterized by a wide range of porosity and permeability values" (Amthor 1994). Dolostones show highest values; limestone permeability tens to hundreds of times lower than dolostone horizontal permeability. |
| **Leduc average permeability (regional): ~26 mD** (arithmetic mean) | Weides & Majorowicz (2014) — Canadian Journal of Earth Sciences | "average permeability is between 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ m² (Wabamun) and 26 × 10⁻¹⁵ m² (Leduc)" — 26 × 10⁻¹⁵ m² = **~26 mD**. Based on ~50,000+ core analyses across central Alberta. This is the best available quantitative average. |
| **Permeability anisotropy / heterogeneity: High (carbonate reef facies)** | Amthor et al. (1994); Lyster et al. (2024) | "horizontal permeability averages tens of times to several hundred times the vertical permeability" (Amthor 1994). Lyster (2024): "highly heterogeneous, exhibiting a range of permeability and pore sizes." |

### Confirming Evidence

- **Well range fits regional context**: The well range (<10–>100 mD) falls within the regional range (1–1000 mD). The average (~26 mD from Weides & Majorowicz) is within the well range, suggesting the well data is representative.
- **Carbonate heterogeneity note**: The wide range reflects a dual-porosity/permeability system where matrix provides moderate perm and fractures/vugs provide high-perm conduits. This is characteristic of Leduc dolomitized reefs.
- **Important caveat on "average"**: The value ~26 mD from Weides & Majorowicz (2014) is an arithmetic mean of core-plug measurements across the formation. Formation-scale effective permeability for CO2 injection may differ due to fracture networks, vug connectivity, and sub-seismic heterogeneity.
- **Recommended value**: Use **<10 to >100 mD** as the well-specific range (CarbonStorage.io), with **regional average ~26 mD** (Weides & Majorowicz 2014) for context. The full regional range is 1–1000 mD (Amthor et al. 1994).

### Area Discrepancy Notes

1. **Sequestration Lease Area (CSA) vs. Plume Area**: The 2,491,617 acres value likely represents the full Carbon Sequestration Agreement (CSA) "Location" boundary (potentially hundreds of km² across multiple townships), not the Phase 1 injection area. Under Alberta's CSA framework, the Location is defined by Schedule A of the agreement and can be modified by the Minister based on plume modelling (per Article 6 of the standard form CSA; see ABlawg analysis, Nov 2024). The Phase 1 plume (~50 km²) is only a small fraction of the leased area.

2. **Regulatory significance of max plume**: The 5.6 km maximum radius (~99 km²) was the basis for the AER's standing decision in the E3 Lithium / PrairieSky / Canpar appeals (Appeal 1959099). Canpar's interest was located adjacent to the injection well within the modelled plume boundary. The AER noted that the "full extent" would require additional approvals (ABLawg, May 2026).

3. **Comparison with analogues**: Quest CCS (Shell) has an Area of Interest (AOI) spanning ~40 townships (~3,730 km² or ~921,000 acres) — comparable order-of-magnitude to Origins' total lease area.

**Key observations from analogue comparison:**
- Origins has a **much thicker gross interval** (~137 m) than either Quest (35–46 m) or Clive (~8.75 m net), which provides larger total pore volume per well.
- Origins has **lower porosity** (6 %) than Quest (16 % average) — consistent with carbonate vs. sandstone lithology.
- Origins' permeability range (<10–>100 mD) overlaps the lower end of Quest's range (20–500 mD) but the **carbonate's fracture/vug connectivity** may yield effective permeability higher than core-plug measurements suggest.

- The Leduc Formation at Origins is from the same regional reef complex as Clive but targets a **different pool (TD UND vs. D-3A)** and a **pressure-depleted saline aquifer rather than an EOR reservoir**. This means direct injectivity analogy to Clive is limited.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Source reliability hierarchy**: The most reliable values are from AER regulatory documents (Approval 13463 PDF via ABlawg; Appeal 1959099). Project-well values from CarbonStorage.io are secondary aggregations of AER well-file data and should be treated as Tier 5 (not auditable to original well-file without verification against AER well data).

2. **CarbonStorage.io unit label error**: The data section on CarbonStorage.io labels the injection rate as "1,600,000 Mt/year" while the overview section correctly states "1,600,000 t/yr". The physically plausible value is **1.6 Mt/yr (1,600,000 tonnes/year)**. Downstream users should be aware that the "Mt/year" header is a data-entry error and should always cite as "t/yr".

3. **Grokipedia → replaced with direct AGS citations**: All regional Leduc property ranges previously sourced from Grokipedia (an LLM-generated wiki) are now sourced directly from:
   - **Amthor, Mountjoy & Machel (1994)** — AAPG Bulletin, peer-reviewed Leduc porosity/permeability study
   - **Weides & Majorowicz (2014)** — Canadian Journal of Earth Sciences, ~50,000+ core analysis dataset
   - **Lyster et al. (2024)** — GHGT-17 proceedings, Leduc-Ireton CCS characterization
   - **AGS Digital Data 2024-0014/0017** — Government of Alberta porosity-thickness maps

4. **Dead AER URL**: Source 4's original URL (AER Integrated Application Registry) returned 404. Approval 13463 is now confirmed at the ABlawg.ca mirror. The AER's application registry may have moved or been taken down.

5. **No core analysis or DST data published**: Values for well 4-36-39-25 are likely derived from wireline logs and drillstem tests. No publicly available core analysis, SCAL, or mercury injection capillary pressure data exist for this specific well. Lyster et al. (2024) note that most core analysis for Leduc was conducted at ambient conditions and may not capture in-situ effective permeability.

6. **Pressure-depletion context**: The initial reservoir pressure (~15.3 MPa) is significantly sub-hydrostatic (~19.6 MPa hydrostatic at 2,000 m). This is consistent with a pressure-depleted saline aquifer and represents a different reservoir state from the EOR-focused ACTL/Clive CCS (Leduc D-3A pool). The depletion state affects injectivity, storage capacity, and geomechanical behaviour.

7. **Temperature log requirement**: Approval 13463 condition (h) requires a one-time temperature log across the Leduc and Nisku formations within 2 years of injection start — this will provide additional verification of geological containment.

8. **Quantitative plume estimate**: The plume radius values (4 km nominal, 5.6 km max) from the AER appeal proceeding are the most authoritative public plume estimates. They were the basis for regulatory standing decisions in the E3 Lithium and Canpar appeals.
