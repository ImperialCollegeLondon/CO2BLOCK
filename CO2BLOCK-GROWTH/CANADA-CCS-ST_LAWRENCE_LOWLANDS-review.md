## Strengths

1. **Formation-level reservoir characterization** — The Potsdam Group (sandstone), Beekmantown Group (dolomite), and Trenton/Black River/Chazy Groups (limestone) each have documented thickness, porosity range, permeability range, and caprock (Utica Shale). This is the best formation-level documentation among the zero-project basins.

2. **Structural domain distinction** — The document correctly separates the St. Lawrence Lowlands into two structural domains: the flat-lying Platform and the Appalachian thrust belt. This is geologically important because the thrust belt has structurally trapped gas reservoirs (Saint-Flavien) that could be depleted-field storage targets.

3. **Emissions context** — "~6.4 Mt/yr large point sources (44% of QC emissions) located above storage." This is a specific, actionable number showing that CO₂ sources and storage are co-located.

4. **Clean Prosperity 2024 citation** — Prospective storage estimate of 2,800–3,200 Mt is cited with section and table number.

5. **Jurisdictional complexity acknowledged** — The Evenick basin boundary issue (St. Lawrence Lowlands is not a separate Evenick basin; it falls within Appalachian UBI 39 fringe) is transparently noted in the header.

## Issues & Recommendations

### 1. INRS Pre-Feasibility Currency

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| INRS Chair in Geological CO₂ Sequestration (2010-2014) | The most recent INRS publications cited are from 2013 (Bédard et al., Energy Procedia) and 2010 (GeoConvention). The Chair ended in 2014. There has been ~11 years of no active CCS research in the St. Lawrence Lowlands. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a note on the current status of CCS research in Quebec: whether the INRS Chair was renewed, whether MERN has ongoing CCS assessment programs, or whether CCS research in Quebec has effectively lapsed. |

### 2. Prospective Resource vs. Viable Storage

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Clean Prosperity 2024 estimate: 2,800–3,200 Mt | This is a "prospective" resource estimate — meaning it assumes favorable geological conditions without site-specific characterization. The INRS studies suggest significant geological uncertainties (variable porosity, thrust belt complexity, fault compartmentalization). |
| **Recommendation** | Add a distinction between prospective resource (Clean Prosperity 2024) and effective/viable storage (which the INRS studies were working toward but did not finalize). Ideally, add a breakdown of how much of the 2.8–3.2 Gt is contingent vs. prospective. |

### 3. Saint-Flavien Gas Field Potential

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Saint-Flavien is mentioned only in passing (line 53) | Saint-Flavien is the most significant gas discovery in Quebec (produced ~17 Bcf from Trenton-Black River in thrust sheets). A depleted gas field in the Lowlands would be the most viable candidate for pilot-scale CCS — but this is not developed. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a subsection on Saint-Flavien's potential as a depleted-field CCS target: produced volumes, reservoir pressure history, current status, and any operator interest in CCS. |

### 4. Missing Policy Developments

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Quebec *Plan pour une économie verte* (PEV) 2030 | Quebec's climate plan includes CCUS as a stated priority, but the document does not reference PEV or any Quebec-specific CCS policy framework. If there are PEV-related funding programs or CCS feasibility studies since 2020, they should be documented. |
| **Recommendation** | Search for post-2020 Quebec CCS policy: PEV 2030 references to CCUS, MERN strategic plans, or NRCan-funded Quebec CCS studies that postdate the INRS Chair. |

### 5. CCS1 Well Relevancy

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| CCS1 Well (Cape Breton, 2014) listed in assessments | As noted in the Scotian review, CCS1 is a Carboniferous basin well in Cape Breton (NS), not the St. Lawrence Lowlands. The document itself notes this but still includes it in the assessment table. |
| **Recommendation** | Move CCS1 Well to a clearly separate "Related Assessments (Other Basins)" section. |

### 6. Minor Issues

- **Line 7** — "Evenick 2021 does not list 'St. Lawrence Lowlands' as a separate basin" — this is correct as noted. However, the INRS literature consistently refers to "St. Lawrence Lowlands" as a distinct CCS assessment region. A note on this terminological tension would help downstream users.
- **No mention of the **Gaspé Peninsula** — the Gaspé has significant petroleum exploration history (Galt No. 1 gas discovery, Junex shale gas) and is in the Appalachian Basin. If any CCS assessment exists for Gaspé, it should be noted. If not, a negative finding should be stated.
- **No mention of **Quebec's carbon market (WCI/CaT)** integration with CCS — Quebec is part of the Western Climate Initiative cap-and-trade system. The document could note whether Quebec CCS would generate offset credits under this system.
- **No discussion of **Hydro-Québec's potential role** — Hydro-Québec's abundant low-carbon electricity could power capture facilities, a unique advantage for Quebec vs. other provinces.
