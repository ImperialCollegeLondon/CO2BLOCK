## Reservoir Thickness

### Primary Values

| Value | Type | Source (Tier) | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|------|---------------|----------------|
| **40 m gross** | Gross reservoir thickness | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) [T2-CP] | *"The BCS is a 40m thick reservoir with the highest quality sands in the bottom 30m perforated for injection."* |
| **30 m** | Perforated / injection interval | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) [T2-CP] | *"...bottom 30m perforated for injection."* |
| **40–48 m (mean 44 m)** | Gross reservoir thickness | Shell, Gen-4 Report (2011) [T1-Reg] | *"BCS... 40m to 48m with a mean of 44m"* |
| **~45 m** | Gross thickness (average) | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) [T1-PJ] — citing BCS | *"The formation has an average thickness of approximately 45 m"* |
| **38 m** | Net sand | Rock & O'Brien, AAPG #80577 (2017) [T2-CP] | Slide shows *"Net Sand - 38m"* for Radway 8-19 well |
| **40–80 m** | Regional range | AGS PRS 2024-001 (Herbers et al.) [T1-Gov] | *"The Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS), 40-80 m thick at burial depths of 1000-3600 m"* |
| **45.7 m (150 ft)** | Gross thickness | carbonstorage.io [T3-Comp] | *"Gross thickness (ft): 150"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- **Gross vs net resolved**: GHGT-15 says 40 m gross with bottom 30 m perforated (net pay for injection). AAPG independently reports 38 m net sand. The N/G ratio of 0.9 reconciles these: 40 m × 0.9 = 36 m, consistent with 38 m.
- The Gen-4 model (Shell's own reservoir model) gives a range of 40–48 m with mean 44 m across the full Area of Interest (~180 × 190 km).
- The AGS survey gives a broader regional range (40–80 m) as the BCS formation thins toward the northeast (where it pinches out at the Sub-Cretaceous unconformity) and thickens toward the southwest.
- The carbonstorage.io value (45.7 m = 150 ft) is a tertiary source; the units suggest it derives from a pre-metric Shell report. It is consistent with other sources but **not independently verifiable**.
- **Recommended value**: Gross thickness **40–45 m**; injection interval (perforated net pay) **30 m**; net sand **~38 m** (N/G 0.9 fully consistent).

## Porosity

### Primary Values

| Value | Method (where known) | Source (Tier) | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|----------------------|---------------|----------------|
| **~17%** | Formation-scale average (injection interval) | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) [T2-CP] | *"The high-quality injection interval has an average of ~17% porosity... across the Storage Complex."* |
| **~17%** | Formation-scale average | Rock et al., GHGT-13 (2017), Energy Procedia [T1-PJ] | *"...the storage reservoir, with average porosity of about 17%..."* |
| **~17%** | Formation-scale average | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) [T1-PJ] | *"porosity of about 17%"* |
| **17%** | Well log (Radway 8-19) | Rock & O'Brien, AAPG #80577 (2017) [T2-CP] | Slide shows *"Porosity – 17 %"* |
| **14% (mid-case)** | Full-zone model average | Shell, Gen-4 Report (2011) [T1-Reg] | *"Mid Case average 14%"* |
| **0.16 (16%)** | Core / log analysis (Well 8-19) | Shell, D-65 Application [T1-Reg] | Porosity log analysis value |
| **0.11–0.19** | Range across all wells | Shell, D-65 Application [T1-Reg] | Range across injection and monitoring wells |
| **8–21%** | Core plug measurements (three facies zones) | Radway 8-19 BCS Geomechanics Report (2011) [T1-Reg] | *"In-situ measured porosities on samples from the three zones were spread over the range 8 to 21 percent."* |

### Confirming Evidence

- The convergence on 17% is widely cited but **not truly independent** — GHGT-15, GHGT-13, IJGGG, and AAPG all share Shell/Rock authorship. These represent **two independent data streams**: (1) the Shell corporate/model stream (Gen-4, D-65, GHGT presentations), and (2) the AGS government survey (regional context only).
- The Gen-4 mid-case (14%) is lower because it averages over the full BCS zone including lower-porosity sections. The N/G ratio of ~0.9 means the sand-prone intervals have higher porosity (~17%).
- The Radway 8-19 geomechanics report provides the most granular data: thin-section porosity up to 20.7% (measured plug porosity 18.8%) for the high-quality zone, with an overall range of 8% to 21% across all three facies zones. This confirms that the 17% average is plausible for the high-quality interval but that significant heterogeneity exists.
- **Measurement Methodology**: D-65 values are likely a mix of log-derived and core-plug porosity. The geomechanics report specifies helium porosimetry on core plugs (±0.5 p.u. accuracy). Log-derived porosities (density/neutron) have ±1–2 p.u. uncertainty. The 17% value from GHGT-15 is described as an "average across the Storage Complex," suggesting it is a model-weighted average rather than a direct measurement.
- **Recommended value**: **17%** (high-quality injection interval). Use **14%** for full-formation average. The 8–21% core-plug range captures the heterogeneity.

## Permeability

### Primary Values

| Value | Type / Scale | Source (Tier) | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **20–500 mD** | Core plug / well-test range (injection wells 2-10, 3-4, 5-35, 7-11) | Shell, D-65 Application [T1-Reg] | Well-test and core analysis range across injection wells |
| **150 mD** | Core plug measurement (Well 8-19) | Shell, D-65 Application [T1-Reg] | Core analysis permeability at deep monitoring well |
| **~1000 mD** | Formation-scale average (stated) | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) [T2-CP] | *"The high-quality injection interval has an average of... ~1000mD permeability across the Storage Complex."* |
| **Darcy-range** | Formation-scale (stated) | Rock et al., GHGT-13 (2017) [T1-PJ] | Describes BCS permeability in the darcy range |
| **~1000 md** | Formation-scale (citing GHGT-15) | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) [T1-PJ] | *"average permeability close to 1000 md"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- ⚠️ **CRITICAL: This is the weakest property in the dataset.** The ~1000 mD value traces to a **single source chain**: GHGT-15 (conference presentation) → cited by IJGGG, paraphrased by GHGT-13. There is no independent peer-reviewed measurement confirming 1000 mD at the Quest site.
- The **regulatory D-65 data shows 20–500 mD** across multiple wells (injection wells 2-10, 3-4, 5-35, 7-11, plus monitoring well 8-19). This is **demonstrably the harder, more conservative data** from directly measured core plugs and well tests.
- The Gen-4 report's effective permeability in the reservoir model has not been publicly confirmed at the 1000 mD level. If the Gen-4 model used 1000 mD to match pressure data, that would be supporting evidence; if it used lower values, that would contradict the 1000 mD claim. The Gen-4 data was not available for review on this point.
- The scale discrepancy explanation (core plug 150 mD vs formation-scale 1000 mD) is physically plausible but unconfirmed at Quest: permeability measured at the core-plug scale (~1 in³) samples only the rock matrix, while formation-scale values capture high-permeability streaks and fractures that may not be represented in core. However, a 7× difference requires justification.
- **Recommended weighting**: Present **20–500 mD (D-65)** as the primary, measured permeability range. The **~1000 mD** value is the **optimistic end-member** from a single conference source chain. For conservative capacity estimates, use the D-65 range. The 150 mD point value from Well 8-19 is the single best-characterized measurement.
- Permeability anisotropy (Kv/Kh) of ~0.01–0.1 (from GHGT-15) means CO₂ flows preferentially horizontally — this ratio also rests on a single source but is physically reasonable for sandstone. Cross-validation not available.

### SLA Calculation from AER Township Description

The AER Closure Plan lists the following townships in Table 2-1:

| Township | Ranges (W4M) |
|----------|-------------|
| 63 | 22, 21, 20 |
| 62 | 23, 22, 21, 20, 19 |
| 61 | 24, 23, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18 |
| 60 | 24, 23, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18 |
| 59 | 23, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18 |
| 58 | 23, 22, 21, 20, 19 |
| 57 | 22, 21, 20, 19 |
| 56 | 20, 19, and 21 (sections 25–36 only) |

- 39 full townships × 36 sections/township = 1,404 sections
- 12 additional sections (Township 56, Range 21, sections 25–36) = 12 sections
- Total: 1,416 sections × 640 acres/section = **906,240 acres**
- 906,240 acres × 0.00404686 km²/acre = **~3,667 km²**

### Confirming Evidence

- The AER Closure Plan is the authoritative regulatory source.
- Lahvis et al. (2017, Energy Procedia) confirms ~3,700 km² — rounds to the same value.
- The carbonstorage.io value (164,639 acres = 666 km²) is unexplained and conflicts with the AER-defined SLA. **The SLA value from the AER takes precedence.** The carbonstorage.io value may represent an initial lease block, subset, or data entry error.
- **Do NOT confuse**: SLA (~3,700 km²) with the Gen-4 model area (~34,200 km²), the AoR (variable: 461–3,780 km²), the seismic survey area (435 km²), or the actual plume footprint (~30 km²). Each serves a different purpose.
- **Recommended value for SLA**: **~3,700 km²** (exactly 3,667 km² from the AER township calculation).

## Notes on Data Quality

### 1. Source Reliability Assessment

| Property | Truly independent sources | Verdict |
|----------|--------------------------|---------|
| **Thickness** | 3 (GHGT-15, Gen-4, AGS) | **Good** — Gen-4 and GHGT-15 are different Shell teams; AGS is fully independent |
| **Porosity** | 2 (Gen-4/Shell stream, D-65/Regulatory stream) | **Adequate** — the 5-way convergence on 17% is misleading due to shared authorship; the Radway 8-19 geomechanics report provides granular core data |
| **Permeability** | 1 (D-65 regulatory data) | **Weak** — the ~1000 mD value is a single-source chain (GHGT-15 → IJGGG). No independent third-party measurement confirms it. **Use D-65 (20–500 mD) as primary**. |
| **Area** | 3 (AER, Lahvis, Brown/NRAP) | **Good** — AER is legally authoritative; Lahvis is independent journal confirmation; Brown/NRAP provides AoR evolution data |

### 2. Permeability Discrepancy — Critical Explanation

The 1000 mD (GHGT-15) vs 20–500 mD (D-65) discrepancy is the most significant issue in this dataset:

- The D-65 values (20–500 mD) come from **regulatory well-test and core data** across 5+ wells at the Quest site. These are direct physical measurements.
- The ~1000 mD value originates from **a single conference presentation** (GHGT-15) and is repeated by papers with overlapping authorship.
- The document originally weighted the 1000 mD as primary; **this revision reverses that weighting**. The D-65 range is the primary data.
- For capacity or injectivity calculations, the range **20–500 mD** should be used for the base case, with **~1000 mD** as an optimistic end-member.

### 3. Area Confusion Resolution

Multiple area concepts exist for Quest; they serve different purposes and should not be interchanged:
- **SLA (3,667 km²)**: Legal/regulatory boundary for pore space tenure.
- **AoR (461 km² revised)**: Active MMV monitoring boundary.
- **Seismic survey (435 km²)**: Area covered by baseline 3D seismic.
- **CO₂ plume (~30 km²)**: Actual subsurface footprint of injected CO₂.
- **Model area (~34,200 km²)**: Simulation model domain — not a storage boundary.

### 4. Missing Data — CO₂ Fluid Properties

The original document omitted CO₂ density and viscosity, which are essential for any capacity or injectivity calculation. At Quest reservoir conditions (21 MPa, 60°C):
- CO₂ is supercritical with density ≈ 750 kg/m³
- CO₂ viscosity ≈ 0.05–0.06 cP
These values are standard for supercritical CO₂ at these conditions (verified against NIST REFPROP).

### 5. Residual Trapping

No Quest-specific residual gas saturation (Sgr) measurement was found in publicly available sources. Sgr is typically 0.15–0.40 for sandstone reservoirs in analogous CCS projects. This remains a gap — for full capacity assessment, either a Quest-specific measurement or an analog value with appropriate uncertainty bounds would be needed.

### 6. All Values Are Quest-Specific

All values are for the Quest-specific location within the regional BCS aquifer. BCS properties vary significantly across Alberta (the AGS survey notes the formation spans 1,000–3,600 m burial depth with correspondingly variable properties). These values should **not** be applied to other CCS projects targeting the BCS without site-specific verification.
