## Regulatory Gaps & Uncertainties

The existing regulatory framework (Directive 065, MMA, OGCA, TIER) is well-established for project-level approvals. However, several gaps deserve explicit note:

### a) Commercial-Scale Closure Framework
- **Quest** is the only project with a published, regulator-approved MMV plan. Directive 065 Appendix P provides closure criteria, but **no project has reached the closure stage**.
- The regulatory framework for **post-closure liability transfer to the Crown** (per MMA) is untested at commercial scale. Financial assurance requirements for large hubs (e.g., Pathways at C$16.5B+) are not publicly specified.

### b) Long-Term Liability
- Canada lacks a federal CCS liability mechanism analogous to the US EPA Class VI primacy or the UK CO₂ Storage Licensing regime.
- Alberta's *Mines and Minerals Act* transfers liability to the Crown post-closure, but the financial assurance mechanisms for mega-projects remain opaque.

### c) Cross-Border Storage
- All Alberta CCS projects are within a single province, which is correct — no cross-jurisdictional transport or storage has been filed.
- However, the WCSB extends into British Columbia, Saskatchewan, and Montana/North Dakota. No projects currently propose storage in BC/Saskatchewan portions, but this should be noted for completeness.

### d) TIER Carbon Price Freeze
- In May 2025, Alberta froze the TIER Fund credit price at C$95/tonne, abandoning scheduled escalation to C$170 by 2030 (oilauthority.com, May 2025). This directly affects project economics at C$16.5–30B scale.

### e) Pore Space Conflicts
- The Origins project faced AER regulatory appeals from E3 Lithium (lithium brine rights) and PrairieSky Royalty (mineral rights), both dismissed Apr 14, 2026 (AER Appeal #1959099, #1959100). These cases establish precedent for pore space allocation between CCS and mineral extraction.

### Interference & Interconnection Risks

A cross-cutting analysis of pore-space competition among projects targeting the same formations:

| Risk | Projects Affected | Analysis |
|------|-------------------|----------|
| **BCS pressure interference** | Quest, Polaris/Atlas, Wolf Lamont | All three target the Basal Cambrian Sands in the Radway–Fort Saskatchewan–Willingdon corridor. While Quest injects at Radway (~30 km NE of Fort Saskatchewan), Atlas and Wolf Lamont are in adjacent township ranges (Twp 54–56, Rge 16–18 W4M). Directive 065 requires containment analysis per project, but cumulative pressure effects across multiple nearby sequestration schemes are not addressed in any single regulatory filing. |
| **ACTL trunk line capacity** | Wolf Lamont (tie-in), existing ACTL users | ACTL pipeline design is 14.6 Mtpa; current injection is ~2–3 Mtpa (Enhance Energy Clive EOR). Wolf Lamont plans to connect via ACTL. When the pipeline and disposal formation are shared, coordination of injection rates, pressure management, and maintenance scheduling becomes critical. |
| **Leduc Formation competition** | ACTL/Clive (D-3A pool), Origins (Lacombe) | Origins targets the Leduc Formation in the Lacombe area (Twp 39, Rge 25 W4M), while ACTL/Clive injects into the Leduc D-3A pool (Twp 39–40, Rge 24–25 W4M). These are adjacent areas within the same Bashaw reef complex. Origins may tap a separate pool within the Leduc or compete for regional reservoir pressure support. Origins MMV Plan (Dec 2024) notes that site selection leveraged Clive project experience, suggesting geological continuity. |
| **Wabamun & Leduc proximal storage** | Wabamun (BSU/Nisku), Origins (Leduc) | Both projects are in the Central Alberta reef trend. Wabamun targets the BSU at ~3,000 m depth; Origins targets the Leduc at ~2,070 m. Different formations, but hydraulic communication through the Woodbend-Winterburn-Wabamun hydrostratigraphic sequence is possible (Origins MMV Plan §2.3 Hydrogeology acknowledges this). |

**Recommendation**: AER should consider cumulative pressure management across the Edmonton–Lacombe CO₂ injection corridor as project density increases.

### Induced Seismicity Risk

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **WCSB seismicity context** | The WCSB has documented induced seismicity from hydraulic fracturing in the Duvernay (Fox Creek) and Montney plays. Deep CO₂ injection into carbonates (Leduc) and basal clastics (BCS) can alter pore pressure and potentially trigger slip on pre-existing faults. |
| **Quest** | Dedicated microseismic monitoring array; no induced events above perceptible thresholds reported (Quest Annual Reports; CCS Knowledge 2025 case study). |
| **Origins** | MMV Plan (Dec 2024) explicitly includes induced seismicity monitoring: "Passive monitoring stations — continuous — with submissions annually" (Origins MMV Plan, p. 2). Baseline established pre-injection. |
| **ACTL/Clive** | Clive is a mature waterflooded reservoir. EOR operations have not reported induced seismicity, but the long-term effect of transitioning from water to CO₂ injection on fault stability is not well characterized. |
| **Wolf Lamont & Atlas** | Both in pre-injection phase. Atlas is in AER regulatory review. No public MMV plan detailing seismicity monitoring was found as of Jul 2026. |
| **Wabamun** | BSU at ~3,000 m targets deep basal clastics. NRCan-funded 3D seismic is ongoing. Induced seismicity risk assessment methodology expected as part of AER Directive 065 application. |
| **Pathways** | Proposed Cold Lake BCS injection at 1,000–2,000 m depth. Cold Lake area has existing natural seismicity (due to steam injection); baseline characterization will be critical. |

**Cross-cutting assessment**: Projects in the BCS (Quest, Atlas, Wolf Lamont, Pathways) and Leduc (ACTL, Origins) should implement continuous microseismic monitoring as a condition of Directive 065 approval. Quest provides a successful operating model. A cumulative seismicity baseline for the Edmonton–Cold Lake corridor is warranted as injection density increases.

## Conclusion

**Alberta hosts 8 major CCS projects at various stages — all within the Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin (Evenick Alberta Basin, UBI 13).** Regulatory primacy rests with the AER under Directive 065 and the *Mines and Minerals Act* pore space tenure regime. Three projects are **operational** (Quest, ACTL/Clive, NWR Capture), one **under construction** (Polaris/Atlas), two **AER-approved** (Wolf Lamont, Origins), one **in evaluation/sequestration agreement** (Wabamun), and one **in federal/provincial regulatory application** (Pathways). Total operational storage: **>9.8 Mt cumulative as of 2024** (AER ST98; combined CCS); planned capacity: **>50 Mtpa by 2030**.

No CCS projects exist in Alberta outside the WCSB/Alberta Basin. All basin assignments verified spatially and stratigraphically against primary regulatory coordinates.

### Cross-Cutting Risks Summary
- **Pore-space interference**: BCS corridor (Radway–Fort Saskatchewan–Willingdon) has Quest, Atlas, and Wolf Lamont in close proximity. Cumulative pressure effects not addressed per-project.
- **Induced seismicity**: Addressed in Quest and Origins MMV plans; not yet documented for Atlas, Wolf Lamont, or Wabamun in public filings.
- **Regulatory gaps**: Closure framework untested; TIER price freeze (May 2025) affects project economics; federal CCS liability mechanism absent.
- **Cost escalation**: Pathways original C$16.5B estimate revised to C$20–30B by 2026; TIER price uncertainty amplifies financial risk.
