## Issues & Recommendations

### 1. Underdeveloped Mallik Research Significance

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Mallik CO₂-CH₄ exchange is the most significant Tier 3–4 activity | The document covers the basics (1998-2008 timeline, GSC/JOGMEC, field tests), but does not discuss the scientific significance: Mallik is one of the world's most studied gas hydrate sites, and the CO₂-CH₄ exchange concept has been tested at the Ignik Sikumi field trial (Alaska, 2012) and the Shenhu area (China). |
| **Recommendation** | Add a brief note placing Mallik in the context of global hydrate-related CO₂ storage research, and note the Alaska/Ignik Sikumi field trial (US DOE, 2012) as the closest analog. |

### 2. Missing Climate/Emissions Context

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| NWT emissions profile | Beaufort-Mackenzie CCS would address either (a) NWT local emissions (small: ~2 Mt/yr, mostly diesel generation and mines), (b) offshore oil platform emissions (hypothetical, since no new Beaufort Sea oil development is active), or (c) imported CO₂ (unrealistic given distance). The document does not discuss any of these scenarios. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a paragraph on NWT emissions magnitude and sources to contextualize why CCS is absent. |

### 3. Regulatory Framework — Missing Detail

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| CER listed as "No CCS regulation" for NWT | Correct for the *Oil and Gas Operations Act* and *Canada Oil and Gas Operations Act* (COGOA). However, CER's *Carbon Capture, Transportation and Storage Regulatory Framework* (2023-2024 consultation) could be noted as a potential future federal mechanism for northern basins. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a note on CER's emerging CCS regulatory framework (even if not yet enacted) as potential future applicability to northern offshore. |

### 4. Permafrost CO₂ Storage — Missed Opportunity

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| "Permafrost/Sub-permafrost CO₂ Storage" (Frontiers 2023) | This academic study is brief (8 lines). Given that permafrost CO₂ storage as hydrate is one of the few viable CCS concepts for Arctic regions, this deserves more development. |
| **Recommendation** | Expand this section with: (a) how permafrost CO₂ hydrate storage works (thermodynamic stability), (b) estimated capacity range for the Mackenzie Delta permafrost zone, (c) comparison with similar work in Alaska/Norway. |

### 5. Tier 5–7 Exclusions

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Items listed are appropriate but incomplete | The Beaufort-Mackenzie has a history of Arctic research stations, climate change observatories, and proposed methane hydrate production pilots (e.g., Aurora College, NRCan PERD program). |
| **Recommendation** | Consider adding: Aurora Research Institute CCS-related studies; NRCan's PERD (Program of Energy Research and Development) hydrate research; Mackenzie Gas Project pipeline CO₂ transport possibilities (the pipeline was cancelled, but right-of-way land use could be noted). |

### 6. Minor Issues

- **Line 36** — "Mallik Gas Hydrate / CO₂-CH₄ Exchange (1998-2008)" — the timeline should note that while the field program ended in 2008, GSC/JOGMEC publications and laboratory studies continued through at least 2015.
- **No citation for "GHG emissions from Beaufort Sea hypothetical development"** — the Stantec/CIRNAC report URL is listed but no GHG estimate range is given. If available, include the estimated emissions magnitude.
- **Geological storage potential in the Taglu sequence** (the primary gas reservoir of the Mackenzie Delta) is not mentioned. The Taglu field alone has significant gas production history and could be evaluated for depleted field storage.
