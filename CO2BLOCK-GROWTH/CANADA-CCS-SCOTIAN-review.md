## Strengths

1. **Exceptional capacity estimate documentation** — Four independent estimates (KUST Monte Carlo, IIT, Organizer base case, NRCan/NS Gov't) are presented with their methodologies and data sources (40 wells, Petrel framework, sonic porosity). This is the most thoroughly documented zero-project basin in the set.

2. **Clear formation-by-formation breakdown** — Missisauga (Upper/Middle primary aquifer), Logan Canyon, Abenaki Carbonate Bank, Argo Salt caverns — each with seal, depth, and capacity range. This enables direct comparison with Alberta/WCSB storage formations.

3. **MCTL (Maritimes Carbon Trunk Line) concept documented** — The pipeline repurposing concept is correctly identified as an infrastructure concept, not a project. Critical for understanding what would be needed to commercialize Scotian Basin storage.

4. **Barriers to Development section** is the strongest among the zero-project files — six categories (regulatory, emission sources, infrastructure, economics, seismic/geomechanical, public acceptance) with specific detail.

5. **GSC Open File 8996 correctly scoped** — The document correctly notes that GSC 8996 covers Carboniferous sandstones in Maritime provinces, not the Scotian Basin proper. This prevents cross-basin confusion.

6. **Argo salt cavern potential** identified — This is an important secondary storage option (salt caverns for H₂/CO₂) that is often overlooked in CCS assessments.

## Issues & Recommendations

### 1. NS CCUS Strategy Document Status

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| NRCan *Carbon capture, utilization and storage in Nova Scotia* (Jan 7, 2025) | Described as a "strategic assessment" and "government policy." It is unclear whether this document represents official NS government policy, an NRCan resource assessment, or a consultant report. |
| **Recommendation** | Add the exact URL and document type (webpage, PDF, report number) to clarify the authority level. Check whether this was produced by NRCan or jointly with NS Dept NRR. |

### 2. Capacity Range Presentation

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Line 38: "Median 177 Gt; Low 14 Gt; High 1,280 Gt (shelf)" | This is a 91× range from low to high. The document presents this as a single estimate without discussing what drives the uncertainty (storage efficiency factor, pore volume assumptions, seal capacity). |
| **Recommendation** | Add a brief note on the key uncertainty drivers for the Scotian Basin capacity estimates (e.g., storage efficiency factor assumption range of 1–6%). |

### 3. Overlap with Grand Banks / EGU26-770

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| EGU26-770 appears in BOTH Scotian and Grand Banks documents | Lines 42 (Scotian) and 91–100 (Scotian §5) include the EGU26-770 abstract, which is a Grand Banks study. The document itself notes it covers "Grand Banks / Scotian margin." This creates potential double-counting. |
| **Recommendation** | Either (a) denote EGU26-770 as covering the Grand Banks-to-Scotian margin continuum in both documents with a cross-reference, or (b) assign it definitively to one basin. |

### 4. OERA / Dalhousie Reference Detail

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Lines 77–78: "Phased Approach (UiS/KUST)" | Reference to phases and methodology is clear, but no specific OERA report number or DOI is provided for the Phases 1–4 framework. |
| **Recommendation** | Add OERA report numbers or DOIs for each phase where available. |

### 5. CCS1 Well Relevancy

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| CCS1 Well (Cape Breton, 2014) appears in BOTH Scotian and St. Lawrence/Appalachian files | The document correctly notes it is "NOT Scotian Basin proper" (Carboniferous, onshore NS). However, its inclusion in the Tier 3–4 assessment table alongside Scotian Basin assessments is potentially misleading. |
| **Recommendation** | Move CCS1 Well to a "Related Assessment (Adjacent Basins)" subsection explicitly separated from the Scotian Basin assessments. |

### 6. Missing CO₂ Source Detail

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Barriers section (line 138): "NS emissions small (~14 Mt/yr); major emitters in QC/ON require 1,000+ km pipeline" | This is a key economic constraint but no source is cited for the 14 Mt/yr figure or the QC/ON emitter distance. |
| **Recommendation** | Add NRCan or Environment Canada emissions inventory citation. |

### 7. Minor Issues

- **Line 38** — NS CCUS Strategy is listed as Tier 2. It includes regulatory/policy information, which is Tier 2 appropriate, but much of the data is Tier 3 (capacity estimates). Consider adding a mixed-tier notation.
- **Line 78** — "Phase 4: Implementation" — this phase was never reached. Add a status update.
- **No mention of the **Sable Offshore Energy** decommissioning** now underway (2025+). This represents a potential CO₂ source (platform power generation) and potential storage (depleted fields) opportunity that could accelerate Scotian CCS.
- **No discussion of LNG Canada / Pacific Energy's potential influence** on Atlantic CCS economics — this is a national competitive context issue that may affect federal CCS fund allocation.
