## Executive Summary

This report compiles reservoir properties for 12 Canadian CCS projects from individually revised property files. **8 Alberta Basin (WCSB) projects** target either the **Basal Cambrian Sands (BCS)** — a Cambrian-Ordovician cratonic sandstone saline aquifer — or the **Leduc Formation** (Upper Devonian carbonate reef). **4 Williston Basin projects** target the **Midale Beds** (Mississippian carbonate, CO₂-EOR), the **Deadwood Formation / Black Island Member** (Cambro-Ordovician sandstone saline aquifer), and associated capture/storage infrastructure.

**Alberta Basin**: Quest (operational, >7 Mt stored) and ACTL/Clive (operational, ~4–7 Mt stored) are the only active injection sites. Polaris/Atlas (FID June 2024, operations end-2028) and Wolf Lamont (AER approved Mar 2026) are under development. Wabamun (CSA Oct 2025) and Origins (AER approved Jul 2025) are advancing. Pathways Alliance (pre-FID, ~6 Mtpa MOU target) is the largest proposed project. NWR Sturgeon is a capture-only facility feeding ACTL/Clive.

**Williston Basin**: Weyburn (CO₂-EOR since 2000, ~35+ Mt net stored) and Midale (CO₂-EOR since 2005, ~3+ Mt stored) are mature commercial EOR projects. Aquistore (saline aquifer injection since 2015, >585 kt stored) is the most comprehensively monitored CCS site globally. BD3 (SaskPower, since 2014) is the capture facility supplying both.

Key cross-formation observations: BCS projects span extreme permeability ranges (<1 mD to >1,000 mD) across different fairways. Leduc carbonate projects show moderate porosity (6–8%) with wide permeability ranges (1–1,000 mD). Midale Beds exhibit a unique bimodal character: high-porosity/low-permeability Marly dolostone vs. lower-porosity/higher-permeability Vuggy limestone.

Phase 2/3 was proposed in 2017 but withdrawn in 2018 with no active development. As of 2024, cumulative ACTL system storage (all sources) is >10 Mt.

## 3. Basin-Level Patterns and Notes

### 3.1 Alberta Basin — BCS Projects

The 5 BCS-targeting projects (Quest, Polaris/Atlas, Wolf Lamont, Wabamun, Pathways) span an extreme range of reservoir quality:

| Parameter | Quest (Best) | Polaris/Atlas | Wolf Lamont | Wabamun | Pathways (Cold Lake) |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|-------------|---------|---------------------|
| Depth (m) | ~2,000 | ~2,000 | ~1,800–2,200 | 2,300–3,300 | 1,000–1,500 |
| Porosity (%) | ~17 | ~10 (regional) | ~17 (Quest analog) | ~10 (8–12) | Unknown (likely <17) |
| Permeability (effective, mD) | 20–500 (measured); ~1,000 (optimistic) | **1–2** (Edmonton avg) | ~1,000 (formation-scale) | 0.01–1.6 (scenario) | 1–10 (estimated) |
| Status | Operational | Under construction | AER approved | CSA signed | Pre-FID |

Key observation: **Effective BCS permeability varies by >1,000×** across Alberta. The "high-perm streak network" that makes Quest viable is not uniformly developed. The AGS PRS 2024-001 finding that clay content controls permeability (2–3% increase → 3-order reduction) is the single most important geological constraint on BCS CCS viability in Alberta.

### 3.2 Alberta Basin — Leduc Carbonate Projects

The 3 Leduc-targeting projects (ACTL/Clive, Origins, and the WASP-era component of Wabamun) show:

| Parameter | ACTL/Clive | Origins |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| Gross thickness (m) | ~250 (total); 8.75 net | ~137 (at well) |
| Porosity (%) | 6.1 (zone avg) | 6 (well avg) |
| Permeability range (mD) | 1–1,000 | <10–>100 |
| Storage type | EOR + storage | Pressure-depleted saline aquifer |
| Status | Operational | AER approved |

Leduc carbonate reservoirs are consistently moderate-porosity (6–8% average) with wide permeability ranges reflecting facies control, dolomitization, and fracture/vug connectivity. The regional arithmetic mean permeability of ~26 mD (Weides & Majorowicz 2014, ~50,000 core analyses) is a robust benchmark.

### 3.3 Williston Basin — Midale Beds Projects

Weyburn and Midale share the same formation with virtually identical properties:

| Unit | Porosity (%) | Permeability (mD) | Thickness (m) |
|------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Marly dolostone | 25–26 (log); 17 (stressed core) | 6–10 (matrix); fracture-enhanced | 4–6 net |
| Vuggy limestone | 10–12 | 6–50 (matrix); up to 500 (shoal + fractures) | 8–15 net |

The Marly's high porosity at ~1,450 m is anomalous for carbonate at that depth and results from early dolomitization preserving intercrystalline porosity. This is the most thoroughly characterized CCS-EOR reservoir in the world (25+ years of IEA GHG research).

### 3.4 Williston Basin — Cambro-Ordovician Projects

The Aquistore Deadwood Formation is the same regional aquifer as the Alberta BCS but at ~3.2 km depth (vs. 1.5–2.0 km in Alberta). Comparative properties:

| Parameter | Aquistore (SK) | Quest (AB BCS) | Ratio |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|-------|
| Depth (m) | ~3,200 | ~2,000 | 1.6× |
| Porosity (%) | 6–7 | ~17 | ~0.4× |
| Core perm (mD) | 5 | 150 (Well 8-19) | ~0.03× |
| Effective perm (mD) | ~70–88 | ~1,000 | ~0.07–0.09× |
| Net pay (m) | ~112 | ~36 | 3× |

Aquistore demonstrates that deeply buried Cambrian sandstones can achieve adequate injectivity despite low matrix permeability due to high-permeability streaks and thermoelastic effects from cold CO₂ injection.

## 4. Data Quality Summary

### 4.1 Overall Source Reliability

| Quality Level | Projects |
|---------------|----------|
| **High** (multiple Tier 1–2 sources, site-specific) | Quest, Weyburn, Midale, Aquistore, BD3 |
| **Moderate** (mixture of Tier 1–3 sources, some site-specific data) | ACTL/Clive, Origins |
| **Low** (regional data only, pre-FID/pre-characterization) | Polaris/Atlas, Wolf Lamont, Wabamun, Pathways |
| **N/A** (capture facility — storage properties in separate files) | NWR Sturgeon |

### 4.2 Common Limitations

1. **Permeability is the weakest property across all projects**: Scale dependence (core plug → well-test → formation-scale) varies from 3× to >160×. Multiple projects rely on a single source chain for formation-scale permeability.

2. **Overlapping authorship inflates perceived cross-validation**: At Quest, the ~17% porosity and ~1,000 mD permeability convergence is partly due to shared Shell/Rock authorship across multiple publications — not truly independent validation.

3. **Area concepts are frequently conflated**: SLA vs. AoR vs. plume area vs. model area differ by factors of 10–100×. The SLA must never be used for pore-volume calculations without appropriate efficiency factors.

4. **Halite-induced injectivity damage is a cross-cutting risk** for all BCS projects due to the high-salinity brine in the Cambrian aquifer. Quest has demonstrated the problem (skin factor 147, damage zone permeability ~10 mD) and a remediation method (freshwater washes), but projects with native permeability 1–2 mD face a proportionally greater challenge.

5. **CO₂ density is often omitted or miscalculated**: At Weyburn conditions (15 MPa, 60°C), the correct range is 600–750 kg/m³ (not 700–800 kg/m³). At Aquistore, the temperature used for density calculation matters by ~7–11%.

6. **Pre-FID projects have no site-specific data**: Pathways, Polaris/Atlas, Wolf Lamont, and Wabamun all rely on regional BCS data or Quest analogs — the applicability of which varies with lateral position in the BCS fairway.

### 4.3 Recommendations for Database Users

- Use the **D-65 range (20–500 mD)** as the primary permeability for Quest, not the single-source ~1,000 mD value.
- For ACTL/Clive: treat all quantitative values as **"operator-cited, not independently verified"** — no Tier 1 sources exist.
- For Polaris/Atlas: use the **Hofmann 2013 Edmonton-region average (1–2 mD)** for permeability, not the Weides 2014 basin-wide average (~10 mD).
- For Aquistore: use **7% porosity** (site-specific log-based) rather than 21% (regional Black Island estimate).
- For Weyburn and Midale: use **log-derived Marly porosity (25–26%) for volumetric calculations** and **stressed core values (17%) for geomechanical modeling**.
- For Pathways and Wabamun: all values are **regional estimates** — clearly label as such in any downstream calculation.
