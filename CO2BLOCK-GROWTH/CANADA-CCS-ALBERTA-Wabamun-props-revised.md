### Depth-Dependent Porosity Reduction (Athy's Law)

The original file stated that "an additional ~5–10% porosity loss is expected" from the additional ~300–1,300 m of burial at Wabamun vs. Quest. This is a qualitative estimate. For context, sandstone porosity generally follows Athy's law:

**φ = φ₀ × e^(−k × z)**

where φ₀ is initial porosity at deposition, k is a compaction coefficient, and z is depth. For Cambrian-age sandstones:
- **Scherer (1987)** — AAPG Bulletin 71(5), "Parameters Influencing Porosity in Sandstones": Reports that quartz arenites at 2,000–3,000 m typically retain 10–18% porosity, with compaction accounting for ~0.5–1.0 porosity units per 100 m of additional burial in this depth range.
- **Waples & Kamata (1993)** — Porosity–depth trends for basin modeling: Similar compaction coefficients for well-cemented sandstones.

At Quest depth (~2,000 m): ~16–17% porosity (measured).  
At Wabamun depth (~2,300–3,300 m): Applying a compaction rate of ~0.5–1.0 p.u./100 m (Scherer 1987) for the additional 300–1,300 m of burial yields an estimated reduction of ~1.5–10 porosity units, depending on the position within the depth range and local compaction history. This is consistent with the Weides (2014) measured value of ~10% at comparable depths in central Alberta.

**⚠ This is a regional compaction trend, not a Wabamun-specific measurement. The actual porosity will depend on local diagenetic history, quartz cementation, and overpressure history.**

### Recommended Value (BSU)

**~8–12%** (best estimate based on regional BSU data at comparable depths; 10% as the central value from Weides et al. 2014).  
- The **10% central value** is well-supported by Weides (2014) from 6 wells at comparable depths in central Alberta.  
- The **~8% lower bound** is inferred from Athy's law compaction trends applied at the deepest end of the Wabamun depth range (3,300 m), assuming tighter-than-average cementation.  
- The **~12% upper bound** reflects the possibility that the BSU at Wabamun is in the shallower portion of the depth range (2,300 m) with less compaction.  
- **⚠ Neither bound has been directly measured at the Wabamun site.**

## Permeability

### Basal Sandstone Unit (BSU) — Scenario-Based Evaluation

**⚠ Permeability is the most uncertain parameter for the Wabamun BSU.** Measured values span >160× between core-plug scale (<0.01 mD) and regional-scale estimates (1.6 mD). Rather than a single "representative" value, three scenarios are presented below with mechanistic justification.

| Scenario | Permeability Range | Scale / Context | Mechanistic Basis | Source |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|--------|
| **Low** (matrix-only) | **~0.01 mD** (≤1×10⁻¹⁴ m²) | Core-plug scale (96 samples, 6 wells) | Intrinsic matrix permeability measured by probe permeametry; no fracture contribution. This is the *in situ* matrix permeability of the BSU in central Alberta. | Weides et al. (2014) |
| **Mid** (effective, with natural fractures) | **~0.1–1.6 mD** | Regional effective scale | Scale-up from plug to regional scale accounting for natural fractures, larger-scale heterogeneity, and sub-seismic features. Hofmann (2013) reports 1.6 mD at the regional scale. However, the presence and connectivity of natural fractures in the BSU at Wabamun depths is **not confirmed** — this figure represents an upper bound for pre-stimulation effective permeability. | Hofmann et al. (2013) |
| **High** (post-hydraulic stimulation) | **~1–10 mD** (speculative) | Stimulated volume | Multiple sources (Hofmann 2013, Weides 2014) agree the BSU requires stimulation for economic injectivity. Fracture half-lengths of 100–300 m with moderate conductivity could yield effective permeability in this range. **This is speculative — no Wabamun-specific stimulation tests have been published.** | Inferred from Hofmann et al. (2013); no direct source |

### Key Supporting Notes

- **Weides et al. (2014)**: *"The average porosity and permeability of the BSU is 10% and <1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m², respectively."* — 96 probe permeametry samples from 6 wells. This is the most rigorously measured matrix permeability value.
- **Hofmann et al. (2013)**: *"Core test from the wells around Edmonton result in an average regional scale permeability of 1.6 mD."* — The paper describes this as a scale-up, not a direct measurement. The mechanism (fractures vs. heterogeneity vs. scale-dependent flow paths) is not fully explained and likely combines several effects.
- **Hofmann et al. (2013) on stimulation**: *"The Basal Sandstone Unit is well confined and has the lowest initial permeability. Therefore, this formation is the best suited for permeability enhancements by conventional gel-proppant fracturing treatments."* — Favourable for CCS injection with stimulation.
- **The plug-to-regional gap (factor >160×) is unusually large** for a well-cemented quartz arenite. In most tight sandstones, this gap is 10–100×. The >160× gap suggests either: (a) natural fractures contribute significantly to regional-scale permeability (unconfirmed at Wabamun), or (b) Hofmann's scale-up method uses different assumptions than the plug measurements. **This ambiguity has not been resolved in the published literature.**
- **Quest analog permeability (20–1,000 mD) is not applicable** — correctly flagged as inapplicable and overly optimistic for the deeper Wabamun BSU.

### Recommended Value for CO2BLOCK Use

Use **scenario-based ranges** depending on the modeling objective:

| Modeling Purpose | Recommended Permeability | Rationale |
|------------------|-------------------------|-----------|
| Resource / capacity screening | **0.1–1.6 mD** | Effective pre-stimulation, accounting for possible natural fractures |
| Injectivity modeling (pre-stimulation) | **0.01–0.1 mD** | Matrix-dominated; stimulation will be required |
| Injectivity modeling (post-stimulation) | **1–10 mD** | Speculative; depends on stimulation design and execution |
| Conservative (lower bound) | **0.01 mD** | Pure matrix permeability, no fracture contribution |

## Notes on Data Quality

### 1. Project-Specific vs. Regional Data

**There is very limited public project-specific reservoir data for the Wabamun Hub.** Enbridge has drilled two (possibly three) stratigraphic test wells, but the results (porosity, permeability, thickness encountered, injectivity test results) have not been published. The project is still in the evaluation phase. Key data gaps include:

- No public core analysis or well-log porosity/permeability from the Wabamun test wells
- No public injectivity test results
- No public 3D seismic interpretation of the BSU in the specific lease area
- No public storage capacity estimate (P50/P90) for the Wabamun Hub

### 2. Regional BSU Data Applicability

The best available data for the BSU in central Alberta comes from:

- **Weides et al. (2014)** — The most authoritative published study of BSU properties. Based on core analysis from 6 wells in central Alberta, but none are in the immediate Wabamun lease area. Porosity (avg 10%) and permeability (avg <0.01 mD core-plug scale) are based on 96 probe-permeametry samples and helium pycnometry.

- **Hofmann et al. (2013)** — Regional-scale permeability of 1.6 mD after scale-up from plug to regional scale. The scale-up mechanism (fractures vs. heterogeneity) is not fully explained; the >160× gap between plug and regional values is unusually large.

- **Alberta Innovates Basal Aquifer study** — Gives a higher porosity (14.4%) for the basal aquifer system, but this is a broader regional average over a larger area (AB/SK/MB) and includes shallower, higher-porosity portions of the aquifer.

### 3. Quest Analog — Applicability and Limitations

The Shell Quest CCS project (operational since 2015) targets the Basal Cambrian Sands (BCS), which is the same formation as the BSU. However, there are critical differences:

| Parameter | Quest (BCS) | Wabamun (BSU) | Impact |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|--------|
| Depth | ~1,800–2,100 m | **2,300–3,300 m** | Deeper = lower porosity, lower permeability due to compaction |
| Location | Fort Saskatchewan area (NE of Edmonton) | **Wabamun area (W of Edmonton)** | Different diagenetic history; deeper basin position |
| Reservoir quality | Excellent (17% φ, 20–500 mD) | **Expected lower** | Direct application of Quest values would be **overly optimistic** |

**Quest data should NOT be used as a direct analog for Wabamun.** The BSU at Wabamun depths will have significantly lower porosity and permeability due to greater burial depth and associated compaction. If Quest values are used in screening calculations, they must be adjusted downward (recommend applying a porosity reduction factor based on depth-dependent compaction trends, e.g., Athy's law parameters from Scherer 1987).

### 4. WASP Study Limitations

The Wabamun Area CO₂ Sequestration Project (WASP, ~2008–2012) was a University of Calgary-led study. Key limitations:
- WASP selected the **Nisku Formation** as its primary target, not the BSU
- The WASP study area (60×90 km) is much larger than the current Enbridge lease
- WASP was a feasibility study, not a site-specific characterization

### 6. Recommendations for CO2BLOCK Use

1. **For resource assessment (BSU)**: Use regional BSU values (porosity 8–12%, central value 10%; thickness ~40 m; areal extent ~150,000–220,000 ha depending on CSA vs. CSEA). **Clearly label all values as regional estimates.**

2. **For dynamic modeling**: Use scenario-based permeability ranges (see Permeability section above). Pre-stimulation injectivity is expected to be low (matrix 0.01 mD level). Inclusion of stimulated (fractured) scenarios is warranted.

3. **Key uncertainty**: The two stratigraphic test wells drilled in 2023–2024 may eventually yield public data through AER well records. Revisit this file if AER cores logs or test results become available.

4. **Quest analog values should not be used** without applying a depth-dependent property degradation factor (Athy's law, Scherer 1987). If used for bounding calculations, note that Quest values represent the optimistic/upper bound and Wabamun BSU properties are likely significantly lower.

5. **The Nisku Formation** (secondary target, 1,550–2,200 m depth) may have better reservoir properties than the deeper BSU but was the focus of the earlier WASP and Project Pioneer studies. No public core data are available for the Nisku in the Wabamun lease area either.

6. **Area use for capacity calculations**: Use 150,000 ha (CSA North, BSU-only) for a conservative estimate of BSU storage capacity. Use 220,000 ha for a total project estimate across both formations. The 734,192 acre figure from carbonstorage.io should be treated as unverified pending regulatory confirmation.
