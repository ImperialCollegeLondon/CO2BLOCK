#### INRS Currency Note — 11-Year Lapse

The INRS Chair in Geological CO₂ Sequestration ran from 2010 to 2014. No follow-on research chair, MERN CCS assessment program, or NRCan-funded Quebec CCS characterization study has been identified as active since 2014. The most recent peer-reviewed publication from the Chair is Bédard et al. (2013); the 2017 INRS 3D geological model (Bédard et al.) focused on geothermal rather than CCS. This represents an approximately 11-year gap with no active CCS-specific research or assessment in the St. Lawrence Lowlands. By contrast, other Canadian jurisdictions (AB, SK, BC) have maintained continuous CCS research programs through NRCan, provincial agencies, and industry consortia throughout this period. The absence of renewed research activity in Quebec correlates with the absence of a provincial CCS regulatory framework (see § Regulatory Framework).

#### Prospective vs. Viable Storage — Critical Distinction

The Clean Prosperity 2024 estimate of **2,800–3,200 Mt** is classified as **prospective storage resource** — the highest-level, most uncertain category in CCS resource classification (akin to "prospective resources" in petroleum exploration). It assumes favorable geological conditions across the basin but is not supported by site-specific characterization.

The INRS pre-feasibility studies (2010–2014) represent an attempt to move from prospective to **effective/viable storage** using the Bachu et al. (2007) methodology. The INRS work established basin-scale effective capacity for the Potsdam, Beekmantown, and Trenton/Black River/Chazy formations, incorporating porosity, permeability, and caprock integrity constraints at the basin scale. However, **site-scale matched capacity** — the next step that would identify specific injection sites with pore-volume-specific characterization — was **never finalized**. No site-specific storage efficiency factors, injectivity tests, or dynamic simulations were completed for any St. Lawrence Lowlands location.

Therefore:
- **Prospective resource** (Clean Prosperity 2024): 2,800–3,200 Mt — basin-wide volumetric, no site characterization
- **Effective capacity** (INRS 2010–2014): evaluated at basin scale but not apportioned between contingent and prospective
- **Contingent / viable storage**: **none quantified** — no site-specific characterization exists for any Quebec CCS target

This means the 2,800–3,200 Mt figure should be treated as a **geological ceiling with high uncertainty**, not a storage resource that could be developed without substantial additional characterization. Industry best practice (e.g., SPE/SEG SRMS, DOE NATCARB methodology) would downgrade most of this volume to sub-commercial or unevaluated categories pending site-specific work.

### 3. Saint-Flavien Depleted Gas Field — Potential CCS Target

| Attribute | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Location** | Saint-Flavien, Quebec (south shore of St. Lawrence, ~50 km SW of Quebec City) |
| **Discovery** | 1974 (gas discovery in Trenton-Black River formation, Appalachian thrust belt) |
| **Production** | ~17 Bcf cumulative gas production |
| **Reservoir** | Trenton-Black River Group (Middle Ordovician limestone/dolomite) in imbricated thrust slice |
| **Depth** | ~2,500 m |
| **Original Pressure** | ~30 MPa (estimated); substantially depleted by production |
| **Current Status** | **Depleted gas field** — production declined; field effectively exhausted |
| **Operator** | Historically operated by provincial/national interests; no current active CCS operator |
| **CCS Relevance** | Depleted gas field in regional thrust slice with proven trap, seal (Utica Shale), and reservoir properties; analogous to depleted-field CCS projects in other basins |
| **Challenges** | Thrust belt complexity: compartmentalization from imbricate faulting, limited field extent (~17 Bcf is small relative to CCS project requirements), uncertain reservoir continuity between thrust slices |
| **CCS Interest** | **None documented** — no operator or proponent has announced CCS evaluation of Saint-Flavien |

When considering a depleted gas field for CCS:
- **Capacity bound by produced volume**: At ~17 Bcf (~1 Mt equivalent assuming 0.058 t CO₂/Mcf displacement), the Saint-Flavien field alone would provide roughly **0.8–1.2 Mt CO₂ storage capacity** — sufficient for a pilot project but not commercial-scale storage
- **Thrust belt compartmentalization**: The imbricate thrust structure that traps gas also creates multiple isolated compartments, limiting continuous storage volume
- **Regional context**: If the broader thrust belt contains analogous depleted structures with similar cumulative production, aggregate capacity could be higher — but no comprehensive inventory exists

Saint-Flavien is the only proven hydrocarbon reservoir in the Quebec Lowlands with production history. Its small size relative to CCS project requirements and lack of operator interest mean it does not change St. Lawrence Lowlands' CCS project status.

### Post-2020 Policy: Plan pour une économie verte (PEV) 2030

Quebec's *Plan pour une économie verte* (PEV) 2030, adopted in 2020, is the province's primary climate policy framework. PEV 2030 targets a 37.5% GHG reduction below 1990 levels by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2050. The plan **mentions CCUS as a technology option** but does not include dedicated CCS funding programs, storage characterization mandates, or regulatory pathway commitments.

Key PEV/CCUS findings:
- **No PEV-specific CCS funding** allocated for storage characterization or pilot projects
- **No MERN CCS strategic plan** published under PEV
- **No NRCan–Quebec CCS bilateral agreements** identified (contrast with Alberta's NRCan–AB bilateral under NRCan's CCUS Strategy)
- **Quebec's 2030 Plan for Green Economy — 2024 Update**: continues to reference CCUS as emerging technology but no regulatory or fiscal framework for CCS deployment

**Status**: PEV 2030 creates policy cover for future CCS but has **not translated into actionable CCS programs** in the St. Lawrence Lowlands.

### Quebec Carbon Market (WCI/CaT) and CCS

Quebec is a participant in the Western Climate Initiative (WCI) cap-and-trade system (linked with California). Under WCI rules:
- CO₂ that is geologically sequestered **may be eligible for offset credits**, provided it meets quantification, monitoring, and permanence requirements
- Quebec has adopted WCI offset protocols for various sectors but **has not developed a CCS-specific offset protocol**
- The California Air Resources Board (CARB) has adopted CCS quantification methodology under its Low Carbon Fuel Standard; Quebec has not equivalent
- **No Quebec CCS project has applied for WCI offset credits** because no project exists

**Implication**: A future Quebec CCS project could theoretically generate WCI offset credits, but the regulatory pathway (protocol, MRV framework, permanence accounting) does not yet exist.

## Enabling Factors — Unique to Quebec

### Hydro-Québec's Potential Role

Quebec benefits from the lowest-carbon electricity grid in North America: Hydro-Québec generates over 99% of its electricity from hydroelectric sources, providing abundant low-cost, zero-emission electricity. This creates a structural advantage for CCS deployment:

- **Capture energy penalty**: Carbon capture processes (particularly amine-based post-combustion capture) require substantial steam and electricity. Quebec's hydroelectricity can supply this without additional emissions, avoiding the parasitic emission penalty that coal- or gas-fired capture imposes in other jurisdictions.
- **Industrial electrification synergy**: Hydro-Québec's industrial electrification programs could integrate with CCS at major emitter sites (cement, refining, steel) in the Montreal–Quebec City corridor.
- **No current CCS-electrification integration**: Despite this advantage, no study has quantified the life-cycle emissions benefit of Hydro-Québec-powered CCS for St. Lawrence Lowlands emitters.

**Note**: Hydro-Québec's potential role is currently **theoretical**. No Hydro-Québec CCS program, feasibility study, or partnership has been identified. The advantage is noted as a distinctive feature of the Quebec CCS context, not an active initiative.

## Conclusion

**St. Lawrence Lowlands / Quebec has ZERO CCS projects.** Only **Tier 3–4 assessments**: INRS pre-feasibility (2010–2014), Clean Prosperity 2024 prospective estimate (2.8–3.2 Gt effective / 100% prospective, 0% contingent/viable), and regional screening studies. **No CCS regulatory regime exists in Quebec** (no pore space tenure, no liability framework). Major emitters are present (~6.4 Mt/yr in the corridor) but no infrastructure exists. The Saint-Flavien depleted gas field is a documented structural trap but is too small (~1 Mt capacity) for commercial-scale CCS. PEV 2030 provides policy cover but no programs. Hydro-Québec's electricity advantage is a distinctive but unrealized enabling factor. Basin assignment confirmed.
