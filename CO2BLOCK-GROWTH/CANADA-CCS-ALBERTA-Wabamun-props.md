### Assessment for Wabamun Hub

- **Wabamun-specific**: No public thickness data from Enbridge's test wells. The two stratigraphic wells drilled (spring 2023 and winter 2023/24) have not had results published.
- **Regional estimate**: BSU thickness in the Wabamun area is expected to be **30–50 m** based on regional mapping from AGS and Weides et al. (2014). The formation thins toward the northeast (sub-Devonian unconformity) and thickens slightly to the southwest.
- **Recommended value (BSU)**: **~40 m** (regional average from multiple sources; note: this is a regional analog value)
- **Note on Nisku**: The Nisku Formation was the primary WASP target (Keith & Lavoie 2009) at depths of 1,550–2,200 m. It is a Devonian carbonate with reefal geometry. Thickness and properties are expected to be variable. The hub evaluation may target Nisku for the southern portion of the lease area.

| **~17%** (Quest analog, injection interval) | Rock et al., GHGT-13 (2017); Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) | BCS average porosity ~17% in the high-quality injection interval at Quest. **Quest analog — not applicable to Wabamun due to greater depth.** |

### Assessment for Wabamun Hub

- **Wabamun-specific**: No public porosity data from Enbridge's test wells. The BSU at Wabamun depths (2,300–3,300 m) is expected to have lower porosity than the same formation at Quest depth (~2,000 m) due to compaction with depth.
- **Regional estimate (BSU)**: **~10%** (Weides et al. 2014) to **~14%** (Alberta Innovates basal aquifer study). The Weides value (10%) is from core plug measurements in central Alberta at comparable depths. The Alberta Innovates value (14.4%) is from a broader regional assessment that includes shallower portions.
- **Depth correction**: Porosity in sandstones typically decreases exponentially with depth. At 2,300–3,300 m (Wabamun BSU) vs. ~2,000 m (Quest BCS), an additional ~5–10% porosity loss is expected from mechanical compaction. This supports using the lower end of regional estimates.
- **Recommended value (BSU)**: **~8–12%** (best estimate based on regional BSU data at comparable depths; 10% as the central value from Weides et al. 2014). **⚠ This is a regional analog estimate, not measured at the Wabamun site.**

### Basal Sandstone Unit (BSU)

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote / Notes |

| **<1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m² (<0.01 mD)** (avg, core plugs) | Weides et al. (2014), Can. J. Earth Sci. 51, p. 783 | *"The average porosity and permeability of the BSU is 10% and <1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m², respectively."* — Core plug measurements (probe permeametry, 96 samples). **⚠ Regional core-plug data, not Wabamun-specific.** |
| **1.6 mD** (regional-scale average) | Hofmann et al. (2013), Stanford Geothermal Workshop | *"Core test from the wells around Edmonton result in an average regional scale permeability of 1.6 mD."* — Scale-up from plug to regional scale. **⚠ Regional estimate.** |
| **20–500 mD** (Quest analog, expected range) | Shell Quest D-65 Application; ERCB Decision 2012 ABERCB 008 | *"expected range of... 20-500 mD for permeability"* — **Quest analog; Wabamun BSU at greater depth will likely have lower permeability.** |
| **150 mD** (Quest analog, well 8-19) | Shell Quest D-65 Application | Core analysis permeability from well 8-19 — **Quest analog only.** |
| **33–1,000 mD** (Quest analog, IEAGHG) | IEAGHG 2024-TR03, CO₂ Storage Site Catalogue | *"The BCS formation has an average porosity of 17% and a permeability range of 33 mD - 1,000 mD."* — **Quest analog only.** |
| **Fracture stimulation required** (BSU) | Hofmann et al. (2013); Weides et al. (2014) | Both studies conclude the BSU requires hydraulic stimulation for economic flow rates. Hofmann: *"This low expected permeability makes stimulation treatments imperative for efficient heat production."* |
| **Well-confined for stimulation** (BSU) | Hofmann et al. (2013) | *"The Basal Sandstone Unit is well confined and has the lowest initial permeability. Therefore, this formation is the best suited for permeability enhancements by conventional gel-proppant fracturing treatments."* — Favorable for CCS injection with stimulation. |

### Assessment for Wabamun Hub

- **Wabamun-specific**: No public permeability data from Enbridge's test wells. Permeability is the most uncertain parameter for the Wabamun BSU.
- **Core-plug scale**: The Weides et al. (2014) data (96 probe permeametry samples from 6 wells in central Alberta) indicates very low matrix permeability (<0.01 mD) at the plug scale for the BSU. However, the same study notes a "zone of high porosity and permeability" in one well, indicating heterogeneity.
- **Regional scale**: Hofmann et al. (2013) report 1.6 mD as the regional-scale average (accounting for fractures and larger-scale heterogeneity). This is still low relative to Quest's BCS (20–500 mD range).
- **Stimulation requirement**: Multiple sources agree the BSU requires stimulation (hydraulic fracturing) to achieve injectivity. The formation is well-confined by overlying shales and carbonates, making it suitable for gel-proppant fracturing.
- **Recommended value (BSU)**: **~0.01–1.6 mD** (plug to regional scale). Use **1 mD** as a representative pre-stimulation value for dynamic modeling. Post-stimulation permeability could be significantly higher. **⚠ All values are regional estimates, not Wabamun-specific measurements.**

## Notes on Data Quality

### 1. Project-Specific vs. Regional Data

**There is very limited public project-specific reservoir data for the Wabamun Hub.** Enbridge has drilled two (possibly three) stratigraphic test wells, but the results (porosity, permeability, thickness encountered, injectivity test results) have not been published. The project is still in the evaluation phase. Key data gaps include:

- No public core analysis or well-log porosity/permeability from the Wabamun test wells
- No public injectivity test results
- No public 3D seismic interpretation of the BSU in the specific lease area
- No public storage capacity estimate (P50/P90) for the Wabamun Hub

### 2. Regional BSU Data Applicability

The best available data for the BSU in central Alberta comes from:

- **Weides et al. (2014)** — This is the most authoritative published study of BSU properties. It is based on core analysis from 6 wells in central Alberta, but none are in the immediate Wabamun lease area. Porosity (avg 10%) and permeability (avg <0.01 mD core-plug scale) are based on 96 probe-permeametry samples and helium pycnometry. These values are appropriate for regional CCS resource assessment but may not capture local heterogeneity.

- **Hofmann et al. (2013)** — Regional-scale permeability of 1.6 mD after scale-up from plug to regional scale. This accounts for fractures and larger-scale heterogeneity not captured in core plugs.

- **Alberta Innovates Basal Aquifer study** — Gives a higher porosity (14.4%) for the basal aquifer system, but this is a broader regional average over a larger area (AB/SK/MB) and includes shallower, higher-porosity portions of the aquifer.

### 3. Quest Analog — Applicability and Limitations

The Shell Quest CCS project (operational since 2015) targets the Basal Cambrian Sands (BCS), which is the same formation as the BSU. However, there are critical differences:

| Parameter | Quest (BCS) | Wabamun (BSU) | Impact |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|--------|
| Depth | ~1,800–2,100 m | **2,300–3,300 m** | Deeper = lower porosity, lower permeability due to compaction |
| Location | Fort Saskatchewan area (NE of Edmonton) | **Wabamun area (W of Edmonton)** | Different diagenetic history; deeper basin position |
| Reservoir quality | Excellent (17% φ, 20–500 mD) | **Expected lower** | Direct application of Quest values would be **overly optimistic** |

**Quest data should NOT be used as a direct analog for Wabamun.** The BSU at Wabamun depths will have significantly lower porosity and permeability due to greater burial depth and associated compaction. If Quest values are used in screening calculations, they must be adjusted downward (recommend applying a porosity reduction factor based on depth-dependent compaction trends).

### 4. WASP Study Limitations

The Wabamun Area CO₂ Sequestration Project (WASP, ~2008–2012) was a University of Calgary-led study that examined large-scale CO₂ storage in the Wabamun area. Key limitations:
- WASP selected the **Nisku Formation** as its primary target, not the BSU
- The WASP study area (60×90 km) is much larger than the current Enbridge lease
- WASP was a feasibility study, not a site-specific characterization
- The BSU ("Basal Cambrian Sands") was identified as the deepest formation in the stratigraphic sequence but was not the focus of detailed characterization

### 6. Recommendations for CO2BLOCK Use

1. **For resource assessment**: Use regional BSU values (porosity 10%, thickness 40 m, areal extent ~2,200 km²) with appropriate storage efficiency factors. **Clearly label all values as regional estimates.**

2. **For dynamic modeling**: A range of BSU permeability from 0.01 mD (matrix) to 1.6 mD (regional effective) should be considered. Pre-stimulation injectivity is expected to be low; inclusion of stimulated (fractured) scenarios is warranted.

3. **Key uncertainty**: The two stratigraphic test wells drilled in 2023–2024 may eventually yield public data through AER well records. Revisit this file if AER cores logs or test results become available.

4. **Quest analog values should not be used** without applying a depth-dependent property degradation factor. If used for bounding calculations, note that Quest values represent the optimistic/upper bound and Wabamun BSU properties are likely significantly lower.

5. **The Nisku Formation** (secondary target, 1,550–2,200 m depth) may have better reservoir properties than the deeper BSU but was the focus of the earlier WASP and Project Pioneer studies. No public core data are available for the Nisku in the Wabamun lease area either.
