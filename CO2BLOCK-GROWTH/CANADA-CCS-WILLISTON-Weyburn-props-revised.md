## Permeability

| Flow Unit | Permeability Range | Typical Permeability | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Midale Marly** | 1–150 mD | 1–50 mD (matrix); low perm but high porosity | Isotropic pore network; dolomite-dominated; fractures enhance effective perm |
| **Midale Vuggy** | 0.3–500 mD | 10–300 mD (matrix); highly heterogeneous | Anisotropic, fracture-dependent; secondary vuggy porosity; strong permeability heterogeneity controlled by pore-length scales |

## Notes on Data Quality

**Overall quality: Excellent.** The Weyburn-Midale project is one of the most intensively studied and best-documented CCS sites in the world.

1. **Comprehensive peer-reviewed dataset.** Phase 1 (2000–2004) and Phase 2 (2005–2012) of the IEA GHG Weyburn-Midale CO₂ Monitoring and Storage Project produced arguably the most complete, peer-reviewed data set for CO₂ geological storage in association with EOR. Results published in a Special Issue of the *International Journal of Greenhouse Gas Control* (IJGGC Vol. 16, 2013) and a *Best Practices for Validating CO₂ Geological Storage* manual (2012).

2. **Extensive characterization.** The geological model was constructed from ~1,000 wells covering an area 10 km beyond injection limits. Core analysis, well logs, 2D/3D/4D seismic, hydrogeological mapping, geochemical sampling, and tracer studies all contributed.

3. **Porosity/permeability values are well-constrained** by extensive core data across both flow units. The ranges reported are consistent across multiple independent sources (GSA Today, Sask. Geol. Survey, USask MSc thesis, AAPG, RITE). The bimodal reservoir character (high-φ/low-k Marly vs. lower-φ/higher-k Vuggy) is thoroughly documented.

4. **Monitoring verification.** 4D seismic successfully tracked CO₂ plume migration. Geochemical and isotopic studies confirmed no injected CO₂ reached the surface (the well-known 2011 Kerr family allegation was determined by multiple independent studies to be naturally occurring biogenic CO₂, not the injected anthropogenic CO₂).

5. **BGS citation context.** The Riding & Rochelle (2005) British Geological Survey report cites Wegelin (1984) for a bulk average of 7 % porosity and 1 mD permeability. This value likely represents the Midale Marly M1 Unit / Midale Evaporite transition zone — a tighter, lower-porosity interval lithologically distinct from the main reservoir flow units (Marly and Vuggy) used for CO₂ injection. The BGS number should not be read as representative of the main injection intervals. All primary sources (White et al. 2004, Whittaker 2005, USask MSc 2008, RITE 2006) consistently report Marly 16–38 % and Vuggy 8–21 % porosity for the main reservoir units.

6. **Fracture gradient vs. injection pressure.** The original file erroneously labeled "~10–11 MPa" as the fracture gradient. This value is the *injection bottomhole pressure* (the pressure at which CO₂ is injected into the formation). The *fracture gradient* (pressure gradient at which the formation would hydraulically fracture) is higher, estimated at ~15.8–18.1 kPa/m (~0.7–0.8 psi/ft) for Mississippian carbonates in the Williston Basin, corresponding to a fracture initiation pressure of ~23–26 MPa at 1,450 m depth. Injection is maintained safely below this threshold. Exact site-specific min horizontal stress data from formation integrity tests were not found in the Tier 1–3 sources consulted.

7. **Cumulative injected vs. net stored.** The cumulative values reported (e.g., ~35+ Mt by 2023) represent *net storage* (purchased CO₂ that remains in the reservoir after accounting for produced CO₂ that is separated and recycled). Gross cumulative injection is significantly higher. At a typical recycle ratio for a mature CO₂-EOR flood, net retention is approximately 40–50 % of gross injection. Most public-facing summaries conflate "stored" and "injected" — the values here follow the convention of the IEA GHG research project, which reports net stored.

8. **Boundary Dam supplemental supply.** Some secondary sources (e.g., MIT CCS Database) mention "another up to 2000 t/d from Boundary Dam" as a supplemental CO₂ source. Boundary Dam Unit 3 CCS came online in 2014, but volumes actually delivered to Weyburn/Midale have been minor relative to the primary Dakota Gasification supply (which provides ~8,000 t/d). The MIT entry was last updated in 2016 and may overstate the Boundary Dam contribution. The primary CO₂ source for Weyburn-Midale has always been the Dakota Gasification plant.

9. **Caveats.** Some reservoir properties (especially fracture permeability and its anisotropy) are inherently uncertain despite extensive study. The porosity/permeability power-law relationship for the Vuggy unit (n≈8) indicates extreme heterogeneity sensitivity. The 5,000-year risk assessment is model-based and depends on assumptions about fault-seal integrity and geochemical reactions over geologic time. Most published numbers are from pre-2012 sources; post-2012 operational data are less accessible in the public domain (commercial sensitivity under Whitecap Resources/Cenovus).
