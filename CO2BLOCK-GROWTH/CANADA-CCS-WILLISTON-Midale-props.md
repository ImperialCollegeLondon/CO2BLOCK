## Reservoir Thickness

| Unit | Gross Thickness | Net Pay | Notes |
|------|----------------|---------|-------|
| **Midale Marly** | ~5–10 m | **~4 m** | "Average Net Pay (m): 4" per Cenovus 2018 presentation |
| **Midale Vuggy** | ~15–25 m | **~8 m** | "Average Net Pay (m): 8" per Cenovus 2018 presentation |
| **Total Midale Beds** | ~20–30 m | ~12 m | "Thin pay zone less than 30 metres thick" (Grokipedia) |
| **Midale Evaporite (seal)** | Variable, ~5–20 m | — | Anhydrite caprock; "primary sealing units" (ScienceDirect) |

> The reservoir is a thin carbonate platform sequence. The Marly unit is a dolostone; the Vuggy is a limestone. Both are separated and overlain by anhydrite evaporite seals.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Midale and Weyburn are adjacent fields targeting the same formation** — The Midale Beds are continuous across both fields. Many published studies treat "Weyburn-Midale" as a single project. Where Midale-specific data is unavailable, Weyburn data has been used and annotated accordingly.

2. **Cardinal Energy vs. Whitecap** — The Midale Unit was originally operated by Apache Canada, then acquired by Cardinal Energy (who operate it today as "Midale, SK"). Whitecap operates the adjacent Weyburn Unit (the larger of the two). The Midale Unit has 730 MMbbls OOIP vs. Weyburn's 1.4 billion bbls.

3. **Porosity-permeability ranges are field-wide** — The ranges reported (e.g., Marly porosity 16–38%) span the full heterogeneous carbonate platform. Local values at injection sites will vary. The Cenovus 2018 presentation provides the most reliable zone-by-zone breakdown.

4. **CO₂ storage figures are combined** — Most published totals (44 Mt, 30 Mt, etc.) combine injection across both Weyburn and Midale. Midale's contribution is proportionally smaller (~1,200–1,500 t/day vs. Weyburn's ~6,500 t/day), representing roughly 15–20% of total injection volume.

5. **Midale-specific reservoir data is limited in peer-reviewed literature** — Most published studies focus on Weyburn. The key Midale-specific operational data came from Cardinal Energy's corporate website and the MIT fact sheet. The Cenovus 2018 presentation covers the Weyburn Unit but the reservoir properties (Midale Beds) apply to both fields.

6. **Permeability values are stress-dependent** — The Cenovus presentation explicitly notes "Net values, stressed Perm" indicating these are not ambient-core measurements but values at reservoir stress conditions, which are typically lower.

7. **Historical operator transitions complicate data continuity** — The field has changed operators multiple times: Midale discovery by Shell (1953), Apache Canada (CO₂-EOR from 2005), Cardinal Energy (current). Weyburn: PanCanadian → EnCana → Cenovus → Whitecap (2017). Each transition may involve data loss or recategorization.

## Verbatim Quotes

> "The Weyburn and Midale oil fields were discovered in 1954 near Midale, Saskatchewan." — Wikipedia

> "Both Weyburn and the adjacent Midale oil field are highly suitable for CO2-EOR and it is expected that, combined, more than 40 MT CO2 will eventually be stored in these carbonate reservoirs." — OSTR NRCAN Canada

> "The pools are currently producing 176 120 m³ (28 000 barrels) of oil per day, of which 113 220 m³ (18 000 barrels) is incremental production as a result of the CO₂ injection. About 30 million tonnes of CO₂..." — Jensen, Saskatchewan Geological Survey (2015)

> "Our Midale operating area is located near Weyburn, Saskatchewan. It began producing in 1953 and is forecast to continue to produce for an additional 50+ years. It has over 730 million barrels original oil in place ('OOIP') and its low decline production of 3,300 boe/d (net) is supported by both waterflood and CO₂ enhanced oil recovery ('EOR'). Cardinal operates one of Canada's largest CO₂ sequestering facilities in Midale. In 2022, we sequestered 181,000 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent." — Cardinal Energy Ltd.

> "The primary reservoir being developed for CO₂ storage is the Midale formation, with secondary focus on the Frobisher. Whitecap has a 65.3% operated working interest in the Weyburn Unit which produces primarily light oil. The Weyburn property is one of the largest Carbon Capture & Utilization Storage 'CCUS' projects in the world. This internationally recognized, world class project has safely stored 44 million tonnes of purchased, third party CO₂ since 2000." — Whitecap Resources Inc.

> "Midale reservoir — Average Net Pay (m): Marly 4, Vuggy 8. Porosity (%): Marly 25, Vuggy 12. Permeability (mD): 6.0, 6.0. Net values, stressed Perm." — Cenovus Energy, Weyburn Unit Technical Presentation (2018)

> "Injection rates have reached approximately 6,500 tonnes per day at Weyburn and 1,500 tonnes per day at Midale, totaling about 3 million tonnes annually across both fields." — Grokipedia (citing IEA GHG data)

> "Porosity in the Marly unit ranges from 16% to 38% while permeability ranges from 1 mD to greater than 150 mD across the field. For the Vuggy unit, porosity ranges from 8% to 21% with permeability ranging from 0.3 mD to 500 mD." — Glemser (2008), University of Saskatchewan MSc Thesis

> "Producing Formation: Mississippian, Midale. Depth: 1,450 m. Discovered: 1954. Oil Quality: 25–35 °API. Area: 22,000 hectares (85 sq mi)." — Cenovus Energy (2018)

> "We have also recently started CO₂ injection at a pilot CO₂ flood into the Frobisher formation underlying the Weyburn Midale unit. We drilled two (2.0 net) producer wells and three (3.0 net) injection wells in 2023 and initiated CO₂ injection in late 2023. Early results are encouraging with a notable production response coming through approximately one month after injection, increasing oil rates on the two producer wells from approximately 40 bpd to over 200 bpd, per well." — Whitecap Resources Inc., 2023 Year-End Results (Feb 2024)
