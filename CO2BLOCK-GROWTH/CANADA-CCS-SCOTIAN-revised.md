## Capacity Uncertainty Note

The Scotian Basin capacity estimates span an extremely wide range (14 Gt – 1,280 Gt saline; P90 99 Gt – P10 1,472 Gt). This uncertainty is driven by several key parameters:

| Uncertainty Driver | Range / Detail |
|--------------------|----------------|
| **Storage efficiency factor** | 1–6% applied to saline aquifer pore volume (KUST Monte Carlo); base case of 3%. A 1% vs. 6% assumption produces a 6× swing in capacity. |
| **Pore volume assumptions** | Derived from 40 calibrated wells extrapolated across 340,675 km². Net-to-gross ratio, porosity distribution, and formation thickness assumptions vary significantly across sub-basins. |
| **Seal capacity** | Naskapi Shale / "O" Marker capillary entry pressure not fully characterized for CO₂ column heights. Fault seal integrity in salt-tectonized settings is a major uncertainty. |
| **Aquifer versus depleted field methodology** | Depleted fields use material balance (75% efficiency); aquifers use volumetric/stochastic (3% efficiency base). The mixed calculation approach contributes to the range. |
| **Dissolution and mineral trapping** | Not quantified in published estimates. Only structural and residual trapping considered in DOE-NETL methodology. |

**Implication**: The P50 value of 658 Gt (KUST) or median 177 Gt (NRCan/NS strategy) should be treated as order-of-magnitude. Site-specific characterization (3D seismic, appraisal wells, injection testing) would be required to narrow the range by 1–2 orders of magnitude before FID.

## Barriers to Development

| Category | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Regulatory** | No CCS-specific legislation (pore space tenure, long-term liability, MMV requirements); CNSOPB mandate = petroleum only |
| **Emission Sources** | NS emissions small (~14 Mt/yr — Environment Canada *National Inventory Report* 2024, Table A10-12: NS total GHG 13.8 Mt CO₂e in 2022). Major emitters in QC/ON (e.g., 800+ km to Montreal-Toronto industrial corridor) require ~1,000+ km pipeline (MCTL concept). See also NRCan *GHG Emissions by Province* (2024). |
| **Infrastructure** | No CO₂ pipelines; Maritimes & Northeast Pipeline (gas) would need repurposing / twinning. **Sable Offshore Energy decommissioning (2025+)** could provide both a CO₂ source (platform power generation) and depleted-field storage access, potentially reducing infrastructure requirements for a pilot-scale project. |
| **Economics** | No carbon hub anchor tenant; no federal/provincial CCS funding committed for NS; national competitive context (LNG Canada / Pacific Energy projects in BC competing for federal CCUS fund allocation) |
| **Seismic/Geomechanical** | Salt tectonics, overpressure (growth faults), fault reactivation risk — requires detailed site characterization |
| **Public Acceptance** | Offshore CCS novel in Atlantic Canada; First Nations consultation (Mi'kmaq) required |

## Conclusion

**Scotian Basin has ZERO CCS projects (Tier 1–2).** It has **extensive Tier 3–4 assessments** (NRCan strategy, OERA atlas, academic studies) demonstrating **world-class theoretical storage potential** (14 Gt – 1,280 Gt saline; 147–295 Mt depleted fields) in **Missisauga, Logan Canyon, Abenaki, Argo Salt** formations. Critical gaps: **no CCS regulatory regime**, **no pore space tenure**, **no anchor CO₂ source**, **no infrastructure**. All assessments confirm basin assignment.

Key uncertainties in capacity estimates (storage efficiency 1–6%, pore volume extrapolation, seal capacity) remain unaddressed by site-specific characterization. The Sable Offshore Energy decommissioning (2025+) may offer a near-term pilot opportunity but no CCS-specific plans have been announced.
