## Emissions Context

**Northwest Territories emissions**: ~2 Mt CO₂e/year (GNWT 2023 State of the Environment Report), dominated by diesel electricity generation in off-grid communities (+20 remote communities), diamond mines (Ekati, Diavik, Gahcho Kué), and natural gas production/processing in the Mackenzie Valley.

Relevance to CCS in this basin operates along three distinct vectors:

| Vector | Description | CCS Relevance |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Local NWT point sources** | Diesel gen, mine fleets, gas processing (Ikhil, Norman Wells) | Small scale, distributed; <0.1 Mt/yr per source — uneconomical for CCS without aggregation hubs |
| **Hypothetical Beaufort offshore oil/gas** | New offshore production (Amauligak, etc.) | Platform emissions 0.5–2 Mt/yr per facility; CCS listed as SEA mitigation option (Stantec/CIRNAC 2023) — **no proponent exists** |
| **Imported CO₂ transport** | Southern industrial CO₂ shipped/barged to depleted Mackenzie fields | Logistically extreme but consistent with CO₂ shipping corridors studied for Pacific/Arctic Canada (Clean Prosperity 2024); **no proposed route or project** |

**Bottom line**: No NWT-CO₂-source–storage match exists at commercial scale today. If CCS advances here, it would most likely serve imported CO₂ or a future offshore development — not current territorial emissions.

## Assessment Details

### 1. Mallik Gas Hydrate CO₂-CH₄ Exchange (GSC/JOGMEC, 1998–2015+)

| Attribute | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Proponents** | Geological Survey of Canada (GSC), Japan Oil, Gas and Metals National Corporation (JOGMEC), NRCan |
| **Location** | Mallik field, Mackenzie Delta, NWT (69.5°N, 134.5°W) |
| **Concept** | Inject CO₂ into methane hydrate-bearing sediments → CO₂ forms hydrate, releases CH₄ (guest exchange) |
| **Field Tests** | 2002: Thermal stimulation production test<br>2007/2008: Depressurization production tests (JOGMEC/NRCan/Aurora) |
| **CO₂ Injection** | Lab-scale and numerical modeling only; no field CO₂ injection conducted |
| **Post-2008 Activity** | GSC/JOGMEC laboratory studies (microscale CO₂-CH₄ exchange kinetics, pore-scale modeling) continued through at least 2015; no further field campaigns |
| **Global Context** | Mallik is one of the most intensively studied gas hydrate sites worldwide. The CO₂-CH₄ exchange concept was also field-tested at **Ignik Sikumi** (Alaska, 2012 — ConocoPhillips/USDOE, CO₂+N₂ injection into hydrate-bearing sandstone) and **Shenhu area** (South China Sea, 2017+ — GMGS production tests, pilot-scale). Mallik provided the foundational reservoir-scale understanding for all subsequent trials. |
| **Basin Verification** | Mallik = Mackenzie Delta = Beaufort-Mackenzie Basin (Evenick UBI 70) |
| **Status** | **Research pilot only** — no commercial CCS project; hydrate-CO₂ exchange remains experimental |
| **Citations** | Dallimore & Collett (2005) *Scientific Results Mallik 2002*; Kurihara et al. (2008) *JOGMEC Mallik 2007/2008*; GSC Open Files 220702, 210723; Boswell & Collett (2011) *Ignik Sikumi* Fire in the Ice; Li et al. (2018) *GMGS3-SH2* |

### 4. Permafrost/Sub-permafrost CO₂ Hydrate Storage

| Attribute | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Concept** | CO₂ injected into permafrost or sub-permafrost formations where pressure and temperature conditions intersect the CO₂ hydrate phase boundary. Below ~300–600 m (depending on geothermal gradient), temperatures in the Mackenzie Delta are cold enough that injected CO₂ spontaneously forms solid hydrate, providing a low-permeability trapping mechanism — analogous to the methane hydrate stability zone but with CO₂ as the guest molecule. |
| **Permafrost Thickness (Mackenzie Delta)** | 500–700 m onshore, thinning to 0 offshore; hydrate stability zone extends to ~1,100–1,500 m depth (Majorowicz & Osadetz 2003) |
| **Estimated Storage Capacity** | No authoritative pore-volume estimate exists specifically for CO₂ hydrate in the Mackenzie Delta permafrost. Analogous permafrost-hosted CO₂ hydrate storage estimates for the North Slope of Alaska range from **5 to 50 Gt CO₂** (in hydrate + dissolved phase in sub-permafrost aquifers — see House et al. 2003, GCCC Digital Series). A first-order scaling by area suggests a similar order of magnitude for Mackenzie Delta permafrost, but this is **highly speculative** pending dedicated characterization. |
| **Comparison — Alaska** | The USGS/NETL has studied CO₂ hydrate storage in the Alaskan North Slope permafrost as a dual-use concept: CH₄ production via CO₂-CH₄ exchange + permanent CO₂ storage as hydrate. The Ignik Sikumi trial (2012) demonstrated injectivity into hydrate-bearing sand. |
| **Comparison — Norway/Svalbard** | UNIS (University Centre in Svalbard) CO₂-LAB project conducted shallow CO₂ injection tests into permafrost/frozen sediments on Svalbard (2020–2023), showing CO₂ hydrate formation at <100 m depth. Directly analogous to Mackenzie Delta permafrost conditions. |
| **Key Technical Challenge** | Injectivity into hydrate-bearing sediments is extremely low (hydrate reduces permeability by 2–4 orders of magnitude). Feasible strategies include: (a) injection into sub-permafrost *unfrozen* aquifers where hydrate forms at the plume front; (b) thermal stimulation to create permeability, then CO₂ injection. |
| **Key Risk** | Permafrost degradation (thermokarst, talik formation) under a warming Arctic could destabilize shallow hydrate. Storage below ~800 m (within/above hydrate stability zone) would remain stable over century timescales even under moderate warming scenarios (RCP 4.5). |
| **Status** | **Conceptual / modeling only** — no field CO₂ injection test in Mackenzie Delta permafrost |
| **Key Citations** | Frontiers in Earth Science (2023) 1148765; House et al. (2003) *GCCC Permafrost CO₂ Storage*; Majorowicz & Osadetz (2003) *Geothermal Regime Mackenzie Delta*; UNIS CO₂-LAB reports (2020–2023); NRCan PERD Gas Hydrate Reports (multiple) |

### 5. Taglu Sequence — Depleted Field Storage Potential

| Attribute | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Stratigraphic Unit** | Taglu Sequence (Eocene), primary gas reservoir of the Mackenzie Delta |
| **Depleted Fields** | Taglu, Nightigak, Parsons Lake — discovered 1970s, tested but largely undeveloped due to Mackenzie Gas Project (MGP) cancellation (2017). Reservoir quality: deltaic sandstone, φ 15–25%, k 50–500 mD. |
| **CCS Potential** | If any of these fields were produced, depleted reservoirs would offer conventional structural/stratigraphic traps with proven seals (overpressured shales of the Richards Sequence). Storage capacity per field: order 10–100 Mt CO₂ (preliminary; no published storage assessment). |
| **Current Status** | Fields remain **undeveloped**. No production → no depletion → no ready pore space. Any CCS would require gas production first, or direct injection into saline aquifers within the same Taglu fairway. |
| **Citation** | Dixon et al. (1998) *GSC Taglu Sequence Reservoirs*; GSC Open File 4101; MGP Technical Reports (Imperial Oil 2004) |

## Regulatory Framework — NWT / Federal

| Instrument | Scope | Status |
|------------|-------|--------|
| **CER** (Canada Energy Regulator) | Offshore/onshore NWT wells, pipelines | *No CCS regulation*; *Oil and Gas Operations Act* |
| **CER Emerging CCS Framework** (2023–2024) | Nationwide | CER conducted public consultations (2023–2024) on developing CCS-specific regulations under the *CER Act*. A *Draft CCS Regulatory Framework* was published in 2024. Once finalized, this could provide a federal pathway for northern CCS projects — but no northern-specific provisions exist yet. |
| **GNWT** (Dept Infrastructure / ENR) | Onshore NWT | *No CCS Act*; *Petroleum Resources Act* |
| **Federal: Impact Assessment Act** | Major projects | Applies |
| **Inuvialuit Final Agreement / Land Claims** | Indigenous rights | Mandatory consultation |

**Critical Gap**: No CCS pore space tenure, no long-term liability regime, no CCS-specific regulator in NWT. The emerging CER framework may address federal offshore jurisdiction but does not yet cover onshore NWT pore space.

## Conclusion

**Beaufort-Mackenzie Basin has ZERO CCS projects.** Assessment activity limited to: (1) **Mallik gas hydrate research** (CO₂-CH₄ exchange concept, no field CO₂ injection), (2) **SEA hypothetical mitigation mention**, (3) **Academic/numerical modeling** of sub-permafrost CO₂ hydrate storage — including permafrost-specific thermodynamic studies with analogues in Alaska and Svalbard, (4) **Taglu Sequence** depleted-field potential (contingent on gas production first). No regulatory framework, no pore space tenure, no proponent. NWT emissions (~2 Mt/yr) are too small and distributed to justify local CCS without aggregation; imported CO₂ or offshore development would be the more likely drivers. Basin assignment confirmed.
