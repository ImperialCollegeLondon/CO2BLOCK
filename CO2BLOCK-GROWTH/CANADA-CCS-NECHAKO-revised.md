### Geological Risks by Sub-Area

The Geoscience BC assessment identified specific reservoir-quality risks that vary across the Nechako Basin's sub-areas:

**3 Areas NW/West of Williams Lake (Limited Potential):**
- **Area 1 (Chilcotin Plateau)**: Very low porosity (avg. <5%) in Cretaceous volcaniclastics; pervasive clay alteration reduces permeability to sub-millidarcy range; fault compartmentalization from Eocene extension creates risk of small, isolated trap volumes; overpressure absent — all zones exhibit hydrostatic to sub-hydrostatic pressure gradients
- **Area 2 (Nechako River valley)**: Jurassic-Cretaceous sandstones present but extensively zeolite-cemented (laumontite, heulandite) — secondary porosity occluded; permeability typically <0.1 mD; injection rates estimated at <10 kt/yr/well — economically unviable
- **Area 3 (Fraser Plateau)**: Triassic carbonates (Takla equivalent) are fractured but fracture networks are poorly connected; high vertical permeability risk means CO₂ plume migration cannot be contained within target horizon; no structural closure identified at depth

**5 Areas West/South of Quesnel (Conditional Consideration Only):**
- Potential existence of higher-quality Jurassic sandstones in deeper sub-basins but unsupported by well control
- 2D seismic coverage insufficient to confirm structural traps or seal continuity
- Any development would require 3–5 exploration wells and a 3D seismic program — costs estimated at >$50M before pilot injection

**Foresight Canada Key Findings** (2024):
- Total Nechako-adjacent emissions ~4–5 Mt/yr — insufficient to justify a dedicated CCS hub based on point-source capture alone

- No shared CO₂ trunk line exists within 400 km; standalone pipeline economics are prohibitive at <10 Mt/yr throughput

## Regulatory Framework — British Columbia

| Instrument | Scope | CCS Status |
|------------|-------|------------|
| **BCER** (British Columbia Energy Regulator) | Oil & gas wells, pipelines, CO₂ injection (acid gas) | **No dedicated CCS regulation**; acid gas disposal under *Oil and Gas Activities Act*. BCER's acid gas experience is exclusively in **NEBC (WCSB/Alberta Basin)**, not in the Nechako Basin. The BCER has **no regulatory experience with CCS-scale injection anywhere** — current injection rates are <0.2 Mt/yr/well vs. >1 Mt/yr/well required for commercial CCS. |
| **BC Gov't** (Ministry of Energy, Mines & Low Carbon Innovation) | CCS policy, hydrogen, CleanBC | **No CCS Act**. BC's *Carbon Capture and Storage Regulation* (under the *Oil and Gas Activities Act*, enacted 2013) governs acid gas disposal only — it does not address commercial-scale CCS pore space tenure, long-term liability transfer, or post-closure stewardship. |
| **Geoscience BC** | Public geoscience assessments | **No regulatory authority** — research only |
| **Federal** | *Impact Assessment Act* | Applies to major projects |

**Critical Gap**: No CCS pore space tenure regime; no long-term liability framework; BCER experience = acid gas disposal only (~2.7 Mt since 1996, all in NEBC within the WCSB). BC's *Carbon Capture and Storage Regulation* (BC Reg 147/2013) was designed for acid gas reinjection in the oil and gas sector and does not provide the legal framework for purpose-built CCS.

### CleanBC 0.6 Mt/yr Target — Where Will It Be Met?

BC's CleanBC CCUS target of 0.6 Mt/yr by 2030 cannot rely on the Nechako Basin given the negative assessment. The most likely storage pathways to meet this target are:

| Option | Feasibility | Risk |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **NEBC acid gas expansion** — inject additional CO₂ into existing Bluesky/Halfway disposal zones | Technically straightforward; infrastructure exists | Disposal zones in WCSB are already allocated; incremental capacity limited |
| **NEBC dedicated CCS** — new-build CCS at Dawson Creek/Chetwynd LNG or fertilizer plants | High capital cost; would require CCS Act amendments | Regulatory gap; pore space ownership unclear for new CCS |
| **Out-of-province storage** — Alberta (CCUS hubs, e.g., Alberta Carbon Trunk Line, Atlas) | Existing infrastructure; regulatory maturity | Political risk; BC capital leaving province |
| **Carbon mineralization** — ultramafic feedstock (e.g., CO2Lock, Mosaic Forest) | Permanence advantage; scalable in theory | Technology readiness low; no commercial deployment in BC |

**Current BC policy leans toward NEBC acid gas disposal expansion** as the most expedient route, with no public roadmap for Nechako or central BC storage.

## Carbon Mineralization Alternative — CO2Lock SAM Property

### In-Situ Carbon Mineralization Chemistry

The CO2Lock SAM property targets **in-situ carbon mineralization** in ultramafic rocks (peridotite, serpentinite) on the northeast flank of the Nechako Basin. The fundamental geochemical reaction is:

**Reaction**: Mg₂SiO₄ (olivine) + 2CO₂ → 2MgCO₃ (magnesite) + SiO₂ (silica)  
**ΔH**: Exothermic (~95 kJ/mol CO₂ at 50°C) — thermodynamically spontaneous  
**Conditions**: Temperature 50–150°C, PCO₂ 30–100 bar, with water as reaction medium  
**Rate**: In nature: 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ yr⁻¹ (too slow for CCS). CO2Lock's concept: inject CO₂-charged brine into fractured ultramafic rock to accelerate reaction 10³–10⁶× via fracture-surface contact.

### Comparison to Global Mineralization Projects

| Project | Location | Status | Method | Scale | Key Difference vs. CO2Lock |
|---------|----------|--------|--------|-------|---------------------------|
| **CarbFix** (Iceland) | Hellisheidi | Operational since 2014 | CO₂-charged water injection into basalt at ~500–800 m depth; >95% mineralization in <2 years | ~10 kt/yr | Reactive basalt; demonstrated at commercial pilot scale; geothermal infrastructure. CO2Lock is much earlier stage (no injection yet) |
| **Wallula Basalt Project** (USA) | Washington | Pilot completed 2013 | CO₂ injection into tholeiitic basalt; ~1 kt injected | ~1 kt | Demonstrated mineralization in basalt; project concluded. CO2Lock targets ultramafic rock (peridotite), not basalt |
| **OMB Project** (Oman) | Oman | Research-phase | Peridotite carbonation via subsurface CO₂ injection | Research only | Geological analogue to Nechako but no pilot injection. Oman's peridotite is more extensive and better characterized |
| **CO2Lock SAM** (BC) | Nechako NE flank | Pre-pilot concept | Fracture-hosted mineralization in serpentinized peridotite | Concept (<1 kt/yr) | Earliest stage; no injection yet; unique in targeting Canadian Cordilleran ultramafic bodies |

**Assessment**: Carbon mineralization in the Nechako region is at a **concept stage**, not a development project. The SAM property represents the earliest phase of exploration with no demonstrated injectivity, no confirmed mineralization rates, and no regulatory pathway. While ultramafic-hosted mineralization remains a long-term alternative for BC CCS, it is not a near-term (<2030) storage solution of any material scale.

## Conclusion

**Nechako Basin has ZERO CCS projects.** Geoscience BC Phase 1 assessment (2024) **concludes CO₂ storage is not technically recommended** due to low reservoir quality, injection rate challenges, and major uncertainties. No regulatory framework exists for commercial CCS in BC (BCER = acid gas only in NEBC; BC Reg 147/2013 does not cover commercial CCS). Alternative carbon mineralization (CO2Lock SAM) is at pre-pilot concept stage only — no injection tested, no commercial timeline. Basin assignment confirmed.

**The Nechako Basin is unique among all basins assessed: it is the only one where a formal, government-supported geoscience study definitively advises against pursuing CCS.** BC's CleanBC 0.6 Mt/yr target will almost certainly be met through NEBC acid gas expansion or out-of-province storage, not through any development in the Nechako Basin.
