**Report compiled**: 2026-07-23  
**Method**: Tier 1–7 audit of Canadian sedimentary basins against CO2BLOCK Global.xlsx CCS project entries, using Evenick (2021) basin polygons, primary regulatory sources (AER — Alberta; SME — Saskatchewan), open-government project registries, and peer-reviewed capacity assessments.

## Section 1: Basins Excluded from Global.xlsx

The following Canadian sedimentary basins are absent from Global.xlsx despite meeting inclusion criteria (hosting ≥1 operational/pilot/FID CCS project):

### 1.1 Alberta Basin (Evenick UBI 13)

**Status**: ABSENT from Global.xlsx  
**Host projects**: 8 CCS projects at Tiers 1–5 (operational, under construction, AER-approved, and proposed)  
**Reference**: `CANADA-CCS-ALBERTA-missing-revised.md`

The Alberta Carbon Trunk Line and Quest CCS alone had stored **>16 Mt CO₂ cumulatively by mid-2024** (`ALBERTA-revised.md:75`). The AER ST98 2025 (§S10) reports **9.8 Mt cumulative CCS sequestration through 2024** for Alberta (`ALBERTA-revised.md:309`). CER Market Snapshot 2025 lists both operational and in-development projects (`ALBERTA-revised.md:308`). The Alberta Major Projects Database records **8 hub-scale CCS initiatives** (`ALBERTA-revised.md:305`).

### 1.2 Williston Basin (Evenick UBI 76 — Canadian Extent)

**Status**: ABSENT from Global.xlsx  
**Host projects**: 5 CCS projects/pipeline infrastructure  
**Reference**: `CANADA-CCS-WILLISTON-missing-revised.md`

The Weyburn CO₂-EOR project alone has stored **~30 Mt CO₂ cumulatively as of 2024** (`WILLISTON-revised.md:82`). Boundary Dam BD3 has captured **>5 Mt through 2023** (`WILLISTON-revised.md:52`). Combined Canadian Williston storage: **>40 Mt**. The PCOR Partnership Atlas estimates **P50: 360 Mt storage capacity** in seven regional aquifers (`WILLISTON-revised.md:114`). CER Market Snapshot 2025 includes Weyburn, Midale, Aquistore, and BD3 (`WILLISTON-revised.md:198`).

### 1.3 Nechako Basin (Evenick UBI 437)

**Status**: ABSENT from Global.xlsx  
**Host projects**: Zero CCS projects  
**Reference**: `CANADA-CCS-NECHAKO-missing-revised.md`

The Nechako Basin is absent from Global.xlsx but with justification: a formal **Geoscience BC assessment concluded CCS is "not technically recommended"** for this basin (`NECHAKO-missing-revised.md`). The basin is included here as a negative finding — it meets the Evenick definition as a Canadian sedimentary basin, its omission is scientifically warranted, but the exclusion rationale should be documented.

### 1.4 Remaining Basins — Bulk Exclusion (34 Basins)

**Status**: 34 of 35 Canadian basins absent from Global.xlsx (97.1% gap)  
**Host projects**: Zero CCS projects across all 34 basins  
**Reference**: `CANADA-CCS-REMAINING_BASINS_ZERO-revised.md`

The `REMAINING_BASINS_ZERO-revised.md` file documents all 34 basins with zero CCS projects at any Tier 1–4 level. Each was searched against regulatory databases, government programs, GSC assessments, and peer-reviewed literature. None had operational, pilot, FID, or proposed CCS projects. Their exclusion from Global.xlsx is **factually correct** (no projects to map), but the **global gap of 97.1% of Canadian basins** should be noted in any completeness assessment of CO2BLOCK's Canadian coverage.

### 3.3 St. Lawrence Lowlands Clarification

The St. Lawrence Lowlands is **not a separate Evenick basin** — it is a physiographic subregion of the Appalachian Basin (UBI 39). It was analyzed as a separate entity in the original workplan because it contains INRS/Québec formation-level CCS feasibility assessments (`CANADA-CCS-ST_LAWRENCE_LOWLANDS-revised.md`). In the Evenick basin framework, it is correctly subsumed under Appalachian (UBI 39). The file documents zero CCS projects for this subregion.
