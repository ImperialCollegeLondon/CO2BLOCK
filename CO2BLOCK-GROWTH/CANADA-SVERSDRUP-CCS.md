# CCS Projects in the Sverdrup Basin (Canada's Largest Basin)

**Status: NONE IDENTIFIED — Zero operational, under-construction, or formally proposed CCS projects exist in the Sverdrup Basin as of 2025.**

---

## Executive Summary

After comprehensive web searches across government databases, industry reports, academic literature, and regulatory filings, **no Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) projects were found in the Sverdrup Basin**. All of Canada's CCS activity is concentrated in the **Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin (WCSB)** — specifically Alberta, Saskatchewan, and British Columbia — over 2,000 km south of the Sverdrup Basin.

### Key Negative Findings (Evidence of Absence)

1. **CAPP 2025 CCUS in Canada Report** — Lists 8 operational, 4 under construction, 9+ planned projects. **All in Alberta/Saskatchewan**. Storage formations: Basal Cambrian Sands, Leduc, Nisku, Wabamun — WCSB units, not Sverdrup.

2. **CER Market Snapshot 2025** — "11 CO₂ storage facilities under development in western Canada." Jurisdictional regulatory frameworks cited: AB, SK, BC, MB. **No mention of Northwest Territories, Nunavut, or Arctic offshore.**

3. **NRCan Clean Energy Fund / CCS Fund Records** — 4 projects funded 2009: Quest, ACTL, Project Pioneer (cancelled), Swan Hills (cancelled). **All Alberta.**

4. **Geological Survey of Canada Open Files** — Multiple CO₂ storage atlases for: Northeast BC (Granite Wash, Montney), Alberta (Basal Cambrian, Leduc, Nisku), Atlantic Canada (Carboniferous sandstones), Great Lakes Basin. **No Open File for Sverdrup Basin or Arctic Archipelago CO₂ storage.**

5. **USGS Professional Paper 1824-I (2020)** — Comprehensive petroleum resource assessment of Sverdrup Basin. **No CO₂ storage discussion.**

6. **Panarctic Oils Exploration (1969-1985)** — 120 wells, 15 gas fields, 1 oil field discovered. **No CO₂ injection/EOR ever conducted.** Infrastructure abandoned.

## Why No CCS Projects Exist in Sverdrup Basin

| Barrier | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **No CO₂ Emission Sources** | Zero industrial emitters in Queen Elizabeth Islands. Nearest large sources: Alberta oil sands (~2,500 km south). |
| **No Infrastructure** | No pipelines, roads, power lines, ports suitable for CO₂ transport. 1970s exploration infrastructure long abandoned. |
| **No Regulatory Framework** | CCS regulation is provincial (AB, SK, BC). Federal jurisdiction in Arctic offshore is nascent; no CCS-specific regime exists. |
| **No Pore Space Tenure System** | Alberta's *Mines and Minerals Act* pore space tenure (used by Quest, ACTL) does not extend to federal Arctic lands. |
| **Extreme Logistics/Cost** | Arctic drilling: $100M+/well vs $5-10M in WCSB. Seasonal access only. No supply chain. |
| **Permafrost / Gas Hydrate Risk** | Shallow permafrost complicates well integrity; gas hydrates in shallow section create geohazards for injection. |
| **Trap Integrity Concerns** | Eurekan Orogeny (Eocene) deformed eastern basin; faulting/fracturing may compromise seal integrity for CO₂. |
| **Indigenous Rights / Co-Management** | Inuit-owned lands (Nunavut Agreement); any project requires NIRB review, IIBA negotiation. No precedent for CCS. |

## Geological Storage Potential (Theoretical Only)

While no projects exist, the Sverdrup Basin *geologically* possesses some storage-relevant characteristics:

| Attribute | Sverdrup Basin Status | Relevance to CO₂ Storage |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Deep Saline Aquifers** | Triassic-Jurassic sandstones (Isachsen, Heiberg, Awingak Fms) at 2-4 km depth in western basin | Potential reservoirs; porosity up to 15-20% at depth (Chen et al. 2000) |
| **Depleted Gas Fields** | 15 discovered fields (Drake, Hecla, Whitefish, etc.) — **never produced** | Zero depletion; no wellbore infrastructure; fields are "stranded" |
| **Seals** | Thick Triassic shales (Bathurst Island Group), Cretaceous shales; Otto Fiord evaporites (mobile salt) | Good regional seals; **salt mobility creates leakage risk** along diapirs |
| **Structural Traps** | Eurekan anticlines (salt-cored) | Proven hydrocarbon traps; **but Eurekan deformation may have fractured seals** |
| **Storage Capacity Estimates** | **None published** for CO₂. USGS 2008 assessed hydrocarbons only. | No volumetric CO₂ storage resource estimate exists (SRMS or DOE methodology). |

## Conclusion

**The Sverdrup Basin has zero CCS projects and zero prospective CCS projects.** Any claim otherwise confuses:
- **Basin identity** (Sverdrup ≠ Beaufort-Mackenzie ≠ WCSB)
- **Jurisdiction** (Federal Arctic ≠ Provincial Western Canada)
- **Project status** (Hypothetical mitigation mention ≠ Project)

**For CO₂ storage screening purposes:** The Sverdrup Basin should be classified as **"No Activity / Not Assessed / Not Viable in Foreseeable Future"** due to absence of emission sources, infrastructure, regulation, and economic rationale — not due to geological unsuitability alone.
