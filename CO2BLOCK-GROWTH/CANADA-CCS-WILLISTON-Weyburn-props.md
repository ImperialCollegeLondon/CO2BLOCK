## Reservoir Thickness

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Formation** | Mississippian Charles Formation, Midale Beds (oolitic carbonate) |
| **Reservoir architecture** | Two stacked flow units: upper **Midale Marly** (dolostone) and lower **Midale Vuggy** (limestone) |
| **Marly unit thickness** | Average ~6 m (range 0–10 m) |
| **Vuggy unit thickness** | Average ~15–17 m (range 0–20 m) |

## Porosity

| Flow Unit | Porosity Range | Average Porosity | Pore Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Midale Marly** (dolostone) | 16–38 % | ~25–26 % | Intercrystalline micropores (~4 μm diameter), moderate connectivity, isotropic |
| **Midale Vuggy** (limestone) | 8–21 % | ~12 % | Secondary vuggy + intergranular, heterogeneous pore-size distribution |

> *"The Midale Marly has relatively high porosity (16%–38%) and low permeability (1 to >50 millidarcy), whereas the Midale Vuggy has relatively lower porosity (8%–20%) and higher permeability (10 to >300 millidarcy)."* — White et al., GSA Today 14(7) (2004)

## Permeability

| Flow Unit | Permeability Range | Typical Permeability | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Midale Marly** | 1–150 mD | 1–50 mD (matrix); low perm but high porosity | Isotropic pore network; dolomite-dominated; fractures enhance effective perm |

## Notes on Data Quality

**Overall quality: Excellent.** The Weyburn-Midale project is one of the most intensively studied and best-documented CCS sites in the world.

1. **Comprehensive peer-reviewed dataset.** Phase 1 (2000–2004) and Phase 2 (2005–2012) of the IEA GHG Weyburn-Midale CO₂ Monitoring and Storage Project produced arguably the most complete, peer-reviewed data set for CO₂ geological storage in association with EOR. Results published in a Special Issue of the *International Journal of Greenhouse Gas Control* (IJGGC Vol. 16, 2013) and a *Best Practices for Validating CO₂ Geological Storage* manual (2012).

2. **Extensive characterization.** The geological model was constructed from ~1,000 wells covering an area 10 km beyond injection limits. Core analysis, well logs, 2D/3D/4D seismic, hydrogeological mapping, geochemical sampling, and tracer studies all contributed.

3. **Porosity/permeability values are well-constrained** by extensive core data across both flow units. The ranges reported are consistent across multiple independent sources (GSA Today, Sask. Geol. Survey, USask MSc thesis, AAPG, BGS). The bimodal reservoir character (high-φ/low-k Marly vs. lower-φ/higher-k Vuggy) is thoroughly documented.

4. **Monitoring verification.** 4D seismic successfully tracked CO₂ plume migration. Geochemical and isotopic studies confirmed no injected CO₂ reached the surface (the well-known 2011 Kerr family allegation was determined by multiple independent studies to be naturally occurring biogenic CO₂, not the injected anthropogenic CO₂).

5. **Caveats.** Some reservoir properties (especially fracture permeability and its anisotropy) are inherently uncertain despite extensive study. The porosity/permeability power-law relationship for the Vuggy unit (n≈8) indicates extreme heterogeneity sensitivity. The 5,000-year risk assessment is model-based and depends on assumptions about fault-seal integrity and geochemical reactions over geologic time. Most published numbers are from pre-2012 sources; post-2012 operational data are less accessible in the public domain (commercial sensitivity under Whitecap Resources/Cenovus).
