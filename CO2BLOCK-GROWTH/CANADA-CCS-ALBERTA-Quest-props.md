## Reservoir Thickness

### Primary Values

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **40 m** | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) | *"The BCS is a 40m thick reservoir with the highest quality sands in the bottom 30m perforated for injection."* |
| **40–48 m (mean 44 m)** | Shell, Gen-4 Report (2011), Table 40 | *"BCS... 40m to 48m with a mean of 44m"* |
| **~45 m** | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) — citing BCS | *"The formation has an average thickness of approximately 45 m"* |
| **40–80 m** | AGS PRS 2024-001 (Herbers et al.) | *"The Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS), 40-80 m thick at burial depths of 1000-3600 m"* |
| **38 m** (net sand) | Rock & O'Brien, AAPG Search & Discovery #80577 (2017) | Slide shows *"Net Sand - 38m"* for Radway 8-19 well |
| **45.7 m** (150 ft) | carbonstorage.io | *"Gross thickness (ft): 150"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- The GHGT-15 value (40 m) is the thickness of the injection interval as perforated (bottom 30 m of the 40 m are the high-quality zone).
- The Gen-4 model (Shell's own reservoir model) gives a range of 40–48 m with mean 44 m across the full Area of Interest (~180 × 190 km).
- The AGS survey gives a broader regional range (40–80 m) as the BCS formation thins toward the northeast (where it pinches out at the Sub-Cretaceous unconformity) and thickens toward the southwest.
- **Recommended value**: **40–45 m** (use 40 m as a conservative estimate, 44 m as the Gen-4 mean, 45 m as the published average).

## Porosity

### Primary Values

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **~17%** | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) | *"The high-quality injection interval has an average of ~17% porosity... across the Storage Complex."* |
| **~17%** | Rock et al., GHGT-13 (2017), Energy Procedia | *"...the storage reservoir, with average porosity of about 17%..."* (paraphrased from full text) |
| **~17%** | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) | *"porosity of about 17%"* |
| **17%** | Rock & O'Brien, AAPG Search & Discovery #80577 (2017) | Slide shows *"Porosity – 17 %"* for Radway 8-19 well |
| **14%** (mid-case) | Shell, Gen-4 Report (2011) | *"Mid Case average 14%"* (full BCS zone, including lower-quality intervals) |
| **0.16** (16%) | Shell, D-65 Application — Well 8-19 actual | Porosity log analysis value |
| **0.11–0.19** | Shell, D-65 Application — all wells | Range across injection and monitoring wells |
| **16%** | carbonstorage.io | *"Porosity: 16%"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- Multiple independent sources (GHGT-15, GHGT-13, IJGGG 2022, AAPG presentation) all converge on **~17%** for the high-quality injection interval.
- The Gen-4 mid-case (14%) is lower because it averages over the full BCS zone including lower-porosity sections — the net-to-gross of 0.9 means the sand-prone intervals have higher porosity.
- **Recommended value**: **17%** (for the injection interval specifically; 14% for full-formation average).

## Permeability

### Primary Values

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **~1000 mD** | Shell & ESG, GHGT-15 (2021) | *"The high-quality injection interval has an average of... ~1000mD permeability across the Storage Complex."* |
| **Darcy-range** | Rock et al., GHGT-13 (2017), Energy Procedia | Describes BCS permeability in the darcy range |
| **~1000 md** | Rock et al., IJGGG (2022) | *"average permeability close to 1000 md"* |
| **150 mD** | Shell, D-65 Application — Well 8-19 actual | Core analysis permeability |
| **20–500 mD** | Shell, D-65 Application — Injection Wells 2-10, 3-4, 5-35, 7-11 | Range across injection wells |
| **150 mD** | carbonstorage.io | *"Permeability (md): 150"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- The high **~1000 mD / darcy-range** values represent the best-quality injection interval, consistent with the high-quality basal sands.
- The lower **20–500 mD** values from D-65 represent well-test or core-plug measurements that sample smaller volumes and may include lower-quality intervals or near-wellbore effects.
- The permeability anisotropy ratio Kv/Kh of ~0.01–0.1 (from GHGT-15) means CO₂ flows preferentially horizontally.
- **Recommended value**: **~1000 mD** (formation-scale for high-quality interval); note that well-scale measurements range 20–500 mD.

## Area (Sequestration Lease Area — SLA)

### Primary Values

| Value | Source | Verbatim Quote |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **39 townships + 12 sections (~3,667 km²)** | AER, Quest Closure Plan (2023) | *"The CO₂ Sequestration Lease Area (SLA) granted by the Carbon Sequestration Leases is defined as the full extent of 39 townships plus 12 sections."* (Table 2-1 lists Townships 56–63, Ranges 18–24 W4M) |
| **~3,700 km²** | Lahvis et al., Energy Procedia (2017) | *"The Quest sequestration lease area (SLA), which covers an area of about 3700 km² (red outline, Fig. 1)..."* |
| **164,639 acres (~666 km²)** | carbonstorage.io | *"Leased acres: 164,639"* |

### Confirming Evidence

- The AER Closure Plan is the primary regulatory document. The townships listed (Table 2-1) cover Townships 56–63, Ranges 18–24 W4M:
  - 39 townships × 36 sections/township = 1,404 sections
  - 1,404 + 12 additional sections = 1,416 sections
  - 1,416 sections × 640 acres/section = 906,240 acres
  - 906,240 acres × 0.00404686 km²/acre = **~3,667 km²**
- The Lahvis et al. (2017) value (~3,700 km²) rounds to the same number and is from a peer-reviewed Energy Procedia paper.
- The carbonstorage.io value (164,639 acres = 666 km²) is a significantly smaller area — this may represent an initial lease block or a subset, but the full SLA as per the AER is ~3,667 km².
- Note: The "Area of Review" (AOR) for microseismic monitoring is defined as 10 km radially from each injection well, which is a different (smaller) operational boundary.
- **Recommended value**: **~3,700 km²** (3,667 km² precisely from the AER townships). The SLA is the legally defined storage area.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Best primary sources**: The GHGT-15 presentation (Shell/ESG 2021) and the GHGT-13 paper (Rock et al. 2017, Energy Procedia) are the most citable peer-reviewed sources for BCS properties at Quest. Both are by Shell authors with direct access to well data.

2. **Gen-4 Report**: The Shell Quest Generation-4 Integrated Reservoir Modeling Report (2011) is a formal regulatory filing on Open Alberta and is the most detailed single source for BCS properties across the full model area. It gives both the mid-case (14% porosity) and the understanding that the injection interval has higher quality (the other primary sources report ~17% for the perforated interval specifically).

3. **D-65 Application**: The regulatory application (Directive 65) contains actual well-test and core-analysis data. Well 8-19 (the deep monitoring well, cored) shows porosity 0.16 (16%) and permeability 150 mD — these are point measurements whereas the formation-scale values (17%, ~1000 mD) are averages across the Storage Complex.

4. **Area**: The AER Closure Plan (2023) is the authoritative regulatory source for the SLA extent. The carbonstorage.io value of 164,639 acres (~666 km²) is unexplained and conflicts with the AER-definined SLA; the AER value should take precedence.

5. **All values are for the Quest-specific location** within the regional BCS aquifer. BCS properties vary significantly across Alberta (the AGS survey notes the formation spans 1,000–3,600 m burial depth with correspondingly variable properties). These values should not be applied to other CCS projects targeting the BCS without site-specific verification.
