# Aquistore CCS Project — Reservoir Properties (Deadwood Formation / Black Island Member)

**Last updated**: July 23, 2026  
**Project**: Aquistore (PTRC — Petroleum Technology Research Centre)  
**Status**: Operational Research/Demo (Injection began April 2015; ongoing)  
**Reservoir**: Deadwood Formation / Black Island Member (Cambro-Ordovician saline aquifer)  
**Location**: ~10 km outside Estevan, southern Saskatchewan (near SaskPower Boundary Dam)  
**Basin**: Williston Basin (Intracratonic, Onshore)  
**Storage Type**: Deep saline aquifer (NOT EOR)  
**Operator/Manager**: Petroleum Technology Research Centre (PTRC)  
**Well Pair**: 1 injection well (PTRC INJ 5-6-2-8) + 1 observation well (PTRC OBS 5-6-2-8), ~150–152 m apart  
**Source CO₂**: SaskPower Boundary Dam Carbon Capture Facility (coal-fired post-combustion capture)

## Sources Summary

Aquistore is a "slipstream" CO₂ storage project integrated with SaskPower's commercial Boundary Dam CCS facility. The majority of captured CO₂ is sold to Whitecap Resources for EOR at Weyburn; excess CO₂ is sent to Aquistore for saline storage. The project is the most comprehensively monitored CCS site globally, with extensive public-domain characterisation data.

### ⚠️ Important: Spatial Context for Hersi & Iqbal (2025) Porosity Values
The 21% quartz arenite and 8.7% graywacke porosity values are **regional facies averages** derived from petrographic point-counting of the Black Island Member across a 450×500 km area in southeastern Saskatchewan (Hersi & Iqbal 2025). These are **not** site-specific measurements at the Aquistore injection well.

At the Aquistore site, the Black Island Member lies at ~3,130 m depth. The 21% value likely represents the **least-compacted, shallowest-buried portions** of the basin (near the northern subcrop edge). At the ~3.2 km depth of the Aquistore injection well, the Black Island Member porosity is expected to be substantially lower, consistent with:
- Aquistore-specific Deadwood core plug averages of **6%**
- Log-based Deadwood porosity of **7–11%**
- The Husky history-matched model porosity range of **0.2–10.5%** (Hau et al. 2024)

The Menger (2021) GeoConvention abstract also reports quartz arenite porosity of 22–29% from image segmentation — these similarly represent shallower-buried samples.

**Conclusion**: The Hersi & Iqbal values are **valid regional end-members** for the Black Island Member facies but should **not** be applied as representative of the Aquistore injection site. At the Aquistore well location, the recommended porosity range is **6–11%** (site-specific core and log data).

### Confirming Evidence
- Cross-validation: Core plug 6% (Zambrano-Narvaez 2015) and log-based 7% (Roach 2017) agree well and represent the site. Regional values (10–15%, 21%) are 1.5–3.5× higher and reflect less-buried portions of the basin.
- **Recommended value (site-specific)**: 6–11%, with 7% as the best estimate for the injection interval.

### Note on kh→k Conversion
The effective permeability estimate of ~70 mD uses h≈130 m (approximate total net contributing thickness, between the ~112 m net pay and ~88 m perforated interval). If h≈102 m (net pay = 219 m × 51% NTG) is used, k≈88 mD. If h≈130 m (closer to the gross injection zone thickness), k≈69 mD. Both are consistent with the operational injectivity observations and are significantly higher than the core plug average of 5 mD — confirming that effective reservoir permeability is dominated by high-permeability streaks not captured in core plug sampling.

### Confirming Evidence
- Cross-validation: Core plug 5 mD is a point measurement; the effective reservoir permeability of ~70–88 mD from well-test kh is 14–18× higher. This spread is acknowledged and consistent with the presence of thin high-permeability streaks (e.g., the ~10 m upper Deadwood zone hosting the primary CO₂ plume).
- **Recommended value**: 5 mD (core plugs at lab scale); ~70–88 mD (effective reservoir-scale). For reservoir simulation use effective values.

### Key Distinctions: Model Area vs. Plume Area vs. Regional Resource

| Concept | Area | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| **Simulation model / storage capacity area** | ~34 km² | The domain over which static storage capacity (P10–P90: 8.4–27.1 Mt) was calculated. This is a modeler's boundary co-extensive with the 3D seismic survey plus buffer, **not** a plume footprint or regulatory lease area. |
| **Demonstrated plume area (by 2017, after ~36–107 kt)** | ~0.03 km² (200 m wide seismic anomaly) | The 4D seismic anomaly in the upper Deadwood (Roach et al. 2017). Significantly smaller than the model area. |
| **Forecast plume area (50 yr, 200+ kt additional)** | ~1.5 km² (1.4 km diameter) | From Dalkhaa et al. (2017) predictive simulations. Still only ~4–5% of the 34 km² model area. |
| **Regional formation extent** | ~225,000 km² (450×500 km) | The entire Deadwood/Black Island aquifer across SE Saskatchewan. The "gigatonne-scale" and 974 km³ pore space are regional resource estimates, **not** site-specific capacity. |

**Recommended area for CO2BLOCK screening database**: Use either:
- **34 km²** for static capacity estimation (consistent with Peck/Liu methodology), with the caveat that this is the model boundary area, not a demonstrated plume footprint.
- **Regional extent** (450×500 km) only for basin-scale resource assessments, not project-specific screening.

### ⚠️ Temperature Note: CO₂ Density Discrepancy
The Roach et al. (2017) CO₂ density value of ~800 kg/m³ was calculated at **T=110°C and P=39 MPa**, citing log-based porosity work in Roach et al. (2015a). The far-field reservoir temperature at Aquistore is **119–120°C** (Zambrano-Narvaez 2015) or **112.8°C at 3,173 m** (Hau et al. 2024).

- At **P=39 MPa, T=110°C**: ρCO₂ ≈ 790–830 kg/m³ (as used in Roach 2017)
- At **P=39 MPa, T=119–120°C**: ρCO₂ ≈ 700–760 kg/m³
- The 110°C value may reflect the **near-wellbore mixed temperature** in the injection zone (upper Deadwood, where cold ~70°C CO₂ mixes with ~120°C formation brine). Roach et al. (2017) did not explicitly state why 110°C was chosen over 119°C.

**Impact**: Using 800 kg/m³ instead of ~730 kg/m³ overestimates the CO₂ mass in volumetric calculations by ~7–11%. The 800 kg/m³ value is retained in this document because it is the published figure used in the 4D seismic interpretation, but users should apply 700–760 kg/m³ for reserve-grade storage capacity calculations.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Extensively characterised**: Aquistore is among the most comprehensively characterised and monitored CCS sites globally. Data quality is high, with multiple converging lines of evidence (3D/4D seismic, wireline logs, core analysis, downhole P/T gauges, DTS, DAS, RST/PNL saturation logs, groundwater monitoring, InSAR, microseismic arrays).

2. **Core data vs. well-test data**: Core plug measurements (6% porosity, 5 mD permeability) likely reflect sampling bias toward competent intervals and probably underrepresent the effective reservoir-scale properties. Well-test derived kh (~9,000 mD·m) and injectivity indices imply significantly higher effective permeability at the reservoir scale (~70–88 mD). The discrepancy is consistent with the presence of thin high-permeability streaks (e.g., the ~10 m upper Deadwood zone that hosts the primary CO₂ plume) that dominate flow but are underrepresented in core plugs.

3. **Regional vs. site-specific — critical distinction**: Regional literature values (e.g., 10–15% porosity, 300 mD permeability for the Deadwood Aquifer, or the 21% quartz arenite porosity from Hersi & Iqbal 2025) differ substantially from Aquistore-specific core and log data (6–7% porosity, 5 mD core permeability). This reflects:
   - Facies variations across the 450×500 km basin
   - The northern subcrop edge having shallower burial and less compaction
   - Aquistore being located near the deeper southern portion of the basin
   - **All Hersi & Iqbal (2025) values in this document are flagged as [REGIONAL] and should not be applied without spatial correction**

4. **CO₂ density temperature sensitivity**: The density of CO₂ at Aquistore reservoir conditions depends critically on the temperature used. Roach et al. (2017) used T=110°C (ρ≈800 kg/m³), while far-field reservoir temperature is 119–120°C (ρ≈700–760 kg/m³). This introduces ~7–11% uncertainty in mass calculations from seismic or volumetric methods. Users should be explicit about which temperature is used and why.

5. **Perforation plugging**: Perforations 3 (and part of Perf 4) were plugged shortly after injection start and before the April 2015 spinner survey, so only ~55–60% of the completed interval was effectively contributing to injection. This is a significant operational observation relevant to completion design.

6. **Non-isothermal effects**: CO₂ is injected at ~70°C into a ~120°C reservoir, creating substantial thermal perturbations. This affects relative permeability, stress state, and injectivity evolution. Thermally-induced microcracking has been documented and appears to improve injectivity over time.

7. **Salt precipitation**: Stop-start injection has caused halite precipitation in lower-permeability zones near the wellbore, affecting injectivity. This is a key lesson for commercial operations with variable CO₂ supply.

8. **Data accessibility**: Comprehensive datasets (3D/3C seismic, time-lapse PNL/sonic logs, injection volumes, reservoir P/T, core, and simulation models) are available through PTRC's CCUS Data Network for investing partners.

9. **Resolution of 4D seismic**: The seismic data achieved a global NRMS of ~10%, allowing detection of small CO₂ quantities at 3.2 km depth. However, the plume interpretation is limited by seismic wavelength (~100 m), meaning thin or distal plume parts may be undetected. The seismic anomaly in the upper Deadwood is consistent with either a 2 m thick high-saturation (>50%) zone or a 4 m thick low-saturation (<10%) zone.

10. **Icebox caprock thickness uncertainty**: Reported values range from ~15 m (local, at injection well — Roach 2017) to ~30 m (regional — Zambrano-Narvaez 2015; Hau 2024). Fifteen metres of shale is likely adequate caprock at 3.1 km depth with low natural overpressure, but the 2× range should be noted.

11. **Correlativity to the Basal Cambrian Sands (Alberta)**: The Deadwood Formation is the same regional Cambro-Ordovician saline aquifer as the Basal Cambrian Sands (BCS) targeted in Alberta (Quest, Atlas). However, Aquistore's reservoir has significantly different properties:
    - **Total interval**: Aquistore ~200 m vs. BCS ~40 m at Quest
    - **Net pay**: Aquistore ~112 m (51% NTG) vs. Quest BCS ~36 m (>90% NTG)
    - **Porosity**: Aquistore ~6–7% vs. Quest BCS ~17% — reflecting greater burial depth at Aquistore (~3.2 km vs. ~1.5–2.0 km for Quest)

**Verbatim**: *"The Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS) in Alberta is a widespread coarse-grained sandstone, which is like other target storage formations encountered further south; notably at Aquistore and at Decatur."* — IEAGHG 2024 Storage Site Catalogue
