# Aquistore CCS Project — Reservoir Properties

**Project**: Aquistore (PTRC — Petroleum Technology Research Centre)
**Status**: Operational Research/Demo (Injection began April 2015; ongoing)
**Storage Formation**: Deadwood Formation / Black Island Member (Cambro-Ordovician saline aquifer)
**Location**: ~10 km outside Estevan, southern Saskatchewan (near SaskPower Boundary Dam)
**Basin**: Williston Basin (Intracratonic, Onshore)
**Storage Type**: Deep saline aquifer (NOT EOR)
**Operator/Manager**: Petroleum Technology Research Centre (PTRC)
**Well Pair**: 1 injection well (PTRC INJ 5-6-2-8) + 1 observation well (PTRC OBS 5-6-2-8), ~150–152 m apart
**Source CO₂**: SaskPower Boundary Dam Carbon Capture Facility (coal-fired post-combustion capture)

---

## Sources Summary

Aquistore is a "slipstream" CO₂ storage project integrated with SaskPower's commercial Boundary Dam CCS facility. The majority of captured CO₂ is sold to Whitecap Resources for EOR at Weyburn; excess CO₂ is sent to Aquistore for saline storage. The project is the most comprehensively monitored CCS site globally, with extensive public-domain characterisation data.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Extensively characterised**: Aquistore is among the most comprehensively characterised and monitored CCS sites globally. Data quality is high, with multiple converging lines of evidence (3D/4D seismic, wireline logs, core analysis, downhole P/T gauges, DTS, DAS, RST/PNL saturation logs, groundwater monitoring, InSAR, microseismic arrays).

2. **Core data vs. well-test data**: Core plug measurements (6% porosity, 5 mD permeability) likely reflect sampling bias toward competent intervals and probably underrepresent the effective reservoir-scale properties. Well-test derived kh (~9,000 mD·m) and injectivity indices imply significantly higher effective permeability at the reservoir scale. The discrepancy is consistent with the presence of thin high-permeability streaks (e.g., the ~10 m upper Deadwood zone that hosts the primary CO₂ plume) that dominate flow but are underrepresented in core plugs.

3. **Regional vs. site-specific**: Regional literature values (e.g., 10–15% porosity, 300 mD permeability for the Deadwood Aquifer) differ substantially from Aquistore-specific core data. This likely reflects facies variations and the fact that Aquistore targets a specific structural location near the basin margin. The DEEP Border 1 well (25 km SW) provides the nearest comparable full-core data.

4. **Perforation plugging**: Perforations 3 and part of Perf 4 were plugged shortly after injection start, indicating that only ~55–60% of the completed interval was effectively contributing to injection. This is a significant operational observation relevant to completion design.

5. **Non-isothermal effects**: CO₂ is injected at ~70°C into a ~120°C reservoir, creating substantial thermal perturbations. This affects relative permeability, stress state, and injectivity evolution. Thermally-induced microcracking has been documented and appears to improve injectivity over time.

6. **Salt precipitation**: Stop-start injection has caused halite precipitation in lower-permeability zones near the wellbore, affecting injectivity. This is a key lesson for commercial operations with variable CO₂ supply.

7. **Data accessibility**: Comprehensive datasets (3D/3C seismic, time-lapse PNL/sonic logs, injection volumes, reservoir P/T, core, and simulation models) are available through PTRC's CCUS Data Network for investing partners.

8. **Resolution of 4D seismic**: The seismic data achieved a global NRMS of ~10%, allowing detection of small CO₂ quantities at 3.2 km depth. However, the plume interpretation is limited by seismic wavelength (~100 m), meaning thin or distal plume parts may be undetected. The seismic anomaly in the upper Deadwood is consistent with either a 2 m thick high-saturation (>50%) zone or a 4 m thick low-saturation (<10%) zone.

9. **Correlativity to the Basal Cambrian Sands (Alberta)**: The Deadwood Formation is the same regional Cambro-Ordovician saline aquifer as the Basal Cambrian Sands (BCS) targeted in Alberta (Quest, Atlas). The BCS is the basinward equivalent of the Deadwood/Black Island interval. However, Aquistore's reservoir is much thicker (~200 m vs. ~40 m for BCS at Quest) but has lower average porosity (6–7% vs. ~17% for BCS), reflecting greater burial depth (~3.2 km vs. ~1.5–2.0 km).

**Verbatim**: "The Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS) in Alberta is a widespread coarse-grained sandstone, which is like other target storage formations encountered further south; notably at Aquistore and at Decatur." — IEAGHG 2024 Storage Site Catalogue
