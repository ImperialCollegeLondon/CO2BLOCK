### 5. US Appalachian CCS Context

The US portion of the Appalachian Basin has **active CarbonSAFE and related CCS projects** under DOE funding:

| Project | Lead | Location | Status |
|---------|------|----------|--------|
| **CAB-CS** (Central Appalachian Basin CarbonSAFE) | Battelle | Ohio | Pre-feasibility / Characterization |
| **Midwest Regional CarbonSAFE** | Various | Ohio / West Virginia / Pennsylvania | Characterization |
| **CO₂-EOR in Appalachian Trend** | Multiple operators | Ohio, Pennsylvania, West Virginia | Active EOR (not storage-only) |

**Key US vs. Canadian regulatory differences:**

| Factor | United States | Canada (Appalachian) |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Regulatory Framework** | EPA Class VI (UIC program) for injection wells | No CCS-specific regulation in QC/NB/NS |
| **Pore Space Rights** | Defined at state level (severable from mineral rights) | No pore space tenure regime in any province |
| **Funding** | DOE CarbonSAFE ($50M+ for characterization) | No NRCan CCS Fund awards for Appalachian region |
| **Long-Term Liability** | EPA / state frameworks (post-injection monitoring) | No liability framework established |

This contrast underscores that the Canadian Appalachian regulatory gap — not geology — is the primary barrier to CCS development.

### 6. Gaspé Peninsula — Petroleum Exploration Context

The Gaspé Peninsula (Quebec Appalachian thrust belt) has **no CCS assessments** but has a history of petroleum exploration that provides geological context:

| Well / Activity | Operator | Target | Result | CCS Relevance |
|----------------|----------|--------|--------|---------------|
| **Galt No. 1** | SOQUIP / Hydro-Québec (1980s) | Trenton-Black River | Gas discovery (non-commercial) | Demonstrates reservoir presence and charge in Gaspé thrust belt |
| **Junex Gaspé wells (2007–2014)** | Junex Inc. | Utica Shale (shale gas) | Gas shows; fracture stimulation tested | Characterized Utica Shale as caprock; demonstrated overpressure |
| **Junex 3D seismic** | Junex Inc. | Gaspé thrust belt | ~200 km² 3D seismic acquired | Provides structural mapping of potential storage traps |
| **Forest Oil (Haldimand)** | Forest Oil Corp. | Trenton-Black River | Oil shows; structural closure identified | Confirms trapping mechanism viability |

**Key observations for CCS evaluation:**
- The Gaspé thrust belt contains the same Cambro-Ordovician reservoir/seal pairs as the St. Lawrence Lowlands platform, but in structurally imbricated thrust sheets
- Utica Shale is present as regional caprock
- Existing seismic and well data could constrain future CO₂ storage assessments
- No CCS-specific evaluation has been performed; all data derives from hydrocarbon exploration

### 8. Bécancour Industrial Zone (QC Transition)

The **Bécancour industrial zone** (Quebec, south shore of St. Lawrence River, ~100 km northeast of Montreal) is a significant CO₂ emitter region situated at the transition between the St. Lawrence Lowlands and Appalachian geology:

| Facility | Type | CO₂ Source |
|----------|------|------------|
| **Bécancour Refinery** (Valero) | Petroleum refining | Process + combustion emissions |
| **Methanex** (Bécancour) | Methanol production | Process CO₂ |
| **Yara (Bécancour)** | Fertilizer / ammonia | Process CO₂ (high-purity stream) |
| **Bécancour industrial park** | Multiple industries | Various point sources |

**Geological context**: Underlain by St. Lawrence Lowlands platform (Potsdam/Beekmantown/Trenton) transitioning eastward into Appalachian thrust belt. Proximity to potential storage formations makes it a candidate for future CCS hub development, though **no project or assessment currently exists**.

## Regulatory Framework — Quebec / New Brunswick / Nova Scotia

| Jurisdiction | Instrument | CCS Status |
|--------------|------------|------------|
| **Quebec (MERN)** | *Mining Act* / *Petroleum Resources Act* | **No CCS Act**; no pore space tenure; *Sustainable Development Act* policy only |
| **New Brunswick** | *Oil and Natural Gas Act* | **No CCS legislation** |
| **Nova Scotia** | *Onshore Petroleum Atlas* / *Offshore Accord* | **No CCS regulation** (offshore = CNSOPB) |
| **Federal** | *Impact Assessment Act* | Applies to future projects |

**Critical Gap**: No province in Canadian Appalachians has CCS pore space tenure regime or long-term liability framework.

**Carboniferous sandstone limitation (NB/NS):** GSC Open File 8996 (Carey & Durling 2023) found that Carboniferous sandstones in New Brunswick and Nova Scotia have limited CCS potential due to low porosity (typically 5–7% in Cumberland Group) and low permeability (<25 mD at target depths). Horton Bluff sandstones show somewhat better properties (φ up to 15%, perm up to 25 mD) but are not regionally extensive enough for commercial-scale storage.

## Conclusion

**Appalachian Basin (Canadian portion) has ZERO CCS projects.** Only **Tier 3–4 assessments** exist: INRS St. Lawrence Lowlands pre-feasibility (2010–2014), Clean Prosperity 2024 prospective estimate (2.8–3.2 Gt), and academic hydrocarbon-analog papers. The Gaspé Peninsula, Anticosti Island, and New Brunswick have petroleum exploration data but **no CCS-specific evaluation**. NB/NS Carboniferous sandstones show limited storage potential (GSC Open File 8996). Major point sources (~10 Mt/yr) exist across the region — including the Bécancour zone and Saint John — but no capture or storage project has been proposed. **No regulatory regime, no pore space tenure, no proponent.** US Appalachian CCS (CAB-CS in Ohio, other CarbonSAFE projects) is **not in Canadian territory** and highlights the regulatory gap. Basin assignment confirmed.
