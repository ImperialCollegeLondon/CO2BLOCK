## Strengths

1. **Definitive negative finding** — Geoscience BC Report 2024-08 concludes "storage is not technically recommended." This is the strongest possible finding for a zero-project basin and is correctly presented as the primary result.

2. **Specific technical reasoning documented** — The document lists the reasons: "injection rates unachievable in low-quality reservoirs," "major uncertainties in reservoir continuity, containment integrity, reservoir quality." This is more informative than a generic "no projects" finding.

3. **Alternative pathway noted** — Carbon mineralization via CO2Lock's SAM property is identified. This is an important distinction because ultramafic carbon mineralization is geologically distinct from sedimentary CCS and may be the only viable storage pathway for the Nechako region.

4. **Regulatory framework correct** — BCER's experience is limited to acid gas disposal (~2.7 Mt since 1996), not CCS. BC has no CCS Act. This is accurately characterized.

5. **High-case resource estimate in context** — The 197 Mt estimate is presented alongside the caveat "high uncertainty" and compared to NEBC Bluesky/Halfway P90 for reference.

## Issues & Recommendations

### 1. Limited Assessment Detail

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Only Geoscience BC Phase 1 (2024) is documented | This is the primary assessment, but the document could benefit from more detail on the assessment methodology: 12 wells, which formations were tested, what poroperm ranges were observed, and what the specific injection rate modeling showed. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a table of the 12 wells used in the Geoscience BC assessment, their depths, and the key petrophysical measurements that led to the "not recommended" conclusion. |

### 2. Missing CO2Lock/SAM Detail

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| CO2Lock's SAM property is mentioned but not described | The SAM property carbon mineralization concept (ultramafic rock weathering to permanently store CO₂ as carbonate minerals) is described in only one line. |
| **Recommendation** | Expand the carbon mineralization alternative section: (a) how in-situ carbon mineralization works (Mg₂SiO₄ + 2CO₂ → 2MgCO₃ + SiO₂), (b) pilot scale/status, (c) comparison to CarbFix (Iceland) or other global mineralization projects. |

### 3. Regulatory Framework — Acid Gas Disposal Distinction

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| "BCER experience = acid gas disposal only (~2.7 Mt since 1996)" | This is correct but could be more specific. Acid gas disposal operations under the *Oil and Gas Activities Act* are mostly in NEBC (WCSB), not the Nechako Basin. The distinction matters because one might assume the BCER has CCS-regulatory capacity when it doesn't. |
| **Recommendation** | Clarify that BCER's acid gas disposal experience is in NEBC (WCSB / Alberta Basin), not in the Nechako Basin. The BCER has no regulatory experience with CCS-scale injection. |

### 4. Missing Emissions Context

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| No mention of BC's CleanBC targets | CleanBC sets a 0.6 Mt/yr CCS target by 2030, but there is no Nechako-specific roadmap. The document does not connect provincial CCS targets to the Nechako assessment outcome. |
| **Recommendation** | Add a short context paragraph: If Nechako CCS is not feasible, where does BC plan to meet its CCS targets? (Likely NEBC within the WCSB, or out-of-province storage.) |

### 5. Missing Highway 16 Corridor Emitter Inventory

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| "CO₂ Supply: Foresight Canada: challenges with scale, transport distances, emissions composition" | This is referenced but no specific emitter data is provided. The Highway 16 corridor (Prince George to Prince Rupert) has significant industrial emissions (pulp mills, LNG terminals). |
| **Recommendation** | Add a table or paragraph documenting the major CO₂ sources in the Highway 16 corridor, their annual emissions, and the transport distance to potential Nechako storage sites. |

### 6. Minor Issues

- **Line 40** — "3 areas NW/west of Williams Lake: limited potential with geological risks" — the specific geological risks (low permeability, fault compartmentalization, overpressure?) are not named. A one-line summary per area would help.
- **Line 67** — "No CCS Act" for BC — BC's *Carbon Capture and Storage Regulation* under the *Oil and Gas Activities Act* does exist for acid gas, but not for commercial CCS. Clarify this distinction.
- **No comparison to NEBC** — The Geoscience BC assessment likely compared Nechako reservoir quality to NEBC (where the Bluesky/Halfway formations have commercial CCS potential). Documenting this comparison would strengthen the negative finding.
- **The Nechako Basin is the ONLY basin in the set where a formal geoscience assessment definitively recommends against CCS** — this should be highlighted as a unique finding.
