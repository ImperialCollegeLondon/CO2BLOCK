## Stratigraphic Clarification

### The AER DDS Pool Code Issue

The AER DDS Application #1959125 lists the pool as **"WILLINGDON/BSL QTZ UND"** (Willingdon field / Basal Quartz undefined). This has caused confusion because:

- The **Basal Quartz** (AER code 3340, Kbs_qtz) is a Lower Cretaceous (Aptian) sandstone within the **Mannville Group** — a shallower fluvial/estuarine unit that produces hydrocarbons in the Willingdon field.
- The **Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS)** is a Cambrian–Ordovician cratonic sandstone at ~1,800–2,200 m depth — the proven CCS reservoir at Shell's Quest project and the target for Wolf Lamont.

**Resolution**: The AER DDS field/pool code is an **administrative assignment** — the AER system assigns disposal/CCS wells the pool code of the nearest producing field, not the injection interval. The following evidence confirms BCS as the actual target:

1. **Depth range**: Wolf Lamont targets ~1,800–2,200 m (per regional BCS depth maps and Quest analog). The Cretaceous Basal Quartz in east-central Alberta lies at ~1,300–1,600 m — too shallow for the stated depths.
2. **Operator communications**: Wolf Midstream, Whitecap Resources, and all press releases consistently reference "Basal Cambrian Sand" as the target.
3. **Core evidence**: Well 100/06-05-055-17W4/00 (within the Wolf Lamont area) cored the BCS for CCS evaluation, presented at the CEGA Core Conference (June 2024).
4. **Quest analog**: The Shell Quest CCS project injects into the same BCS aquifer system ~60–80 km northwest, with identical depth range (~2,000 m) and reservoir properties.

> **Note**: The Cretaceous Basal Quartz may exist as a secondary or overlying storage interval in the storage complex, but it is NOT the primary injection target. If future data confirms stacked storage across both formations, separate property compilations would be required for each.

- Multiple independent sources (AGS, ERCB Quest Decision, Moradi, Smith et al.) agree on BCS thickness in the 35–50 m range at analogous Quest location.

**Recommended value**: **40–50 m** (gross thickness for Wolf Lamont, inferred from Quest analog and regional AGS data)

### Confirming Evidence

- **Quest regulatory (Tier 1)**: ERCB Quest Decision para 175 gives 11–19% — this is the most authoritative single source for Quest BCS porosity.
- **Quest operational (Tier 3)**: Smith et al. (2022) GHGT-15 reports ~17% average; Moradi (2016) reports ~18% from density logs. These are independently derived and consistent.
- **Regional (Tier 1)**: Alberta Innovates (Meikle, 2024) gives 14.4% for the entire storage-suitable area, which is lower than the Quest site-specific average — expected, as regional averages include lower-quality rock.
- **Cross-validation**: The 11–19% regulatory range brackets the ~17–18% operational averages. All sources are consistent.

**Recommended value**: **17%** (formation-scale average for Wolf Lamont, based on Quest analog. Regional variance: 11–19%.)

## Permeability

### Primary Values

| Parameter | Value | Source | Notes |
|-----------|-------|--------|-------|
| BCS permeability range | **1 mD to >1,000 mD** | Moradi (2016); Quest core data | Log-normal distribution; tidal channels are high-perm streaks |
| BCS average permeability (Quest, formation-scale) | **~1,000 mD** (~1 Darcy) | Smith et al. (2022) GHGT-15 | **Formation-scale average for CCS** — represents effective permeability of the high-perm streak network |
| BSU matrix permeability (central AB — **geothermal context**) | **<1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m² (~0.01 mD)** | Weides et al. (2014) | **Non-stimulated matrix permeability only.** Weides studied the BSU for geothermal viability and explicitly stated that hydraulic stimulation is required because matrix permeability alone is insufficient for commercial fluid flow. This is NOT representative of BCS as a CCS target. |
| BCS high-perm streaks (estuarine/tidal channels) | 100–1,000+ mD | Stantec/CEGA (Noad, 2024); Quest experience | "Anecdotal evidence of high perm streaks in associated tidal channels that have not materially affected the project" |
| LMS permeability (seal baffle) | ~4 mD | Moradi (2016) | Vertical permeability barrier despite moderate k |
| UMS permeability (major seal) | <1 mD | Moradi (2016) | Effective seal; corroborates Quest containment |
| Devonian Basal Red Beds | 0.001–1 mD | Moradi (2016) | Additional baffle |
| Kv/Kh ratio | **0.1–0.5 (estimated)** | Estimated — no direct BCS measurement identified | AGS PRS-2024-001 notes: "interstitial clay and silt... a critical control on permeability and even a 2–3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude." Kv/Kh likely varies by facies. |

### Confirming Evidence

- **Formation-scale average (~1 Darcy) vs. core plug (~0.01 mD)**: This is the single most important distinction in the entire permeability dataset. CCS viability comes from the connected high-perm streak network (tidal channels, shoreface sands), not the tight matrix. Quest has successfully injected >9 Mt CO₂ since 2015 (Alberta Law Review 2025), conclusively demonstrating that the formation-scale effective permeability supports commercial CCS.
- **Weides et al. (2014)**: Their <0.01 mD average is correct for their dataset (6 wells, core plugs, non-stimulated BSU) but **irrelevant for CCS injectivity assessment** without context. The original document's inclusion of this value alongside CCS values was misleading.
- **Smith et al. (2022)**: ~1,000 mD average from GHGT-15 proceedings — this is the peer-reviewed, formation-scale CCS value.
- **AGS PRS-2024-001**: The clay-content control on permeability explains the orders-of-magnitude difference between matrix and effective permeability.

> **Critical note**: Permeability heterogeneity is the primary reservoir risk for BCS CCS. The high-perm streak network controls injectivity; this network's distribution and connectivity at Wolf Lamont is unknown without site-specific well-test data.

**Recommended value**: **~1,000 mD** (formation-scale average, Quest analog). Range: 1 mD (tight matrix) to >1,000 mD (high-perm channels). For CO₂ injectivity modeling, use **~500–1,000 mD effective**.

## Notes on Data Quality

1. **Wolf-specific data is limited.** No public reservoir properties report specifically for the Wolf Lamont Carbon Hub has been released. Whitecap Resources (the subsurface partner) presented core from well 100/06-05-055-17W4/00 at the CEGA Core Conference (June 2024) for the "Basal Cambrian Sand for Carbon Sequestration" evaluation at Lamont — this data is likely proprietary.

2. **Quest CCS is the direct operational analog.** Quest has injected >9 Mt CO₂ into the BCS since 2015 with no containment issues (Alberta Law Review 2025). The Wolf Lamont hub targets the same regional aquifer system approximately 60–80 km north/northeast of the Quest injection site. The Stantec (2024) review states Quest has "performed well, with anecdotal evidence of high perm streaks in associated tidal channels that have not materially affected the project."

3. **Stratigraphic clarification**: The AER DDS pool code "WILLINGDON/BSL QTZ UND" is an administrative field/pool assignment from the nearest producing field (Willingdon, Basal Quartz — Lower Cretaceous Mannville Group). It does NOT indicate the injection interval. The confirmed injection target is the Cambrian Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS) at ~1,800–2,200 m depth, consistent with all operator communications. See the Stratigraphic Clarification section above.

4. **The 1 Darcy formation-scale permeability (Smith et al., 2022) vs. the 0.01 mD matrix permeability (Weides et al., 2014)** are NOT contradictory — they measure different things at different scales. The Darcy-scale value represents the effective permeability of the connected high-perm streak network that makes BCS viable for CCS. The Weides value represents non-stimulated matrix permeability from a geothermal study. Both are correct for their respective contexts but should not be conflated.

5. **Key uncertainty — heterogeneity**: As AGS PRS-2024-001 notes: "a critical control on permeability and even a 2–3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude."

6. **Area data is preliminary.** The 9-township extent from the AER application represents the application boundary. The final sequestration lease and pore space unit agreement areas will be determined by Alberta Energy and Minerals post-evaluation.

7. **Timeline**: Selected Mar 2022 (RFPP) → AER approved Mar 2026 (Approval 13513). The CCS Knowledge Centre notes that "recent Directive 065 sequestration application reviews took less than six months for the Wolf Lamont Carbon Hub." Initial target operation date slipped from "end of 2024" (2022 announcement) to "2025" (CER Jan 2025) and remains in development as of mid-2026.
