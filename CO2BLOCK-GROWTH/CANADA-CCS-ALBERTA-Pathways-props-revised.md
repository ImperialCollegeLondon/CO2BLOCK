# Pathways Alliance CCS Hub — Reservoir & Project Properties (REVISED)

> **Project:** Pathways Alliance / Oil Sands Alliance CO₂ Transportation Network and Storage Hub
> **Location:** Cold Lake area, northeastern Alberta, Canada (Municipal District of Bonnyville / Lac la Biche County)
> **Basin:** Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin (WCSB) / Alberta Basin
> **Primary Storage Formation:** Basal Cambrian Sandstone (BCS) — Cambrian-Ordovician saline aquifer

**Critical Lateral Variability Caveat:** Quest's ~17% porosity is from the western BCS fairway (~200 km west of Cold Lake). The 2–3% clay increase causing 3-order permeability reduction (see Section 4) also implies porosity is strongly controlled by clay content and diagenesis.

## 4. Permeability

### Basal Cambrian Sandstone

| Parameter | Value | Source | Tier |
|-----------|-------|--------|------|
| Permeability control | "Interstitial clay and silt were found to be a critical control on permeability" | AGS PRS 2024-001 | Tier 3 |
| Clay sensitivity | "Even a 2–3% increase in clay content has been found to reduce vertical and horizontal permeability by three orders of magnitude, even in coarse-grained sediments" | AGS PRS 2024-001 | Tier 3 |
| Quest analogue permeability | **~1,000 mD** (average) | "average permeability close to 1000 md" — Rock et al. 2022, IJGGG | Tier 3 |
| Permeability range at Cold Lake | **Unknown.** No site-specific public data for Cold Lake hub. Based on AGS diagenetic framework, likely significantly lower than Quest due to proximity to eastern subcrop, increased cementation, and clay content. | AGS PRS 2024-001; PRS 2023-001 | Tier 3 |
| Diagenetic impacts | "Potassium feldspar alteration to kaolinite during burial reduces the porosity and permeability" | AGS PRS 2023-001 | Tier 3 |

**Lateral Variability Warning:** Quest's ~1,000 mD native permeability is from the high-quality western BCS fairway near Fort Saskatchewan. The AGS studies document extreme heterogeneity — a 2–3% clay increase reduces permeability by **three orders of magnitude** (1,000 mD → ~1 mD). At the Cold Lake subcrop margin, where the BCS is thinner, shallower, and more cemented, native permeability is expected to be in the **1–10 mD range** — 2–3 orders of magnitude lower than Quest. This is a material risk for the 10–12 Mtpa (or even the 6 Mtpa MOU) injection target.

> **Data Quality Note:** The dramatic permeability reduction from minor clay content increase makes site-specific permeability characterization critical for injectivity assessment. Pathways has drilled 2 test wells (2024) to evaluate this. However, **test well data has not been independently confirmed** via AER well license database search — the sole source is EnergyNow (Tier 7).

#### Quest Halite-Induced Injectivity Degradation — Critical Risk Factor

The Quest CCS facility has experienced significant halite precipitation damage despite having ~1,000 mD native permeability. This is documented in a peer-reviewed study:

| Parameter | Value | Source | Tier |
|-----------|-------|--------|------|
| Halite damage mechanism | "As dry CO₂ is injected, water can evaporate from the brine into the CO₂ driving precipitation of halite. Plugging of the perforation tunnel and pore throats in the near wellbore area can cause injectivity damage." | Rock et al. 2022, IJGGG | Tier 3 |
| Measured skin value | "solving Hawkins' formula... with a recent skin value of 147" | Rock et al. 2022 | Tier 3 |
| Damage zone permeability | "a damage zone permeability of 10 md was obtained, a two orders of magnitude reduction compared to average reservoir permeability" | Rock et al. 2022 | Tier 3 |
| Remediation | Freshwater wash treatment performed in 2020; successful at restoring injectivity | Rock et al. 2022 | Tier 3 |

**Implications for Pathways (Cold Lake):**
- Quest's native permeability ~1,000 mD → halite damage reduced near-wellbore permeability to ~10 mD (98% reduction)
- Cold Lake BCS native permeability is expected to be **1–10 mD** (based on AGS diagenetic framework and subcrop proximity)
- If halite damage causes a similar 2-order-of-magnitude reduction, effective near-wellbore permeability at Cold Lake could fall to **0.01–0.1 mD** — near-zero for commercial injectivity
- This risk is heightened by high-TDS formation brine expected in the BCS at the Cold Lake subcrop margin
- Freshwater remediation is possible (as demonstrated at Quest) but increases operating costs and requires a freshwater supply

> **This is arguably the single most important injectivity risk factor for Pathways.** The project's target injection rate (even at the reduced 6 Mtpa MOU target) must account for potential halite-induced injectivity degradation across multiple wells over the project lifetime.

## 8. First-Order Storage Efficiency Calculation

The original file records storage capacity in Mtpa (flow rate) but did not estimate whether the known pore space at Cold Lake can support the target injection rate. Below is a first-order calculation using conservative BCS properties:

| Parameter | Value | Source / Basis |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| BCS thickness (Cold Lake) | ~40 m | AGS PRS 2024-001 (lower end of 40–80 m range, conservative for subcrop margin) |
| Estimated porosity | **10%** (conservative, based on AGS diagenesis discussion; Quest = 17%) | Estimated — no site-specific data |
| CO₂ density at ~1,200 m, ~45°C | ~650–750 kg/m³ | Standard CO₂ phase behavior |
| Storage efficiency factor | **2–4%** (saline aquifer, no hydrodynamic trapping) | Industry standard for "open" saline aquifers (CSLF, US-DOE methodology) |
| Pore volume per km² | 40 m × 1 km² = 40 × 10⁶ m³ rock × 0.10 porosity = **4.0 × 10⁶ m³ pore volume/km²** | Calculated |
| Mass stored per km² | 4.0 × 10⁶ m³ × 700 kg/m³ × 0.03 efficiency = **~84,000 tonnes/km²** | Calculated |
| **Area needed for 6 Mtpa × 30 years** | 6 Mtpa × 30 yr = 180 Mt ÷ 0.084 Mt/km² = **~2,140 km²** | Calculated |
| **Area needed for 10 Mtpa × 30 years** | 10 Mtpa × 30 yr = 300 Mt ÷ 0.084 Mt/km² = **~3,570 km²** | Calculated |
| **Area needed for 40 Mtpa × 30 years** | 40 Mtpa × 30 yr = 1,200 Mt ÷ 0.084 Mt/km² = **~14,300 km²** | Calculated |

**Tension:** Even the reduced 6 Mtpa MOU target × 30 years implies a storage area of ~2,140 km² (roughly 24 × 24 townships or ~530,000 acres). This is plausible for a Cold Lake hub lease but at the upper range of typical CCS lease areas. The original 10–12 Mtpa target would require proportionally more area (~3,570 km²). The 40 Mtpa full build-out would require ~14,300 km² — a very large area exceeding most single-hub CCS leases globally.

**Caveats:**
- If BCS thickness is >40 m (e.g., 60–80 m in channel fairways), the required area decreases proportionally
- If permeability is in the 1–10 mD range (expected at Cold Lake), injectivity constraints may limit the effective storage area regardless of pore volume
- This calculation does not account for pressure interference between injection wells, which would reduce effective storage efficiency
- The CDL study (proprietary until 2028) likely contains the definitive storage capacity estimate for the Cold Lake area

## 11. Quest CCS Analogue — With Lateral Variability & Depth Caveats

Quest is the only operational CCS project injecting into the BCS. It is frequently cited as a Pathways analogue. **However, lateral variability between the Quest site (~200 km west) and the Cold Lake hub makes direct transfer of properties unreliable.**

| Parameter | Quest (Fort Saskatchewan) | Pathways (Cold Lake) | Implications |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------|--------------|
| BCS depth | ~2,000 m | ~1,000–1,500 m | Lower CO₂ density at Cold Lake → less mass stored per pore volume |
| BCS thickness | ~45 m average | 40–80 m (regional AGS range) | Comparable, but may be thinner near subcrop |
| Porosity | ~17% | Unknown; likely lower (diagenesis + subcrop proximity) | Lower porosity → less storage capacity |
| Permeability | ~1,000 mD | Unknown; estimated 1–10 mD | 2–3 orders of magnitude lower → injectivity risk |
| Halite damage | Skin=147, near-wellbore perm ~10 mD | If native perm = 1–10 mD, halite could reduce to ≤0.1 mD | **Injectivity could approach zero** |
| Lateral separation | ~200 km west | — | Different depositional fairway; Quest is in the western high-quality BCS |
| CO₂ stored | >9 Mt since 2015 | Target: 6 Mtpa (MOU) | Pathways annual target = ~6× Quest's annual rate |
| Number of injection wells | 3 wells | Up to 19 planned | Pathways needs ~6× the well count for ~6× the rate |

> **Critical Caveat:** "Quest is ~200 km west in a different BCS depositional fairway. Properties (porosity ~17%, permeability ~1,000 mD) are from the high-quality western BCS and are NOT representative of the Cold Lake area. They should not be used as a direct analogue without applying significant downward adjustment for diagenetic alteration, subcrop proximity, and expected lower reservoir quality at Cold Lake."

## 13. Data Quality Notes

1. **Regulatory / Pre-FID Stage:** The Pathways CCS Hub is in pre-FID / regulatory application stage. Detailed technical data (injectivity, well counts, precise storage capacity) is proprietary.

2. **CDL Study Confidentiality:** The CDL (Canadian Discovery Ltd.) Regional Geological CCS Assessment of the BCS (March 2023) is **proprietary and confidential until March 2028**. Results attributed to this source cannot be independently verified within CO2BLOCK's audit window.

3. **Source Provenance — Pre-Takedown Documents:** The Pathways Project Overview PDF (Aug 2023) was published before the June 2024 Competition Act-related website shutdown. It was distributed via The Narwhal, not directly by the proponent. Pre-2024 documents may include MEG Energy as a proponent; post-2024 documents consistently list 5 proponents.

4. **Test Well Verification:** The claim of 2 test wells drilled (2024) and 2–3 more planned comes from a single Tier 7 source (EnergyNow, Dec 2024). This has not been independently confirmed via AER well license database search.

5. **Source-Tier Confidence:** All technical property values for the Cold Lake hub are **LOW CONFIDENCE** because no Tier 1–2 source has published site-specific data. Regional BCS data from AGS (Tier 3) provides the geological envelope but is not site-calibrated.

6. **Quest Analogue Transfer:** Every value transferred from Quest to Pathways carries an additional confidence reduction due to 200 km lateral separation, 500–1,000 m depth difference, and documented BCS heterogeneity.

7. **Storage Capacity vs. Emissions Target:** The 6 Mtpa MOU target is a regulatory enforcement target (tied to TIER benchmark). The 22 Mtpa and 40 Mtpa figures are aspirational targets from pre-2024 documents. These should not be treated as comparable unless explicitly reconciled.
